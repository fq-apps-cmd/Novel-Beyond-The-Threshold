At the same time—

Far beyond the Mortal Realm, past the boundaries that separated one world from another, something shifted.
It wasn’t loud. There was no explosion, no roar, no visible tear. There was no light, no darkness—nothing that could be pointed to, measured, or observed through ordinary means.
And yet—
Something changed.
Just—
A subtle absence.
Not emptiness.
Not void.
Something more precise than that.
As if something vast had occupied that place… something with weight, presence, continuity—and then simply ceased.
Not destroyed.
Not removed.
Just no longer… existing in relation to anything else.
Even space did not rush to fill it.
It lingered.
Uncertain.

The First to Arrive
The “sky” above a barren stretch of land in the Spirit World twisted faintly—not in color, not in shape, but in definition. The distance between two points hesitated as though unsure how far apart they should be.
A ripple spread outward.
Not from a center.
Not from a source.
But as if the world had briefly remembered something…
…and then forgotten it again.
Then—
Someone appeared.
No movement.
No descent.
No arrival.
He was simply there.
A tall man in layered robes of deep azure stood with his hands folded behind his back. The moment he existed within that space, the land responded—not shifting, not breaking—but settling around him, as though recognizing his presence and adjusting itself accordingly.
Loose grains of dust stilled too quickly.
Ambient energy curved subtly to avoid brushing directly past him.
His gaze fixed forward.
Eyes narrowing.
“…So it wasn’t just me.”

Another Arrives
A second figure stepped into existence beside him.
A woman, silver-haired, calm in a way that felt intentional. Her robes stirred despite the complete absence of wind. The air around her seemed to hesitate, as though uncertain whether it was permitted to move near her at all.
Light bent faintly when nearing her form—slowing, diffusing, then continuing as if nothing had happened.
“I felt it as well,” she said quietly.
Her voice did not travel.
It simply existed where it was heard.
Her gaze swept across the space before them, careful, deliberate—not searching for something visible, but for something that should have been there and wasn’t.
“But this…”
A pause.
“…this is different.”

More Gather
Others appeared.
One by one.
Each arrival identical—
No transition.
No crossing of distance.
Only presence.
A handful in total.
Only those strong enough to feel something was wrong.
One of them, broad-shouldered with a rough voice, let out a low chuckle. The sound felt… heavier than it should, as if it pressed into the space rather than moving through it.
“So this is what called all of us here?” he said. “I don’t see anything. Just empty air.”
The man in blue didn’t respond immediately.
His gaze remained fixed.
“…Look again.”

The Investigation Begins
The silver-haired woman stepped forward, slow and deliberate. She extended her senses—not forcefully, but carefully, letting her awareness unfold in controlled layers.
Like touching something submerged in darkness.
For a moment—
Nothing.
Then—
A subtle resistance.
Not physical.
Not energetic.
Conceptual.
Her brows furrowed.
“…There’s a discontinuity,” she said. “A region where the laws… thin out.”
As she spoke, the space in front of her subtly misbehaved.
Dust drifting in the air slowed unnaturally as it passed through that region—some particles hesitating, others skipping forward in tiny jumps.
Light that crossed the area dulled slightly—not dimming, but losing coherence, as though it forgot how to travel in a straight line.
The broad man scoffed.
“That’s your imagination. Space doesn’t just go missing.”
“…No,” she replied softly.
Her gaze didn’t waver.
“…It doesn’t.”
That was the problem.

The First Test
He stepped forward anyway.
Curiosity overriding instinct.
“Let’s test that.”
His hand moved through the air.
Nothing.
Normal.
The sense of distance remained.
The space gave way as expected.
He pushed further, reinforcing the motion with power—just enough to interact with deeper layers of reality.
And then—
He stopped.
“…What?”

The First Unease
His hand continued moving.
But he could no longer tell where it was.
Not hidden.
Not resisted.
Not blocked.
His perception failed to return anything meaningful.
No distance.
No boundary.
No separation.
“…There’s… nothing there.”
The others turned.
The man in blue stepped forward.
“What do you mean by nothing?”
The broad man tried again.
Slower.
Deliberate.
“My hand…”
A pause.
“…it isn’t touching anything.”
“That’s expected,” someone muttered. “It’s empty space.”
“No.”
His voice hardened.
“It’s not space.”
Silence followed.
Because they all understood.
If it were space, there would still be something to perceive.
Space still had distance.
Structure.
Relational existence.
This had none.

A Deeper Look
The silver-haired woman stepped closer.
This time, she focused completely.
The world around her quieted.
Not metaphorically.
Actually.
Energy stilled.
Motion slowed.
Even the subtle flow of existence around them seemed to wait.
Then—
Her eyes widened.
“…This shouldn’t exist.”

The Realization
The man in blue turned.
“Explain.”
She didn’t look at him.
“We all perceive layered space,” she said. “We understand distance beyond simple position. We can sense folds, shifts, transitions between worlds.”
A pause.
“This is not that.”
The broad man crossed his arms.
“…Then someone moved between layers.”
She shook her head.
“No.”
She turned to him.
“That would leave relation.”
“Distance.”
“Trace.”
“Echo.”
Her voice lowered.
“This leaves none.”
She looked forward again.
“It is there.”
A breath passed.
“And not there.”

What They Could Not Accept
The man in blue exhaled slowly.
“…Then they didn’t pass through space.”
She nodded.
“…Nor through layered traversal.”
Her voice dropped further.
“It feels like something existed outside of position entirely… and then intersected reality just enough to leave behind… this.”
The silence deepened.
Even beings who could perceive beyond worlds—
felt it.

The Attempt
Another figure stepped forward.
“I will test it properly.”
Power surged outward.
Space bent.
Reality adjusted.
Law responded.
His hand pressed forward—
And—
His power vanished.
No impact.
No resistance.
No transfer.
Just gone.
He staggered.
“…What did you—”
“No one touched you.”
The man in blue remained calm.
The figure flexed his hand.
“…My power didn’t reach anything.”
That was worse than failure.
It meant—
There was nothing to reach.

True Discomfort
The broad man frowned.
“…I don’t like this.”
Another voice whispered:
“What kind of existence ignores the structure of worlds entirely?”
No one answered.
Because no one knew.

The Warning
“We should leave,” the silver-haired woman said.
“Without understanding it?” another challenged.
“You won’t understand it.”
A pause.
“And if you push further…”
She looked at the void.
“…I don’t know what it will do.”

Unseen Observer
Far below.
In the Mortal Realm.
Mateo sat beneath a tree, steady, still, grounded in something simple and real.
Then—
Something flickered.
“…Hm?”
And suddenly—
He saw everything.
Not partially.
Not vaguely.
Perfectly.
The figures.
The distortion.
The exact point.
“…Oh.”
His breathing remained calm.
“…That’s where I was.”
He watched.
“…They can’t touch it.”
Pause.
“…Good.”
Head tilting slightly.
“…Why is it still there?”
Something stirred.
Familiar.
Distant.
“…Did I leave that behind?”
He reached—
With awareness alone.
For a moment—
Close.
Then—
Gone.
“…Huh.”
“…So even I can’t access it anymore.”
A small pause.
“…That might be for the best.”

Back at the Disturbance
The figures withdrew.
One by one.
Not hurried.
But not comfortable.
The man in blue remained the longest.
His gaze stayed fixed on the empty space, eyes narrowing as if trying to force meaning from something that refused to be understood.
“…Whatever existed here…”
A long pause.
“…was never part of any world to begin with.”
Then he turned.
And was gone.

Mateo’s Closing Thought
Back in the Mortal Realm, Mateo opened his eyes slowly.
The world returned in full.
Children shouting. Wind moving. Life continuing as though none of this mattered.
He rested his elbow against his knee.
“…Alright.”
A pause.
“…That answers nothing.”
He stood.
Stretching slightly.
“…Guess I’ll focus on things that actually matter right now.”
From inside the house:
“Mateo! Get inside!”
He sighed.
“…Coming.”
A faint smile appeared.
“…From beyond existence… to getting yelled at to come eat.”
Another pause.
“…Not bad.”
