"…Again?"
The word carried no anger, no urgency—only the quiet exhaustion of something that had already seen too much.
Mateo drifted in a place that could not truly be called a world. There was no sky above him, no ground beneath him, no horizon unless he decided one should exist. Distance, direction, even silence itself existed here only because he allowed them to. Everything rested in perfect stillness, a sealed and endless expanse shaped entirely by his will.
He raised one hand.
Reality unfolded.
Stars flared into being in perfect formation, suspended across a darkness that immediately began to make sense around them. Galaxies spiraled outward in elegant arcs, time layered itself into motion, and worlds were born in the spaces between thought and intent. Mountains rose, seas spread, wind moved, and life—bright, fragile, stubborn life—began to flourish.
For a brief moment, Mateo watched.
Then he sighed.
"…Incomplete."
His fingers curled inward, and the entire reality folded in on itself. Light vanished. Time dissolved. Structure unraveled so thoroughly that even the memory of it seemed to vanish with it.
Silence returned.
Mateo leaned back into it, one arm behind his head, drifting once more through the endless void that had become less a home than a habit.
"I've run out of things to do."
There had been a time when that would have sounded tragic.
Now it was only true.

Once, creation had fascinated him.
He had made realities the way others might sketch ideas into dust—testing systems, changing laws, adjusting how energy moved through living things, how the strong rose and the weak broke, how time favored one world and starved another. He had observed civilizations build themselves from nothing, watched language emerge from noise, watched ambition turn into empire and sorrow turn into doctrine.
He had created them all.
And eventually, that too had become repetitive.
At first, he believed the problem was scale, so he built larger worlds. Then smaller ones. Then worlds of endless war, worlds of perfect peace, worlds where cultivation came easily, and worlds where every step upward demanded blood.
None of it lasted.
None of it surprised him for long.
Except…
Mateo's gaze shifted downward, through layers of existence beyond counting, and settled once more on the worlds beneath his own sealed realm.
Those worlds were different.
Because they were not his alone.
Because they changed in ways he could not fully predict.
Because what grew within them was not sustained by his direct will.
They had room to become something he had not planned.
That was what drew him back to them, again and again.
The lower realms.
The mortal worlds.
The places where people were born into weakness and spent entire lives trying to claw their way upward.
Mateo's attention sharpened.
"…Let's see."
He focused not on one person, but many.
He saw a boy gripping a blade too heavy for him, arms trembling, stance broken, yet refusing to release it. He saw a girl practicing beneath pouring rain, repeating the same strike until all unnecessary motion had been beaten out of her muscles. He saw a crippled beggar rise from the dirt with one eye swollen shut and still spit blood at the heavens as if the heavens had personally insulted him.
He watched.
Time unfolded beneath his gaze in broad, graceful sweeps. Days passed. Years followed. Decades compressed into moments.
The boy grew stronger, though never elegant. The girl sharpened into precision. The beggar endured. Again and again, Mateo saw the same thing in different forms—people who should have broken… choosing not to.
"…Persistent," he murmured.
But persistence alone was never the whole story, and he knew that better than anyone.
He watched a gifted youth born under endless praise, someone hailed as a once-in-an-era genius in the Mortal Realm. Every technique came easily to him. Every battle ended quickly. People gathered around him not because of what he had built, but because talent itself made them believe he was destined for greatness.
And when he reached the upper threshold of that world, Mateo's attention sharpened further.
"…Here it comes."
The change was never dramatic.
No visible gate descended from the heavens. No voice declared passage granted. No hand reached down from above to lift the worthy into the next world.
Instead, the world simply stopped being enough.
They outgrew it.
One by one, those who reached the limit disappeared from their native realm, absent for only the briefest moment before reappearing somewhere higher—somewhere denser, older, and far less forgiving.
And then the truth revealed itself.
The lower-realm genius who had never needed to struggle arrived above and found himself… ordinary. His talent remained, but it was no longer singular. Those around him had their own brilliance, deeper foundations, harsher histories, and strengths forged through pressure he had never known. His early dominance meant little there.
He plateaued.
Then slowly, quietly, he became one more name among many.
Mateo watched without pity.
"…Predictable."
Then he watched another.
This one had been mocked in the lower realm—called mediocre, inconsistent, lacking the grace and brilliance others displayed so effortlessly. Even when that cultivator advanced, people dismissed him. They believed him too slow, too plain, too ordinary to matter.
But he never stopped.
He failed. Rebuilt. Failed again. Continued anyway.
He ascended.
And in the next realm, he was still overlooked.
Not because he lacked value, but because the world above rarely had patience for those who bloomed late. Still, he endured. Where others relied on talent, he relied on momentum. Where others stalled once praise failed them, he kept moving because he had never depended on praise to begin with.
Centuries passed.
Then millennia.
The same figures who had once scoffed at him could do nothing but look upward in silence. The "mediocre" cultivator they had dismissed rose beyond them one realm at a time, until even his shadow was beyond their reach.
Mateo smiled faintly.
"Interesting."
Talent mattered.
So did insight, timing, luck, opportunity, inheritance, and pressure.
But talent alone was never enough.
Neither was hardship.
There was no single path that guaranteed ascension, and no single flaw that guaranteed collapse.
That was what fascinated him.
Variation.
He watched another figure from the lower realms cross upward and, against every expectation, become close to someone from one of the oldest and highest lineages in existence. At first they fought, then competed, then spoke, then eventually stood side by side. Mateo had watched enough conflict to understand how unusual that was.
Not all powerful clans looked downward.
Not all strength demanded arrogance.
Not all high-born cultivators were blind to worth.
That, too, mattered.
It made the worlds below feel real.
Alive.
Capable of producing things even he had not arranged.
And yet—
There was one cruelty he understood too well.
Not all lower realms were equal.
He knew because he had created some of them himself.
Lives had flourished in his sealed realities. Spirits had awakened. Cultivators had risen, fought, loved, hated, built sects, founded clans, and believed their heavens limitless. To them, their worlds were complete. Their strongest figures were legends. Their wonders incomparable.
But Mateo knew the truth.
His realm was sealed.
The beings within it could grow only so far before the invisible limit appeared. The world would not let them pass beyond a certain point. Their talent, their effort, their destiny—none of it mattered once they reached the ceiling embossed into reality itself.
To them, they were mighty.
Outside his realm, they would have been minor figures.
Not worthless.
Not weak.
But small against the wider structure of true worlds.
That had always bothered him more than he admitted.
Because they had not failed.
They had merely been born into a system that could not carry them further.
As long as his sealed realm remained closed, that would never change.
Which was why, when he finally made his decision, he did not simply destroy everything.
He could have. Easily.
Instead, when he chose to collapse the system, he chose something more difficult.
He would move them.
Every life he had made, every civilization that had flourished beneath his sealed heavens, every being trapped beneath the limits of that false sky—he would attune them to the real worlds. He would weave them into existence as though they had always belonged there.
Not stronger than others.
Not weaker than others.
Equalized.
Adjusted.
Allowed.
The gifts his realm had granted them could not cross over in full. What he had woven into them within that sealed world had been too divine, too excessive, too absolute to survive intact in reality outside. If a mortal realm suddenly inherited beings born with ungodly roots, divine-grade inheritances, or impossible abilities that had not developed naturally, the balance of worlds would break.
The worlds themselves would reject that kind of distortion.
Mortal lives bearing impossible divinity from the start would cause chaos on a scale even he found distasteful.
So he would not do that.
He would preserve them, but not let them carry everything.
They would enter the true worlds with their lives intact, their identities preserved, and their potential reset into something natural—something the wider structure of reality could accept.
They would still have the chance to rise.
This time for real.
This time without a false ceiling above them.
That thought stayed with him longer than most.
Not because it troubled him.
Because it mattered.
For the first time in a long while, he was not simply bored.
He was dissatisfied with distance itself.
He could watch everything.
But he could not touch any of it.
He had tried, once.
The moment his presence brushed one of the lower realms, space cracked and time destabilized. Entire structures strained beneath him. He was too complete, too vast, too heavy for any world beneath his own sealed expanse to contain.
"…Too much."
That had always been the problem.
Not imprisonment.
Excess.
He had become something so complete that only emptiness could still hold him.
This realm was not a prison built by others.
It was the shape of what he had become.
"…So I built myself into a corner."
He laughed softly.
"…That's unfortunate."
Silence followed.
Then—
Something shifted.
A thought.
Simple.
Clear.
Beautiful in its absurdity.
"If I cannot enter those worlds…"
Mateo sat upright.
"…then I won't."
He did not mean retreat.
He meant removal.
Not suppression. Not disguise. Not a lesser version of himself.
Elimination.
He would break down everything he was.
He would dismantle the excess, fragment the weight of his existence, scatter the impossible into something finite, something smaller, something a real world could accept.
He would become mortal.
The void trembled around him.
Fragments of light appeared, thin fractures spreading through reality itself as his decision settled into truth.
"…I stop being this."
Time unraveled first. Space followed. Power split into innumerable pieces, no longer gathered into a single, overwhelming whole. Memory thinned—not erased, but stretched across something smaller. Something bounded.
As he began to fall through the layers of existence, descending toward the worlds he had only watched for so long, one final thought flickered through him.
Should he erase the sealed realm entirely?
No.
Not erased.
Not preserved.
Left behind.
Collapsed, opened, and emptied of its false limits.
Its people moved into reality.
Its excess stripped away.
Its impossible gifts denied entry unless the worlds themselves allowed them to rise into such things naturally.
That was better.
That was fair.
Then—
Darkness.
"…What?"
Weight returned first.
Then sensation.
Then walls.
Mateo opened his eyes slowly to find a wooden ceiling above him, uneven beams crossing overhead in a room so small it would once have offended him on principle.
He lifted one hand.
Tiny.
"…Oh."
For a long moment, he simply stared.
Then understanding settled into place.
"…I actually did it."
Voices moved nearby.
A woman's. Warm. Tired. Concerned.
A man's. Steadier. Watching more than speaking.
Lucía held him carefully. Rafael stood nearby with arms crossed.
"He's too quiet," Lucía murmured.
"He's looking around like he understands everything," Rafael said.
Mateo closed his eyes briefly.
	Because I do.
"…It worked," he murmured.
Lucía tilted her head. "What was that?"
"…Nothing."
He exhaled slowly, feeling the weight of a body that was no longer infinite, the confinement of bones and breath and skin.
	Good.
Far beyond that small room, the real worlds continued to turn, unaware that the thing which had once watched them from above had finally entered the game.
And somewhere, hidden beneath all of it, a sealed realm had begun to break open.
Not in death.
In release.
