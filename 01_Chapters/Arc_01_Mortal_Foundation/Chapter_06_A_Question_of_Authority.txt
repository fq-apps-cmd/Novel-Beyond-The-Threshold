By the third time Mateo was told not to go somewhere, he had already decided he would.
It wasn’t simply stubbornness, nor was it pure mischief—though he would not deny either entirely. It was, more than anything, a matter of efficiency. A restriction existed for a reason, but if that reason had never been properly examined, then the restriction itself remained questionable.
Mateo preferred clarity.
And clarity, more often than not, required testing.

“…You’re doing it again.”
Tomas’ voice carried a familiar tone of restrained disbelief as he walked alongside Mateo, who had very clearly taken a path that did not lead to any approved training ground. Their steps fell unevenly across the packed ground—one path worn from repetition, the other less defined, resisting slightly underfoot as if unused routes preferred to remain that way.
Mateo did not turn.
“…Doing what?”
“Walking toward somewhere you’re not supposed to be.”
“That’s an assumption.”
Tomas gave him a long look. “You’re heading toward the elder meeting hall.”
“…Correct.”
“…You’re not allowed in there.”
Mateo slowed slightly, finally glancing at him.
“…That depends.”
Tomas frowned faintly. “On what?”
Mateo’s gaze returned forward as he continued walking, completely unhurried. The air felt more structured the closer they moved toward the hall—not heavier, but more defined, as if intention had settled more firmly in that part of the village.
 Let’s test the stability of enforcement.
“…On whether they realize I’m not supposed to be there.”
A short silence followed.
“…That’s not how rules work,” Tomas said.
Mateo reached the door.
“…It is,” he replied calmly, placing a hand against the wood. The surface held firm beneath his touch—simple, solid, unreinforced. Predictable.
“...if enforcement fails.”
And then he opened it.

The room inside fell silent almost immediately.
A group of elders sat around a low table, their discussion halting mid-sentence as the door opened without announcement. Several of them turned sharply, expressions shifting from mild irritation to confusion as they took in the unexpected visitor.
The room itself held sound differently than outside. Voices didn’t dissipate—they lingered just long enough to overlap. The positioning, the spacing, even the height of the ceiling allowed conversation to carry farther than intended.
“…Who allowed you in here?” one of them asked, his voice firm but not yet aggressive.
Mateo stepped inside as though the question itself was optional.
“…Are you asking me,” he said mildly, “or questioning your own awareness?”
The silence that followed was different from before.
Tomas, who had stopped just inside the doorway, stared at Mateo as if something fundamental had just shifted.
 …He’s doing it again.
One of the elders leaned forward slightly, frowning.
“…You’re a child.”
Mateo inclined his head a fraction.
“…That is a current condition.”
The response made one of the elders blink.
“…Then why are you here?”
Mateo moved closer to the table, his steps calm, his posture measured in a way that did not match his age. His presence didn’t dominate the room—but it didn’t yield to it either.
“…Inspection.”
The word landed with far more weight than it should have.
“…Inspection?” another elder repeated.
Mateo gestured lightly toward the room.
“If this meeting is meant to remain private,” he said, his tone even and unhurried, “then the current arrangement is inefficient. The seating alone allows sound to carry outward without resistance.”
One of the elders frowned. “…What are you implying?”
Mateo turned his gaze toward the windows, then the doorway, then back to the table.
“You are audible from outside,” he said simply.
A brief silence followed.
Another elder shifted slightly.
“…Is that true?”
Tomas stiffened as multiple gazes turned toward him at once.
“…Yes,” he admitted after a moment. “…You can hear it from outside the hall.”
The room stilled.
The elder closest to the window rose and tested it himself, confirming the faint echo of voices carrying further than expected—lingering along the corridor before fading unevenly.
“…Why was this not noticed earlier?” he muttered.
“Because no one was standing outside listening,” another elder responded, though his tone lacked confidence now.
Mateo folded his hands behind his back, already turning slightly away.
 Minimal pressure. Maximum disruption.
“…I assumed you were aware,” he added.

“…Wait.”
The word came from one of the older men, his expression narrowing as he studied Mateo more carefully.
“…On whose authority are you conducting this ‘inspection’?”
Mateo paused.
He did not turn immediately.
 Now they’re uncertain.
When he did face them again, his expression remained calm.
“…Authority,” he said slowly, “is often misunderstood. If a door can be opened, it is not truly closed. If information can be heard, then it is not truly controlled. If presence goes unnoticed…”
He allowed the sentence to trail.
The room felt… unsteady for a moment. Not physically—but in certainty.
“…then the limitation only exists in theory.”
The elders exchanged brief glances, uncertainty growing in a way that had not existed moments ago.
“…That still does not explain why you are here,” one of them pressed.
Mateo considered the question, then answered plainly.
“…Because no one stopped me.”
That answer lingered longer than it should have.
Behind him, Tomas closed his eyes briefly.
 …This shouldn’t work.

“…Wait.”
Another elder leaned forward.
“…You’re Rafael’s boy.”
The moment shifted.
Structure returned.
Reality settled.
Mateo blinked once.
“…Correct.”
“…You’re not an inspector.”
“…Also correct.”
“…Then what are you doing here?”
Mateo thought about it.
Then answered honestly.
“…Testing something.”
“…And the result?”
“…It works.”
“…Get out.”
Mateo nodded once without resistance.
“…Reasonable.”
He turned and walked toward the door.

Outside, the air felt lighter.
Not calmer—just less constrained. Sound moved more freely, and the subtle structure of the meeting space no longer held around them.
Tomas stood still for a moment, then followed.
“…Explain.”
Mateo continued walking as though nothing unusual had occurred.
“…They were already uncertain.”
“That’s not an explanation.”
Mateo glanced at him.
“…It is.”
Tomas stopped walking altogether.
“…You walked into a restricted meeting, convinced them you had authority, disrupted their discussion, and left.”
“…Yes.”
“…Why?”
Mateo paused.
For once, he didn’t answer immediately.
Then he turned, and for a brief moment, his posture shifted again—subtly, but unmistakably.
“…Understanding a system requires testing its boundaries,” he said, his voice steady. “If a rule cannot withstand pressure, then it was never solid to begin with. Questioning is not inefficiency—ignorance is.”
Tomas didn’t interrupt. He simply stared.
“…If no one challenges unnecessary limitations,” Mateo continued, “then those limitations persist without reason.”
For a moment—
It made sense.

Then Mateo relaxed, the tension in his posture dissolving as quickly as it had appeared.
“…Also,” he added casually, “it was funny.”
Tomas blinked.
“…What?”
Mateo turned and resumed walking.
“…Watching them question everything was entertaining.”

Diego, who had somehow been present the entire time without being noticed, rushed up beside them.
“…You walked into an elder meeting and made them doubt their own authority?”
“…Briefly.”
“That’s incredible.”
“…It was efficient.”
Tomas remained where he stood for a moment longer before finally moving again.
 I don’t understand him.
Pause.
 He speaks like someone far older… then says things like that.
“…You’re a problem,” Tomas said.
Mateo didn’t look back.
“…That has already been established.”

That night, Mateo sat alone beneath the open sky, the faint silence of the village settling around him. The stillness wasn’t empty—it held small inconsistencies, faint sounds that carried too far or not far enough, subtle signs that quiet did not always mean absence.
The pendant rested in his palm, its fractured star catching the dim light above.
This time—
It pulsed.
Not strongly.
But distinctly.
 …More consistent now.
Mateo watched it closely.
 Fragments… moving.
The thought came more clearly than before.
 Not broken.
A pause.
 Incomplete.
He closed his hand around it.

And for just a moment—
An image surfaced.
A forge.
Heat.
Metal shaping under steady hands.
A large figure, unmoving, unwavering.
The glow of metal held longer than it should have, edges sharper than memory.
Then it was gone.

Mateo exhaled slowly.
“…Gramps.”
The word came without certainty—but it did not feel wrong.
He leaned back slightly, looking up at the sky.
 Something was left behind.
 Something still forming.
A faint smile touched his expression.
“…Good.”
For once, the unknown was not inconvenient.
It was interesting.
And Mateo had always preferred things that were worth understanding.
