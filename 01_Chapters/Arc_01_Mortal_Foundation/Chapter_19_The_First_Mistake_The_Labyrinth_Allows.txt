Mateo continued walking, unhurried, aware of the quiet pressure surrounding him. The path no longer resisted his choice, but neither did it guide him. That absence of resistance unsettled him more than obstruction would have.
It’s letting me move.
He frowned slightly. “…Or it already adjusted.” That meant either he had chosen correctly… or he had been allowed to.
The corridor narrowed slightly, then widened again with unnatural precision. The stone had changed—not in appearance alone, but in purpose. The surfaces carried intent, something placed and forgotten, something that remembered being built.
Mateo brushed his fingers along the wall as he walked. “…You weren’t shaped like this randomly,” he murmured. “…There’s pattern… even in the parts pretending not to have one.” A pause. “…So you’re not broken. Just… incomplete.”
And deeper within—something lingered. Not a presence, not yet, but the suggestion of one. Like a thought too large to fully form.
Mateo slowed, letting his gaze widen instead of narrowing, taking in structure, alignment, the subtle inconsistencies in spacing and angle.
Something is different. Not just the walls. Not just the path.
“The space itself,” he said quietly.
He stopped completely.
Stillness. Listening. Not with his ears—but with everything else.
…There.
A faint unease settled in his chest. Not fear. Not danger.
Recognition.
“…Layered,” he murmured. “…You’re layered on top of something else.”
Something about this place feels… incomplete. Like it knows more than it should.
He exhaled slowly. “…You’re not reacting,” he continued. “…You’re adjusting based on interaction.”
No answer came. But the feeling didn’t fade.
That was enough.
He didn’t move forward. Instead, he crouched and closed his eyes. “I can’t rely on sight alone,” he said softly. “You manipulate alignment before movement. That means you decide what looks correct—and what doesn’t.” A faint pause. “…That’s inefficient.” Another pause. “…For me.”
A memory surfaced—not his, not entirely. Fragments. Observations.
He had once watched beings who did not rely on vision at all. They didn’t “see.” They felt structure.
Mateo opened his eyes slowly. “…I don’t have that.” Not yet. A pause. “…So I approximate it.”
He sat fully now, ignoring the pressure of the space. “My aura is weak,” he said quietly. “…but weakness only matters if I waste it.”
Sending it out directly… wastes too much.
He needed precision. Something small. Controlled.
“A signal,” he murmured. “Not a release.”
He inhaled slowly, gathering what little aura he could access, compressing it inward first. “…Too much spread…” He tightened it. “…Stabilize first.”
A pulse. Not dispersed. Directed.
“…Not a wave. A ping.”
He refined further, thinning it, stretching it. “…Less presence… more interaction.”
Then—he released it.
The pulse spread. Not far. But enough.
And then—it returned.
Not clean. Not consistent. Delayed.
Mateo’s eyes sharpened. “…Took longer.” A faint nod. “…Which means you processed it differently.”
The feedback came back, distorted.
Stone here. Open space there. Something deeper—offset, not aligned beneath him.
“…That’s not depth,” he murmured. A pause. “…That’s fold.”
His gaze lifted slightly. “…Or overlap.”
A faint smile touched his expression. “…Interesting,” he said quietly. “Very interesting.”
His foot brushed something. He stopped and looked down.
A fragment, half-buried in stone, brushed smooth by time.
He crouched, brushing it free. “…This doesn’t belong here naturally.”
A plate of thin metal—warped slightly, edges worn. Etched faintly.
Not a full rune. Not complete.
“…Partial inscription,” Mateo murmured.
He turned it slightly in the dim glow. “…Too shallow for a talisman core… too precise for decoration…” He paused. “…Interface piece.”
A faint trace of residue lingered.
“…Alchemy tool?” he guessed softly. “…Or part of a refining surface.”
A beat.
“…So someone worked down here.”
His gaze lifted slightly. “…And not recently.”
He stored the fragment carefully.
Another pulse.
This time—something answered faster.
Small. Low. Moving.
Mateo stopped mid-step. “…Closer this time.”
Left side. Low. Staggered movement.
The creature lunged.
Mateo shifted—but this time the creature adjusted mid-motion.
Faster.
Mateo’s eyes flickered. “…You’re not reacting randomly,” he murmured. “…You’re deploying variation.”
His hand moved. Precise. Measured. Directed.
He struck beneath the shell.
The creature dropped instantly. Still.
Mateo crouched beside it. “…Same weak core.” A pause. “…Different engagement speed.”
He turned it slightly. “…Not hunting behavior.” Another pause. “…Interruption class.”
He exhaled quietly. “…You’re filtering mistakes.”
He stored it.
Another pulse.
This time—two returns. One ahead. One above.
Mateo froze, then slowly lifted his head.
The ceiling didn’t behave like one.
“…That’s wrong.” A pause. “…Or more accurately—that’s not real space.”
He exhaled slowly. “…Folded structure,” he murmured. “…Compact outside… expanded inside.”
A faint smile formed. “…That explains the density.”
He stood, looking forward again. “…Then this place is bigger than it should be.”
A longer pause.
“…Which means there’s more worth finding.”
His interest sharpened.
The path shifted again.
Faster.
Less delay. Less warning.
He stepped—and the corridor subtly adjusted before he finished placing his foot.
Mateo stopped. “…You’re shortening reaction time.” A faint nod. “…So hesitation becomes error.”
The room ahead opened—and twisted.
“…That’s new.”
He pulsed again.
The response shattered—multiple returns, layered, some true, some false.
Mateo’s gaze sharpened. “…Interference.” A small breath. “…You’re increasing noise to reduce accuracy.”
He straightened slightly. “…Good.”
More layers. More distortion. More challenge.
“…And more data,” he added quietly.
For the first time, he looked forward not cautiously—but with clear interest.
He didn’t rush.
“I don’t have enough information yet,” he said calmly.
A pause.
“…And I’m not wasting an opportunity by guessing.”
He exhaled slowly, measured, controlled. “…Not today.”
The decision settled cleanly.
He turned.
Not retreat.
Recalibration.
“…You’re worth coming back to.”
The labyrinth didn’t stop him. Didn’t shift. Didn’t escalate further.
It simply remained.
Storing.
Resetting.
Waiting.
