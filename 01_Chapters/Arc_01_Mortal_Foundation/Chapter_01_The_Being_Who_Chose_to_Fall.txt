“…Again?”
The word carried no impatience, no anger—only the quiet exhaustion of something that had repeated itself too many times to hold meaning anymore.
Mateo drifted in a place that could not truly be called a world. There was no sky, no ground, no direction unless he allowed those ideas to exist. Distance folded at his will, and even silence—absolute, complete silence—felt artificial, as though it were something he had once chosen and then forgotten how to undo. Everything rested in perfect stillness, a sealed and endless expanse shaped entirely by his intent.
He raised one hand.
Reality unfolded.
Stars ignited across the void in perfect formation, each one placed with precision that required no effort. Galaxies spiraled outward in elegant arcs, space curving naturally to support them. Time layered itself across existence in smooth, uninterrupted motion, and in the spaces between it all, worlds began to form. Mountains rose. Oceans spread. Wind moved. Life—bright, fragile, stubborn life—began.
For a moment, Mateo watched.
Then he sighed.
“… Its Incomplete.”
His fingers curled inward, and everything collapsed. Not violently, not chaotically—just… undone. Light vanished. Time dissolved. Structure unraveled so thoroughly that even the idea of it seemed to fade with it.
Silence returned.
Mateo leaned back into the void, one arm behind his head.
“I’ve run out of things to do.”
There had been a time when that would have sounded tragic. Now, it was simply… accurate.
Once, creation had fascinated him. He had made realities the way others might sketch into dust—testing systems, rewriting laws, reshaping the way energy moved through existence. Worlds built themselves, failed, adapted, collapsed, and tried again beneath his observation. Civilizations rose from nothing, developed language, built power, formed doctrine, and vanished without leaving anything that surprised him for long.
At first, variation was enough.
Eventually, it wasn’t.
Patterns revealed themselves too quickly. Chaos followed rules once observed long enough. Even destruction lost meaning when nothing was permanent unless he chose it to be.
Except—
His awareness shifted downward.
Beyond his sealed realm, beneath layers of existence without count, were other worlds.
Real ones.
Not shaped entirely by him. Not contained by his expectations.
The Mortal Realms.
“…Let’s see what's going on in the real world.”
This time, he did not focus on one life.
He watched many.
A boy struggled beneath the weight of a blade too large for him, arms trembling, stance broken, yet refusing to release it. A girl practiced beneath relentless rain, repeating the same strike until every unnecessary motion was stripped from her body. A man knelt in mud and blood, barely conscious, yet still refused to fall forward.
Mateo watched as time unfolded in sweeping motion. Days became years. Years became decades.
“…Persistent.”
But persistence alone was never the whole story.
“…Here it comes.”
The change was never dramatic. There was no gate, no voice, no divine hand descending from the heavens. Instead, something more fundamental occurred.
The world simply stopped being enough.
They outgrew it.
One by one, they vanished—not destroyed, not erased—just… absent. Then, after the briefest moment, they appeared again in a higher world. A denser world. A harsher world.
And immediately—
They were no longer exceptional.
The boy who had once dominated faltered. The girl who had perfected her form found it insufficient. The man who endured nearly collapsed under pressure that demanded more than survival.
“…They always think they’ve reached the end.”
Mateo observed another.
A genius.
Someone whose talent eclipsed everyone in his realm. Every technique came easily. Every fight ended quickly. His rise required no resistance.
He ascended.
And stagnated.
In a world where others possessed similar brilliance—paired with deeper refinement, stronger foundations, harsher histories—his advantage disappeared. He had never needed to struggle deeply, never needed to build resilience through failure.
Without that—
He stalled.
Then faded.
“…Predictable.”
Then Mateo watched something else.
Someone overlooked.
A cultivator dismissed as mediocre. His progress uneven. His techniques flawed. His growth inconsistent.
He failed.
Repeatedly.
Yet he continued.
He ascended.
At first, nothing changed. In the next realm, he was still ignored. Still dismissed.
But he did not stop.
Centuries passed.
Then millennia.
The same figures who had once dismissed him could do nothing but look upward in silence. While others relied on talent that failed them when challenged, he relied on persistence that never stopped compounding.
He did not rise quickly.
He rose absolutely.
Mateo smiled faintly.
“Interesting.”
Talent mattered.
So did effort, timing, pressure, environment, inheritance.
But none of them alone determined the outcome.
There was no single path to ascension.
That was what held his attention.
Variation.
He continued observing. Another figure caught his gaze—someone from the lower realms crossing paths with a cultivator from a higher lineage. They fought first. Then again. Then spoke. Then fought again. Over time, competition turned into something else.
Understanding.
Eventually—
Alliance.
Not all strength produced arrogance. Not all high-born cultivators looked downward. Not all powerful structures rejected what came from below.
That, too, mattered.
It made those worlds feel… alive.
Not predetermined.
And yet—
There was a truth Mateo understood too well.
Not all lower realms were equal.
He knew because he had created some of them himself.
Within his own sealed realms, life had flourished. Civilizations grew beneath artificial skies. Cultivators rose, built sects, formed clans, and believed the limits of their world were the limits of existence.
But Mateo knew the truth.
They were contained.
Invisible ceilings existed. Thresholds that could not be crossed, no matter how much effort or talent existed within them. The system simply refused to allow anything beyond a certain point.
To those inside—
They were powerful.
Outside—
They were small.
Not worthless. Not weak.
But incomplete.
He had always disliked that.
Because they had not failed.
They had simply been born into something that could not carry them further.
So when Mateo finally made his decision, he did not destroy those worlds.
He changed them.
He moved them.
Every life, every civilization, every being that had existed within his sealed existence was woven into real worlds—adjusted, balanced, rewritten into existence as though they had always belonged there.
Not stronger.
Not weaker.
Equal.
The overwhelming advantages those beings possessed could not remain. Divine inheritances, unnatural foundations, impossible advantages—if introduced directly into true worlds, they would destabilize everything.
So Mateo removed that excess.
Not their lives.
Not their potential.
Only the imbalance.
This time—
They would rise properly.
Without artificial ceilings.
That thought lingered longer than most.
Because for the first time in a long while, he wasn’t bored.
He was dissatisfied with distance.
He could watch everything.
But he could not touch it.
He had tried once.
The moment his presence brushed a lower realm, space fractured and time warped. Entire dimensions strained beneath him.
“…Too much.”
He was too complete.
Too vast.
Too heavy.
This void was not a prison.
It was the only thing that could contain him.
“…So I built myself into a corner.”
He laughed softly.
“…That’s unfortunate.”
Then—
A thought formed.
Simple.
Direct.
“If I cannot enter those worlds…”
He sat up.
“…then I won’t.”
Not through suppression.
Not through disguise.
Through removal.
He would dismantle himself.
Not reduce.
Not hide.
Remove what made him too much.
Time unraveled first. Space fractured. Power shattered into countless fragments. His existence—once infinite—began to contract into something bounded.
From limitless—
To defined.
As he fell through existence toward the worlds he had once only watched, one final thought surfaced.
Should he erase it?
“…No.”
Not erased.
Not preserved.
Left behind.
Opened.
Released.
Then—
Darkness.
“…What?”
Weight returned first.
Then sensation.
Then boundaries.
Mateo opened his eyes slowly.
A wooden ceiling.
Uneven beams crossing above him.
Small.
Confined.
He lifted one hand.
Tiny.
“…Oh.”
He stared for a long moment.
Then—
“…I actually did it.”
Voices moved nearby.
Lucía held him carefully. Rafael stood close, watching more than speaking.
“He’s too quiet,” Lucía murmured.
“He’s looking like he understands too much,” Rafael said.
Mateo closed his eyes briefly.
Because I do.
“…It worked,” he said quietly.
Lucía blinked. “What was that?”
“…Nothing.”
He exhaled slowly, feeling breath compress inside a body that was no longer infinite. The weight of bone. The constraint of flesh.
Good.
Far beyond that small room, the worlds continued to turn—unaware that something which had once watched them from above had finally entered their reach.
And somewhere, beneath everything—
A sealed realm had begun to break open.
Not in destruction.
In release.

For a brief moment, Mateo paused.
Not because something had made a sound—
and not because he had seen anything.
The air felt…
off.
Not heavier. Not lighter.
Just slightly out of place, like something had shifted in a way the world hadn’t fully corrected yet.
His gaze lifted faintly toward nothing in particular.
Then—
It was gone.
