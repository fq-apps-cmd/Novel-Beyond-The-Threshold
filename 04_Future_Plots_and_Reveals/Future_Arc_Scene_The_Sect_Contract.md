# Future Arc Scene — "The Sect Contract" (Locked In)

Future Arc Scene — "The Contract" (Locked In)  
When This Happens  
Somewhere after the forest arc, when Mateo is engaging with a sect, guild, or larger organizational structure for the first t ime. He's known enough to attract attention but still publicly underestimated. His true  
cultivation level is hidden — everyone assumes he's significantly lower than he actually is.  
The Setup  
A recruiter or elder from the organization spots Mateo and sees potential — but not the full picture. This person is experienced, shrewd, and accustomed to signing talented young cultivators into contr acts that  
heavily favor the organization. He's done this dozens of times. He knows how to dangle resources, status, and opportunity in front of hungry young cultivators and walk away with a deal that locks them in for years.  
He thinks Mateo is a promising but underdeveloped talent.  
He is catastrophically wrong.  

The Contract Scene — Fully Updated with More Humor  
The Approach  
Mateo first notices Elder Dracon the way most people do — as an obstacle.  
The old man is sitting on a bench near the sect's recruitment pavilion, eating something out of a cracked bowl with the unhur ried contentment of someone who has achieved complete harmony with the concept of  
lunch. His robe is open at the collar, revealing a thin undershirt that has seen better centuries. One sandal is on. The othe r is beside him on the bench, being used as a plate for what appears to be dried fruit.  
He is using a sandal as a plate.  
Mateo observes this.  
Files it.  
Continues walking.  
"Hey."  
Mateo slows.  
"You. Kid."  
Mateo turns. The old man is pointing at him with a pair of chopsticks that are definitely not regulation sect -issue and may in fact be carved from a tree branch.  
"...Yes?"  
"You here for recruitment?"  
"...Possibly."  
The old man studies him. This is the moment where the casual spiritual scan occurs — a lazy extension of awareness, the cultivator equivalent of sizing someone up by their shoes.  
The scan returns: nothing interesting.  
Dracon nods to himself.  
"Sit down."  
Mateo looks at the bench. The available space is occupied by the sandal-plate and a collection of items that defy immediate categorization.  
"...Where?"  
Dracon looks at the bench. Looks at the items. Looks at Mateo.  
Then sweeps everything off the bench with one arm, sending dried fruit, a clay cup, something wrapped in cloth, and the sanda l-plate clattering to the ground.  
"There."  
Mateo stares at the debris.  
Then sits down.  
This is going to be interesting.  
The Negotiation  
The elder leans forward, expression warm, voice measured — the practiced generosity of someone who has calculated exactly how much he can offer while still coming out ahead.  

"I'll be straight with you," Dracon says, leaning forward with the practiced warmth of someone who has done this many times b efore. "I see potential. Real potential. And I don't say that often."  
He says it constantly. He said it to three other recruits this week. Two of them were below average. The third was a cat that had wandered into the pavilion.  
He signed the cat.  
It's fine. The paperwork will sort itself out. It always does.  
"I'm prepared to offer you a recruitment package," Dracon continues. "Monthly stipend. Resource access. Training allocation. The works."  
He produces a scroll from somewhere inside his robe — a space that should not be able to contain a scroll of that size, but Mateo chooses not to examine that detail too closely.  
"Ten Tier 5 spirit stones per month."  
Mateo's expression does not change.  
Internally, however, something shifts.  
Ten Gold spirit stones. Per month.  
He is currently in possession of zero spirit stones of any tier. His entire financial history in this world consists of herbs he grew himself, items he borrowed without explicit permission, and one incident involving old  
man Chen's shop that remains officially unsolved.  
Ten Gold stones per month is not a fortune by sect standards. For inner disciples of large sects, it is a reasonable allowanc e. For outer disciples, it is generous. For someone at the level Dracon believes Mateo to be at,  
it is extraordinary.  
Mateo knows this.  
He also knows that his face must not show this.  
Because if his face shows this — if even a flicker of the internal calculation currently racing through his mind becomes visible — the negotiation shifts, and he needs it to stay exactly where it is.  
"...That is generous," Mateo says, his tone carrying the precise calibration of someone who is impressed but trying not to ap pear desperate.  
Dracon nods. Magnanimous.  
Internally, Dracon is running his own calculations.  
The kid's maybe Spirit Awakening. Could be Qi Gathering peak. Either way, he's years from Core Formation. If he makes it at a ll. The ten Gold stones are bait — enough to make the offer irresistible, cheap enough that  
the sect won't notice for years. By the time the kid hits a ceiling and stagnates, the sect will have gotten its money's wort h in labor, and Dracon will have gotten credit for another recruitment. Everyone wins. Mostly  
Dracon.  
"There's more," Dracon says.  
Of course there is.  
"Access to the mid-tier resource hall. Personal training allocation — six hours per month with a designated instructor. And..."  
He pauses. This is the hook. The part he's most proud of.  
"If you reach Core Formation before the age of twenty-five..."  
He lets it hang.  
Mateo waits.  
"...automatic elevation to Core Disciple, Tier 3. Enhanced monthly stipend of fifteen Gold spirit stones. And a one-time signing bonus of..."  
Another pause. Dramatic. Dracon is enjoying this.  
"...fifty Tier 5 spirit stones."  
He says this the way a fisherman describes the size of his bait — with full knowledge that no fish will ever actually reach it.  
Mateo blinks.  
Once.  
Fifty Gold spirit stones. As a signing bonus. Triggered by a condition he has already met.  
The internal response is immediate, involuntary, and requires every ounce of control Mateo possesses to keep off his face.  
Fifty. Gold. Stones.  
He could buy old man Chen's entire shop. Multiple times. He could buy old man Chen. He could fund his own herb operation, equ ip himself for three expeditions into the deep forest, and still have enough left over to  

eat well for a year.  
And this man is offering it as a throwaway clause he never expects to pay.  
Mateo's hands, resting on his knees, do not move. His breathing does not change. His expression maintains the careful balance of a young cultivator weighing a life-changing opportunity.  
But somewhere behind his eyes, in the part of his mind that no one has ever been able to read, a very small, very quiet voice says:  
...I am going to take everything this man owns.  
"...Can I ask a question about the stipend?" Mateo says.  
Dracon beams. The hook is set.  
"Of course."  
"...The ten Gold spirit stones. Are they disbursed from the signing date, or from the first monthly cycle?"  
Dracon's internal response: He's already thinking about the money. Beautiful. He's hooked. Landed. Done.  
"From the signing date," Dracon says, waving a hand magnanimously. "We're not stingy here."  
Mateo nods slowly.  
"...And the Core Formation clause — section four. The signing bonus triggers upon verified achievement of the condition, regardless of when within the contract p eriod it occurs?"  
Dracon nods again. Why wouldn't he? The clause is a fantasy. A carrot on a stick so long the donkey would die of old age befo re reaching it.  
"Correct. Anytime before twenty-five. You've got years."  
Mateo reads the contract one more time. Every line. Every clause. Every condition.  
He picks up the brush.  
...This man has no idea what is about to happen to him.  
"...I accept."  
He signs.  
The Reveal  
The contract is signed. Sealed. Binding.  
Dracon leans back, satisfied. Another successful recruitment. He's already mentally composing the version of this story he'll tell over dinner — the promising young recruit, the generous offer, the masterful  
negotiation. He might embellish slightly. He usually does.  
Mateo sets down the brush.  
Then looks up.  
"...About the Core Formation clause," Mateo says.  
Dracon smiles warmly. "You have plenty of time. Don't rush yourself. Focus on building a solid foundation and —"  
"...I would like to collect now."  
The smile doesn't fade.  
It fossilizes.  
Dracon's face holds the expression of warmth and generosity for approximately two more seconds while his brain catches up to his ears. The result is a facial expression that has never existed before in nature — a  
smile that is technically still present but has been completely evacuated of all meaning, like a house with the lights on and nobody home.  
"...Excuse me?"  
"The contract states that upon verified achievement of Core Formation before the age of twenty-five, all associated benefits activate immediately. Enhanced stipend. Core Disciple elevation. And the signing bonus  
outlined in section four."  
Mateo's voice has not changed. His posture has not changed. His expression remains the same mild, slightly grateful, slightly uncertain look he has been wearing throughout the entire negotiation.  
"...I have met the conditions."  
Dracon stares at him.  
"...You — that's not — you're not —"  
"...Would you like verification?"  
And then Mateo releases his aura.  
Not dramatically. Not explosively. He simply... stops holding it back. The suppression that he has maintained so carefully, s o consistently, for so long — he sets it down the way someone might set down a bag they've  
been carrying. Casually. Without ceremony.  
And the room changes.  
The air compresses. Not painfully, not threateningly — but with a density that is unmistakable. The ambient energy in the room shifts, orienting itself around Mateo the way iron fi lings orient around a magnet.  
Core Formation.  
Stable.  
Settled.  
Not fresh. Not fragile. Not the shaky, barely-held core of someone who just broke through.  
Established.  
Dracon's mouth opens.  
Closes.  
Opens again.  
He stares at Mateo.  
Then at the contract.  
Then at Mateo.  
Then at the contract again.  
Then at his own hand — the one that signed the contract — as though it has personally betrayed him.  
And then — the realization hits.  
Not the aura. He's a dragon who once arm-wrestled a celestial beast for territorial rights over a mountain range. A Core Formation brat doesn't make his hair stand on end.  
But the understanding of what just happened — the slow, excruciating, frame-by-frame reconstruction of the last ten minutes, every word Mateo said, every hesitation that was actually calculation, every gr ateful smile  
that was actually a predator positioning its jaws, the question about the signing date that wasn't curiosity but confirmation , the question about section four that wasn't clarification but target acquisition — THAT  
makes his hair stand on end.  
Not from spiritual pressure.  
From pure, undiluted, volcanic indignation.  
His hair rises — slowly, strand by strand — propelled not by any external force but by the sheer internal pressure of a man whose pride has just been surgically removed, turned inside out, and handed back to him in  
front of a contract that he wrote himself.  
His face cycles through colors. His hands grip the edge of the bench. His spiritual pressure flares involuntarily — not as an attack, but as the energetic equivalent of a man's blood pressure spiking so hard his body  
forgets to regulate itself.  
He points at Mateo.  
His finger trembles.  
Not from fear.  
From the specific fury of someone who has lived longer than civilizations, outwitted beings that could reshape reality, stare d down tribulations that would annihilate worlds — and has just been hustled by a teenager  
over a piece of paper he wrote on a bench next to a sandal he was using as a plate.  
"You — you — you little —"  
He sputters.  
"You KNEW! YOU KNEW BEFORE YOU SAT DOWN!"  
"...I knew before you spoke," Mateo says calmly.  
"YOU — YOU SAT THERE AND — THE FACE — YOU MADE THAT FACE —"  
"...Which face?"  
"THE GRATEFUL FACE! THE 'OH THIS IS SO GENEROUS' FACE! YOU — THAT WAS FAKE?!"  
"...I was genuinely grateful," Mateo says. "The terms were very favorable."  
"FOR YOU! THEY WERE FAVORABLE FOR YOU!"  
"...That is what generous means."  
Dracon makes a sound that is not a word in any language, living or dead. It is the vocal equivalent of a man's dignity leavin g his body.  
"The stipend — TEN GOLD STONES — I offered you TEN GOLD STONES thinking you'd be GRATEFUL for YEARS —"  
"...I am grateful. I would like my first payment now, please."  
"AND THE SIGNING BONUS — FIFTY — FIFTY GOLD —"  
"...Section four was very clearly written."  

"I WROTE SECTION FOUR!"  
"...Then you should be familiar with its terms."  
"I WROTE IT BECAUSE NO ONE EVER MEETS THE CONDITIONS!"  
"...Someone has now met the conditions."  
"YOU MET THEM BEFORE THE INK WAS DRY!"  
"...Technically, I met them before I sat down. The ink is a separate matter."  
Dracon's left eye twitches. His right hand is still pointing at Mateo. His left hand is gripping the contract so tightly the edges have begun to crumple. A vein has appeared on his forehead that was not previously visible  
and seems to have its own heartbeat.  
"You — you absolute — you shameless little —"  
He searches for the word.  
Mateo waits patiently.  
"...THIEF."  
"...I signed a contract that you offered," Mateo says. "That is the opposite of theft."  
"IT IS THEFT WITH EXTRA STEPS."  
"...It is commerce."  
"IT IS — YOU —"  
Dracon stops. Breathes. Forces his hand down. Forces his hair down. Forces the vein on his forehead to stop pulsating, though it complies only reluctantly.  
He closes his eyes.  
Opens them.  
And smiles.  
Too calmly.  
Too smoothly.  
The shift from apoplectic rage to serene composure happens in under two seconds, and it is the most terrifying thing Mateo ha s seen since arriving in this world.  
The Reversal  
"Well," Dracon says, his voice suddenly silk. "That is certainly impressive. Truly remarkable. A young man of your caliber is exactly what this sect needs."  
Mateo's eyes narrow.  
Something just changed.  
"Unfortunately..."  
That word.  
That single word carries the weight of centuries of shamelessness refined to a razor edge.  
"...I don't actually have the authority to finalize contracts of this tier."  
Silence.  
"...What," Mateo says.  
"This contract requires countersignature from the sect's inner council. Without that, the resource allocation, the stipend ad justments, and — ah, yes — the signing bonus outlined in section four are all..."  
He shrugs.  
Broadly.  
With the specific energy of a man who has just discovered an escape hatch he did not know existed until thirty seconds ago an d is now sprinting through it with absolute shamelessness.  
"...pending."  
Mateo stares at him.  
"...You offered me terms you don't have the authority to honor."  
"I offered terms I fully intended to honor," Dracon corrects, standing up and brushing crumbs off his robe with the dignity o f a man who was not just eating dried fruit off a sandal twenty minutes ago. "The authority  
question is merely procedural."  
"...There is no procedural clause in section four."  
"I'm sure there's a subsection somewhere."  
"...There is no subsection."  
"Then I'll draft one."  
"...You cannot retroactively add clauses to a signed contract."  
"Watch me."  
Dracon adjusts his remaining sandal. Picks up the other one from the ground. Does not put it on. Tucks it under his arm. Nods once at Mateo with the warm, avuncular smile of a man who is about to commit a crime  
against contractual law and feels absolutely no remorse about it.  
"I'll submit the paperwork at my earliest convenience."  
He turns toward the exit.  
"...That contract is binding," Mateo says, standing.  
"Absolutely," Dracon agrees, already walking. "Binding pending review."  
"...There is no review clause."  
"There is now."  
"...You just said that."  
"It bears repeating."  
Mateo takes a step forward.  
Dracon takes a step faster.  
"...Elder Dracon."  
"Terribly sorry, urgent business, sect matters, you understand —"  
"You owe me fifty Gold spirit stones."  
"Pending review —"  
"AND ten Gold stones for this month's stipend."  
"I'll look into it —"  
"That is SIXTY Gold spirit stones —"  
"Math is a wonderful skill, you should cultivate it along with everything else —"  
And then Elder Dracon does something that no elder of any sect should ever do in front of a new recruit.  
He runs.  
Not a dignified retreat. Not a strategic withdrawal. Not even the hurried walk of someone with somewhere important to be.  
He bolts.  
Full speed, worn robe flapping, one sandal on, the other tucked under his arm like a stolen treasure, moving with a velocity that should not be physically possible for a man his age, in that condition, with that footwear.  
Disciples in the courtyard stop and stare.  
A junior instructor drops a training manual.  
Two outer disciples who had been sparring freeze mid-exchange as the old man blurs past them trailing the scent of cheap wine and contractual fraud.  
Mateo stands in the recruitment pavilion for approximately one and a half seconds.  
Then he moves.  
"GET BACK HERE."  
The door slams open.  
"THAT CONTRACT IS SIGNED AND SEALED —"  
"PENDING —"  
"STOP SAYING PENDING —"  
"— REVIEW —"  
"THERE IS NO REVIEW —"  
"I'M INVOKING ELDER PRIVILEGE —"  
"THAT IS NOT A REAL THING —"  
"IT IS ABSOLUTELY A REAL THING I AM INVOKING IT RIGHT NOW —"  
They tear through the compound — a disreputable old man and a teenager who should not be able to keep up with him but absolutely is — leaving a trail of bewildered disciples, scattered paperwork, one overturned  
tea set, and a profound institutional crisis in their wake.  

A senior disciple presses himself against a wall as Dracon rounds a corner at full speed.  
"Was that — was that Elder Dracon?"  
"...Running?"  
"I didn't know he could run."  
"I didn't know he could move that fast."  
"What's chasing him?"  
"...The new recruit."  
"...The one who just signed?"  
"...Apparently."  
Mateo rounds the same corner half a second later.  
"YOU ARE A THIEF," Mateo shouts.  
"I AM A RESPECTED ELDER OF THIS SECT —"  
"YOU ARE A THIEF IN AN ELDER'S ROBE —"  
"THESE ARE NOT MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE —"  
"GIVE ME MY SPIRIT STONES —"  
"WHAT SPIRIT STONES —"  
"THE SIXTY GOLD SPIRIT STONES YOU OWE ME —"  
"I DON'T RECALL ANY SUCH ARRANGEMENT —"  
"YOU SIGNED IT TWENTY MINUTES AGO —"  
"MY MEMORY IS NOT WHAT IT USED TO BE —"  
"YOUR MEMORY WAS FINE WHEN YOU WERE LISTING THE TERMS —"  
"THAT WAS A DIFFERENT CONVERSATION —"  
"IT WAS THE SAME CONVERSATION —"  
"AGREE TO DISAGREE —"  
From a balcony above, the sect's vice-leader watches the chase unfold with the resigned expression of someone who has seen Elder Dracon cause problems before but n ever quite at this velocity or volume.  
He sips his tea.  
"...Who is that chasing Elder Dracon?"  
A junior attendant beside him swallows.  
"...The new recruit, sir."  
"...How long has he been here?"  
"...Approximately fifteen minutes, sir."  
The vice-leader sets down his tea.  
"...What is he shouting about?"  
"...Spirit stones, sir. Sixty of them. Gold tier."  
A long pause.  
"...Elder Dracon signed a contract with financial obligations?"  
"...It appears so, sir."  
The vice-leader picks up his tea again.  
"...This is going to be expensive."
