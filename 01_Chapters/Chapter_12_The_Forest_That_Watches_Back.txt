The forest did not greet Mateo when he entered. It simply… continued. That, in itself, was enough to make most cautious.

For Mateo, it was relaxing.

He moved without urgency, his steps light but deliberate, his body naturally adjusting to every irregularity in the terrain without conscious effort. Where roots twisted beneath the ground, his ankle corrected before imbalance could form. Where the soil softened unexpectedly, his toes flexed, redistributing weight through the ball of his foot rather than sinking into it. There was no wasted motion. And more importantly—no need to rush.

By midday, Mateo had already stopped taking the forest seriously. He found a decent patch of sunlight breaking through the dense canopy and lay back against a slightly elevated mound of roots, one arm resting behind his head. Above him, the sky fractured into pieces through layered branches. The wind shifted lazily. Something small scurried nearby.

Mateo closed his eyes. "…This is better," he murmured.

He didn't sleep. Not really. His breathing slowed, his pulse evened, but his awareness stretched outward instead of inward. He could feel subtle movement through the ground, minor shifts in pressure where creatures passed nearby, the faint disruption of airflow when something larger moved between trees. His mind… drifted. But only on the surface.

That was when the forest decided to challenge his idea of "relaxation."

The air changed. Not dramatically. But enough. Mateo's eyes opened just slightly. Heavy steps. Controlled. Multiple. Then—they appeared.

A pack. Not small. Not weak. At the front—the alpha. Its fur shimmered faintly under filtered light, not quite metallic, but reflective in a way that suggested structure beneath softness. Its frame carried density beyond normal beasts—every movement controlled, every shift measured. Behind it, several younger wolves lingered close, still learning but already dangerous. The alpha stopped. Its eyes locked onto Mateo.

Mateo did not move. "…You're in the way," he said calmly.

Silence. The wolf growled low, its body lowering slightly as it assessed him. Mateo sighed. "…I am not food. I am not threat. I am also currently comfortable."

The wolf blinked. That was new. Mateo tilted his head slightly, studying it with equal interest. Beneath the growl, he could feel something else—confusion, layered with instinctual caution. "…You should leave," Mateo added. "There are better uses of your time."

The pack did not move. One of the younger wolves stepped forward. The alpha stopped it immediately. Its gaze remained fixed on Mateo.

Why are you not afraid?

The question wasn't spoken. Not in words. But Mateo heard it anyway. "…Because I would prefer not to be inconvenienced," he replied.

The alpha's ears twitched. Now it was suspicious. Mateo exhaled slowly and decided this was taking too long. He sat up halfway, stretched slightly—then suddenly flinched. Subtle. But intentional. His gaze snapped past the pack. "…Ah."

The wolves stiffened instantly. Mateo's posture changed—just enough to introduce tension. "…You might want to move," he said, voice lower now. The alpha turned, sensing what Mateo implied. Nothing. Mateo's breathing quickened—controlled, but appearing unstable. "…It's coming this way."

The pack erupted in motion. Not panicked. But immediate. The alpha didn't look back. They were gone within seconds. Mateo watched them leave, then leaned back against the roots again. "…Works every time."

He didn't stay long. The forest above shifted first. A shadow passed across the ground, not from a cloud, but from something vast moving overhead. Mateo looked up.
And saw it.

A hawk. Massive. Its wings stretched across the sky, easily sixty meters from tip to tip, its flight silent except for the subtle displacement of air that reached the ground seconds after it passed. Its feathers shimmered faintly, some edged in a soft metallic sheen that caught light at impossible angles. It did not hunt. It observed.

Mateo remained still.

	That one matters.

He adjusted immediately, lowering his presence, reducing movement, allowing his breathing to settle into something smaller, quieter, less noticeable. The hawk passed.
And Mateo stood slowly. "…Alright," he muttered. "Less casual."

From there, his movement changed. Still relaxed—but precise. He began collecting herbs, moving between clusters with subtle shifts in direction that avoided obvious paths. Some grew normally. Others did not.

He found one near a shaded rock face: a small vine with deep blue veins, each one faintly glowing as though carrying liquid light. The glow pulsed slowly, like breath. Mateo crouched. His fingers hovered just above it. The air hummed faintly. "…Starvein Pulse Vine… interesting."

Further in, he found a cluster of red leaves, each one surrounded by tiny drifting embers that never fully burned. They floated in slow circles, dissolving and reforming endlessly. "…Emberveil Bloom."

Another area held crystalline growths resembling frozen petals. Thin shards of ice circled the plant in slow rotation without touching the stem. "…Frostglass Heart Lotus."

Then another—soft green leaves surrounded by drifting golden motes. "…Lifebloom Verdant Core."

And deeper still—thin silver reeds crackling faintly with contained lightning. "…Thundertouch Reed." Mateo's fingers tingled faintly as arcs danced between the strands.

Each required care. Not strength. Understanding. Mateo smiled faintly. "This is more like it."

Then—he stopped. The herbs in his bundle shifted subtly. The Starvein Pulse Vine pulsed, interfering with the Emberveil Bloom. The Frostglass Heart Lotus responded, tightening its orbit. The Lifebloom Verdant Core fluctuated between stabilizing and amplifying conflicting energies. The Thundertouch Reed crackled slightly louder.

Even though Mateo had already tried to separate them using layers of leaves and natural dividers to reduce direct contact, the closeness alone was enough to agitate their internal properties. It wasn't hostility—not in any conscious sense. These herbs had not reached a level of awareness where intent could form. But they still possessed something similar to instinct—an intrinsic will that resisted foreign energy signatures that did not align with their nature. Some combinations settled more easily, harmonizing in small, temporary balance, while others reacted poorly, pushing against one another in subtle but growing disorder, as if each refused to yield its identity within shared space.

Mateo frowned. "…That won't work."

He crouched again, laying them out. Observing. Thinking.
"…No separation. No isolation. No stability."

A pause.

Then realization.
"…That's irritating."

He looked around. Carefully.

The forest moved. But nothing close.
Behind him—
The small plant.
It had returned.
And now it stared at him.

Mateo sighed lightly. "…You're about to see something you probably shouldn't."

He raised his hand.

And began drawing in the air.

At first—nothing. Then faint lines appeared, thin streaks of light trailing his fingertips. They remained suspended, intersecting, forming patterns. Runes. Not written. Made. His second hand joined. Faster now. Precise. The runes spun, overlapped, folded inward.
The air bent.
Mateo's expression tightened.
Pressure hit his body immediately. His fingers trembled slightly as his muscles engaged instinctively. Veins rose faintly along his arms. His breathing slowed forcefully as he held something that resisted being held.
"…Still… possible…"
He grasped forward.
Space warped.
Not visibly at first—then violently.
Like fabric stretched too far.
His muscles strained harder now. His face scrunched, jaw tightening. His shoulders locked slightly as resistance pressed inward. His meridians surged unevenly, energy rushing faster than advisable as he forced control.
"Almost…"
The air tore. A small opening formed—no larger than a palm.
Inside—void.
Or something beyond it.
Mateo exhaled sharply. "…That's enough."
He forced it wider. His entire body strained—back muscles tightening, breath forced deeper. Sweat formed along his brow. Then—
He twisted.
The opening collapsed inward.
A small cube hovered in front of him, edges lined with shifting runes.
Mateo grabbed it immediately.
The pressure vanished.
He exhaled slowly. "…Still a bad habit."
His body responded—meridians pulsing unevenly, muscles holding tension longer than needed. But it held. He placed additional runes, stabilizing the structure. Opened it. Stored each herb. Closed it again.
Silence returned.
No interference.
"Better."
He tucked it into his clothing.
Behind him, the small plant stared in complete shock—then ran.
Mateo watched it leave and let out a quiet giggle. "…Well… that was not expected."
He stood again and resumed moving. The forest continued as it always had—but now, it felt slightly more aware of him.

It was near a small body of water when the calm ended. The pond sat in a shallow dip, surrounded by twisted roots and smooth stone. Its surface reflected broken light. Mateo crouched near the edge, digging carefully into the soil. "…It should be here."
He brushed away dirt until golden-threaded roots revealed themselves, pulsing like a buried heartbeat. "…Found you."
The growl came immediately. Mateo didn't turn. "…You again."
The alpha stood at the clearing's edge. This time, there was no confusion. Only intent. Mateo exhaled slowly. "…You chose incorrectly." He stood, his body settling into alignment—feet grounded, weight lowered, core stable. The forest fell quiet.
"…Alright."
And this time—he did not intend to play.
