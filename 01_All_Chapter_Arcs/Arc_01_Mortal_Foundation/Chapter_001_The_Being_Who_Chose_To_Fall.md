# Chapter 1 - The Being Who Chose To Fall

Chapter 1 — The Being Who Chose to Fall  
There was nothing to lie on, and it held him perfectly.  
Mateo drifted on his back, one arm folded beneath his head, the other resting across his stomach, ankles crossed. There was no grass beneath him, no sky above him, no warmth  
and no light except what the dark itself carried. There was only him, suspended in an expanse of emptiness. The dark bent gently around his shoulders, shaped by his form as if  
laying on a bed of space.  
He looked young. That was the strangest part, and he knew it. The form had settled somewhere in his early twenties and simply stayed there — tall, lean, long through the arms and  
legs, the muscle along his shoulders and forearms clean and unemphatic.  
His skin was a deep, warm brown, and whatever illumination existed out here brushed against him and stayed. His hair fell in loose black layers threaded through with streaks of dark  
red that caught faintly when he moved.  
Beneath heavy brows, his eyes were black. Not dark brown, not near-black. Completely black, pupil and iris indistinguishable, and they did not reflect the stars. He had watched too  
many of them burn out to be impressed by the ones still lit.  
He wore layered robes of charcoal and deep umber, the hems frayed, faint red patterning stitched through the fabric in narrow repeating lines. A dark scarf wound loose around his  
throat and over one shoulder, trailing behind him in a slow, weightless drift. There was no wind here. The fabric moved becau se he willed it to, not because of wind or any other  
force. He had wanted to feel as though he was not a being in space but a normal being with wind around him.  
He carried nothing else. No weapons, seals, relics, or anything of that sort. He also did not require any of these items.  
He did not remember being made. There was no beginning he could recall, no first breath, no birth, no creator standing over h im with intent. He had simply been there, complete,  
aware, and too large for anything to hold. Whether the void had sealed him or he had sealed himself, he no longer knew, and he suspected the answer had stopped mattering a very  
long time ago.  
Beyond the edges of a place that had no real end resided the beings who called themselves the peaks of existence. These were the sovereigns. Origin-treaders. Cultivators who had  
climbed so far above the worlds that entire realms bent when they exhaled, and who spoke of themselves as the ceiling of all things because within their own realms they held  
ultimate power. Some had believed they had reached the ceiling of power, unaware that realms existed above them while others knew but had no way of achieving those higher  
realms. Even then they had reached a point that all but a fraction of a percent of cultivators would never reach. The ability to traverse across worlds. Move millions of meters with a  
single step. A realm attained only once every few million years by those who carved a path to the top. Some supreme geniuses could climb faster but even that only occurs once or  
twice per epoch. These were beings who had lived for eons themselves but even they were like children to Mateo. He had follow ed some of them be from the time of birth to their  
rise to power. Some starting off as snotty little children with nothing who surpassed all and others born to supreme clans th at now worshipped them as gods as they elevated their  
clans to the peak. Many of these beings had come on gone. Some died venturing into the void to reach new realms. Some had hid den and cultivated for so long that generations  
had gone by without their clans seeing them. Some whose clans had no idea they existed at all. But Mateo saw all of them. Wat ched them grow. Knew which ones lived and which  
ones had passed on. Some who reincarnated to start anew and many who failed. Some who reached the higher realms. Some who rea ched realms outside of this one where  
cultivation itself worked differently. Others who just roamed the worlds disguised as normal humans, drunks, playboys and so on for pleasure or out of boredom.  
They understood the void. They had mapped it, argued over it, and built entire clans inside the void or around it. To them it was not a mystery but a medium, something that could  
be entered, read, and folded, so that two points separated by a hundred million worlds could be brought edge to edge and crossed in a single unhurried step. They traveled through  
it constantly. Some had fought in it. A few had made their homes inside it. Some created them to protect their clans and their treasures. Others explored them and found an  
unknown amount of treasures inside these spaces.  
Mateo was not in the void. This space he resided in seemed to be completely disconnected from all of reality. A separate zone where he was omniscient and omnipotent. He could  
see all and know all. He could also pierce this veil like a one sided mirror but others could not interact with his world nor see it.  
Their perception did not strain against his world but simply pass by as if nothing was there. It never reached a border at all. It passed straight through the region and returned  
nothing: no resistance, no obstruction, not even the particular silence that marks a place deliberately hidden. There was no position for the void to fold toward, no distance to  
shorten, nothing for their senses to catch on and call strange. He had become bound between realities, as if he had been remo ved from existence entirely.  
Not one of them had ever seen him. Not one of them could. They stood at the summit of everything they had encountered, and they were correct about it. The summit had simply  
never been the top.  
"Let's try this again."  
The words carried no impatience and no anger, only quiet exhaustion. He had uttered this word so many times he had lost count , but he wanted to try, and he would not give up on  
his test.  
Mateo drifted in place, in this endless expanse. This world where there was no sky, no ground, and no direction. Distance fol ded at his convenience. The silence was absolute.  
Everything rested in perfect stillness, sealed inside an endless expanse shaped entirely by his intent.  
He raised one hand, and reality unfolded.  
Stars ignited across the dark in flawless formation, each one set exactly where it was needed with a precision that proved just how many endless eons he had done this. Galaxies  
spiraled outward in elegant arcs and space curved to support them. Time layered itself across the structure in smooth, uninte rrupted motion. In the spaces between, worlds began to  
gather, mountains pushing upward, oceans spreading into basins, wind finding its first path across open water.  
Then came life. Bright, fragile, stubborn life, arriving without any understanding of what it had walked into.  
Mateo watched it take hold. Then he lowered his hand and left it alone.  
That was the part that would have surprised anyone watching, if anyone could have. He did not unmake what he built. A world t hat found its footing was allowed to keep it. Galaxies  
were left to spiral out to their full width, burn through their long and unhurried middle age, and end the way the laws he ha d written intended them to end, without interference and  
without an audience that mattered. Life was left to argue with itself, invent farming and grief and mathematics, build empire s out of nothing and lose them to weather and pride, and  
finish its own story in its own time. He had learned early that the interesting part was never the founding. It was whatever the thing became once he stopped touching it.  
He closed his hand on a creation only when something had gone wrong at the root.  
Sometimes a world simply existed. The oceans moved, the seasons turned, the mountains wore down into hills over ages, and nothing ever crawled out of the water to look at any of  
it. A world like that was not a failure of will, only of chemistry, and he undid it without ceremony and built the next one d ifferently.  
Sometimes the failure was worse. Life arrived, and it arrived wrong. Not cruel in the ordinary way that living things were cr uel to each other when frightened or hungry, because that  
had never bothered him and had produced some of the most interesting histories he had ever watched. Wrong at the foundation. Beings that were born already knowing that  
everything outside themselves was material, that grew in power the way rot grows through a beam, that would inevitably climb high enough to reach across the dark and consume  
the worlds beside them and leave nothing standing anywhere they arrived. He watched those long enough to be certain, because certainty mattered, and then he curled his fingers  
inward and let the whole structure come undone. Light vanished. Time dissolved. The structure of reality unraveled so thorou ghly that the concept of it seemed to fade along with  
everything else. Why not just remove those beings some would ask… It's because after many test he found that these worlds would produce similar beings of those so called  
devourers or evil beings where removed so it was better to start over.  
There was never any anger in it. He simply removed a beam that had been set wrong before the house came down on everyone who would have lived inside it.  
The rest he let flourish.  
And on occasion, he went down and lived in them.  
That was the one freedom his own creations allowed him. They were his, written in his own laws, built to a scale that could t olerate him standing inside them without tearing. He  
could step down into a world he had made, take a body, take a name, and walk from one end of a mortal life to the other. He had done it more times than he had bothered to count.  
He had been a cultivator among cultivators, sweeping the steps of a mountain sect at dawn. He had been a soldier in a war who se cause he had deliberately not looked up  
beforehand, so that he would have to decide about it the way the men beside him decided about it. He had been homeless in a w et city, sleeping under a bridge with his back to a  

wall. He had been a sage, a monk who had shaved his head and kept silence for forty years, a scholar hunched over borrowed bo oks in a lamplit archive. He had been the wastrel  
second son of a rich family, disgraceful and dull and untroubled by it. He had been a man who started with a cart and one bad idea and turned a poor family into tycoons over the  
course of a single unremarkable lifetime.  
None of it ever worked.  
Not because the lives went badly. Because they went too well.  
Everyone he met feared him. Not visibly, and not in a way any of them could have explained if he had asked, which he eventually stopped doing. It arrived before conversation did. A  
merchant would meet his eyes across a stall and forget the price he had been about to name. A sect elder would look at him once and feel, without evidence, that some line existed  
here that should not be crossed. Children went quiet near him. Dogs would not bark. He had suppressed himself down to the threshold of a mortal's strength, then below it, then  
further, until the body he wore was genuinely weak and would genuinely bleed, but this changed nothing. His overwhelming pres ence seemed to linger. It was not a technique, nor  
was it pressure placed on the beings around him, but something that simply was. It was present the way weight is present, and the world felt it even when nothing living did. As a  
beggar he would get yelled at and pushed around just like the others did, but when they got close they seemed to sense something. Like fear rising from the bottom of their souls,  
and they would leave him alone. As a soldier he would notice the enemy's slight hesitation in the fight, the one that would lead to their deaths. Not because he was a better fighter,  
even though he was, and held back, but because something pushed against their souls. Like they knew they fought a beast they could not defeat. They faced a being beyond their  
comprehension wearing the mask of a human, or of whatever species he had taken on.  
So the world bent, and it bent quietly.  
Whatever he set out to achieve was already sitting there waiting for him by the time he arrived. Doors that should have taken a decade of patient work opened in a fraction of the  
time. Techniques he had not trained landed correctly the first time, because the world could not bring itself to let him fail at something. Fortunes appeared. Enemies suffered  
accidents he had not asked for and would not have permitted if he had been consulted. Once, deliberately, he had joined a cultivation family that had lost everything, and he had  
entered it as the child with no talent at all, no spiritual root, no foundation worth measuring, the boy the clan kept out of obligation and nothing else. Within three years the  
strongest cultivator in the region had come down the mountain personally, looked at him for a long moment without being able to say what he was looking at, and named him his  
direct disciple.  
The man never could explain why. Neither could anyone else. The world simply arranged it. It bent to him even when he did not bend it, because somewhere beneath everything, it  
knew he was its creator.  
And beneath all of it, in every world, in every life, they worshipped him. Not always by name and not always knowingly. He ha d built those realms with as much freedom as he knew  
how to write into a thing, no commandments, no descending voice, no scripture, deliberately no god at the top of the sky for anyone to find. It did not matter. Something in them  
knew, at a level far deeper than their souls, that the being walking among them had made them. They built temples he had neve r asked for and pointed them, without  
understanding why, in his direction. They knelt in his presence and stood up embarrassed, unable to account for it. He could do anything at all, cruel or kind or nothing whatsoever,  
and it never dented the reverence, because the reverence had never been earned in the first place. It had been there before h e arrived, always present and eternal.  
He had gone down as a beggar and been treated like a hidden immortal. He had gone down as a tyrant and been forgiven. He had gone down as no one, and the world had insisted  
on making him someone.  
Mateo watched the new galaxies turn, immense and patient and entirely alive, and let out a slow breath.  
"Still incomplete. Not what I'm looking for." He turned the thought over without hurry. "Something's missing from these tests ."  
The worlds were fine. The worlds had never been the problem.  
Silence returned and settled in beside him. He did not mind it.  
Mateo leaned back into the dark, one arm behind his head. "I've run out of things to do."  
Earlier in eternity, the statement would have sounded tragic. Now it was only accurate.  
Creation had fascinated him once. He had built realities in full while others managed only fragments, testing different syste ms of creation, rewriting laws, adjusting the way energy  
moved through existence until it arrived at new phenomenon's. Worlds assembled, failed, adapted, collapsed, and tried again beneath his attention. Civilizations climbed out of  
nothing, invented language, gathered power, hardened that power into doctrine, and disappeared without leaving behind anythin g that surprised him.  
In the beginning, the variation had been enough to hold him. Eventually it wasn't. Patterns eventually started appearing. Cha os, observed long enough, turned out to be following  
rules based on how the realms formed. Even then he would purposely bend those rules, or give chaos a shove in a different direction, simply to see where it went.  
The only thing that still excited him was one of his best creations. He had built a realm of endless lightning storms, or tribulations, as many cultivators called them. He had  
experimented, mirroring what he had seen in the real worlds and then pushing it far past anything they would ever survive. So me of those lightning tribulations covered spaces so  
large that entire realms could have been dropped inside them with room to spare. He created creatures within it at times, and at other times allowed the realm itself to give birth to  
sentience. All manner of things came to be. Lightning phoenixes, dragons, sparrows, beasts of raw current, spirits of pure lightning, celestial beings, and every mythical creature the  
realm could dream into existence. Within it he watched whole worlds thrive, every living thing born with lightning affinity w oven into it from the first breath.  
Most of them never knew that the lightning surrounding them could have annihilated them with ease were it not for him. He cre ated borders. Zones where life could take root and  
grow, and beyond those, vast territories where the storms were allowed to roam free across the width of the realm. As for the creatures born of pure tribulation lightning, he never  
allowed them near the inhabited worlds. He could not. A mere brush from one of them would have shredded a world to pieces. All of these beings could traverse worlds and  
cultivate, but they had limits, and he had imposed those limits himself. The realm he had made was so powerful that no matter how strong they grew, there were regions they would  
never survive. That did not mean there was no hope for them. The realm was so vast that reaching its end was near impossible, and in many areas space was warped so that a  
creature arriving at what it believed was a corner would simply find itself somewhere else entirely. He did allow freedom, an d some creatures met their demise venturing into places  
they should not have gone. Others died in each other's jaws over territory, or mates, or nothing at all. He did not intervene in any of it. Watching over every life in a realm that size  
would have consumed him, and he had not built a nursery. He had built a world, and a world was supposed to be dangerous. Let them live and die by their own choices. The realm  
itself was another matter entirely.  
His awareness drifted downward, past the boundary of his own making. Beyond his sealed realm, beneath more layers of existenc e than were worth counting, were other worlds.  
Real ones. Worlds he had not shaped, that owed him nothing, and that were under no obligation to meet his expectations.  
The immortal realms. The eternal realms and many more with his favorite being the Mortal Realms.  
"Let's see what's actually happening down there."  
He did not narrow his attention to a single life. He watched hundreds of thousands at once and let them run side by side.  
A boy struggled beneath the weight of a blade far too large for him, arms shaking, stance broken, refusing to let go of the h ilt. A girl practiced under heavy rain, repeating one strike  
until every wasted motion had been worn out of her body and only the necessary part remained. A man knelt in mud and blood, b arely conscious, and would not let himself fall  
forward.  
A young man punched forward and heat rose around him as he manipulated flames.  
A beast roaming the woods with wind surrounding its feet and reached incredible speeds with each stride.  
A child born to be an ultimate swordmen with swords or energy hovering around him as he meditated.  
Mateo watched time move in a long sweeping motion, days folding into years, years compressing into decades.  
His sight moved to another scene where he noticed someone about to ascend.  
This part was always interesting to him. No divine hand descended from a sky. What happened was more fundamental as if the world itself controlled it. The world simply stopped  
being enough for them, and they outgrew it. Runes would appear in the sky and an unstoppable aura would be felt. Then a tribulation would appear and if they survived they would  
ascend. They always knew when they would ascend.  
After the briefest interval they appeared again in a higher world. A denser one. A harsher one. And almost immediately, they were no longer exceptional. Not all of them. Some had  
clans and families waiting while others had know one.  
The boy who had dominated everyone around him faltered. The girl whose form was perfect discovered that perfection had a ceiling, and then found the underside of a new one.  
The man who had endured everything nearly buckled beneath a pressure that asked for more than survival.  
"They always believe themselves to be peaking geniuses but across these realms how many have I seen.  
Mateo turned his attention to another. A genius, this one, whose talent eclipsed every peer in the realm. Every technique landed on the first attempt. Every fight ended before it  
properly began. The entire ascent was accomplished without ever meeting resistance.  

Then he ascended, and stagnated.  
In a world where a millions upon millions carried brilliance equal to his own, paired with deeper refinement, older foundatio ns, and brutal paths to where they are now that had cost  
them everything, his advantage evaporated within a few short years. He had never needed to struggle, so he had never built the things that struggle builds. He stalled, then faded,  
and the realm that had once spoken his name forgot it within a generation.  
Then Mateo found something else, and this one held him.  
Someone overlooked. A cultivator written off as mediocre by everyone who had assessed him. His progress came unevenly. His te chniques carried flaws he could never quite file  
down. Growth arrived in fits and stalls, and every sect that took him in considered him a poor investment.  
He failed repeatedly, in public, and was humiliated for it. He continued anyway. And he ascended.  
Nothing changed in the next realm. He was still ignored, still dismissed, still the one people spoke past instead of to. He did not stop.  
Centuries passed. Then millennia. The same figures who had dismissed him could do nothing but look upward in silence. The talented had relied on gifts that abandoned them the  
moment they were genuinely challenged, and he had relied on persistence that never stopped compounding.  
He had not risen quickly. He had risen steadily.  
Mateo smiled faintly. "This was one was Interesting."  
Talent mattered. So did effort, timing, pressure, environment, and inheritance. None of them alone determined the ending, and the ones who assumed otherwise were usually the  
ones who ended early. There was no single path to ascension, and that variation was what kept his attention.  
He kept watching.  
Another figure caught him, someone out of the lower realms whose path crossed with a cultivator born of a far higher lineage. They fought the first time. They fought again. They  
fought a third time, closer to habit than hostility by then. Over the following years the competition wore itself down into s omething else entirely, understanding first, and then an  
alliance that neither of them would have predicted at the start.  
Not all strength produced arrogance. Not every high-born cultivator looked downward at what stood beneath them, and not every powerful structure rejected what came up from  
below.  
That mattered too. It was part of what made the worlds feel alive rather than scripted, with nothing about them settled befor e it happened.  
And there was one truth Mateo understood better than anyone else. Not all lower realms were equal.  
He knew, because he had built some of them himself.  
Inside his sealed realms, life flourished exactly as designed. Civilizations grew beneath artificial skies they had no reason to doubt. Cultivators rose, founded sects, established clans,  
wrote doctrines, and concluded, reasonably enough, that the limits of their world were the limits of existence.  
Mateo knew otherwise. They were contained. Invisible ceilings existed. Thresholds could not be crossed by talent, effort, or suffering, because the system declined to permit anything  
past a certain point, and it had never been built to explain itself.  
Inside, they were powerful. Outside, they were small. Not worthless and not weak, but incomplete, through no failure of their own.  
That was the part he had always disliked. They had not fallen short. They had been born into something that was never capable of carrying them any further.  
So Mateo made a decision.  
He did not destroy those worlds. He changed them. He moved them.  
Every life, every civilization, every being that existed within his sealed expanse was woven into the real worlds below, adjusted, balanced, and rewritten into an existence they  
belonged to so seamlessly that the worlds receiving them remembered them as having always been there.  
Not stronger. Not weaker. Equal.  
The overwhelming advantages they carried could not come with them. Divine inheritances, unnatural foundations, impossibilitie s that had only ever functioned because he had  
written them into existence personally, dropped whole into true worlds, would have torn the balance apart within a generation.  
So Mateo stripped the excess away. Not their lives. Not their potential. Only the imbalance. Let them rise properly, without an artificial ceiling waiting to tell them where their story  
ended.  
The thought stayed with him longer than most did.  
For the first time in a very long while, he was not bored. He was dissatisfied, and dissatisfaction had a shape to work with.  
The new problem was distance.  
He could watch everything, every world across every layer of existence, in as much detail as he cared to resolve. What he could not do was touch any of it. His own creations had  
never been able to refuse him, and that was precisely what had ruined them. These worlds could refuse him. These worlds had never been written by his hand, owed him no  
reverence, and would not arrange themselves around him.  
They would also not survive him.  
He had tried once. His presence brushed against another realm and space fractured along its seams. Fear hit the beings of this realm as they felt and saw what seemed to be the end  
of their realm due to some unfathomable power. Time bent due to his overwhelming power. Dimensions strained under a weight th ey had never felt before. He pulled back before it  
went any further. The beings of this world would never know how close they were to death and to feeling the presence of something so far above them.  
Its was simply too much. Too complete. Too vast. Too heavy for anything real to hold.  
This place was not a prison. It was the only structure in existence capable of containing him without breaking, and he had ma de peace with that distinction a long time ago.  
"I built myself into a corner." He laughed softly, and the sound went nowhere at all.  
"Unfortunate."  
Then a thought surfaced, simple and direct and so obvious that he wondered why it had taken this long to arrive.  
If he couldn't enter those worlds—  
He sat up, the scarf drifting slowly around him.  
—then he wouldn't.  
Not through suppression of his power, because no matter how much he surpressed his power some would still leak. He had spent ten thousand lifetimes proving that and attenpting  
to touch the real worlds. But now he had an idea...removal.  
He would dismantle himself.  
Not reduce his presence nor conceal his power. Remove the parts of him that made him too much for existence to carry, and see what was left standing afterward.  
First he would unravel time. His sole existence was time itself. It was space. It was everything. Time came apart first, unwinding in long quiet strands. Some cultivators would destroy  
worlds and leave paths of destruction just to hold a piece of one of these divine strands of time. Space fractured next. Power broke into fragments and scattered outward into the  
dark. His existence, which had never known an edge, began contracting toward one.  
From limitless, to defined.  
Not all of it scattered. Something small held together while the rest came apart, a fragment that refused to unravel, denser than everything surrounding it like a black hole, falling  
ahead of him instead of dispersing with the rest. He noticed it, decided it did not matter, and let it go.  
The pressure was overwhelming on himself. He could create anything, destroy anything but had never focused his own power on h imself. Beads of sweat drenched his body as his  
sole existence strained against itself. Cracks appeared across his body. Fragments of power that could annihilate everything. He continue to push inward so that his power would be  
pushed out.  
"This is not enough....more...more" as he gritted his teeth the plains of existence unraveled and as it did he remembered...  
"Those worlds...in the lightning realm" He realized he had probably doomed them all.  
"No I will not accept that" and he reached out and pressed on space itself and as he did he finally exhausted his last bit of power. That realm warped and bent. It was being  
compressed and warped but not destroyed. Then that massive realm vanished. He knew it had survived. He had displaced it and pushed it to the real world.  
Not outside of space and time but withing the vast space of the outside.  
He fell through the layers of existence toward the worlds he had watched, and one final question surfaced.  
The sealed realm behind him. Should he completely erase it?  
No he would let it be.  

Left behind. Opened. Released. Maybe it would become what he always wanted it to be.  
Then darkness....  
Weight was the first feeling he felt, and it arrived rudely.  
For the first time in the whole of his existence, Mateo had a real body made of flesh.  
He had the body of what looked human previously but it was created from energy. An energy so vast that even he could not fath om his own limits. He had never needed anything.  
His previous body never grew warm or cold, never grew tired, never felt heavy. He was neither too large or too small. He just was...  
This was flesh, and bone, and blood, and it began telling him things immediately.  
Heat came first, close and damp, wrapped around him on every side and held there by cloth. Beneath it his own skin, thin enough that he could feel the individual threads of the  
blanket pressing into his arm. Beneath that, something more alarming: softness. He was soft everywhere. His bones were small and pliant and gave slightly under their own weight,  
and the top of his skull had not finished closing, and some new instinct that had come installed with the body flinched away from that fact before he could examine it properly.  
He could feel the beat of this heart. It had been going for some time already, and the rhythm of it was peaceful. His lungs p ulled and released, moving air he had never needed, and  
each breath dragged something cool down a throat that stung faintly with the newness of being used.  
Then a pressure gathered low in him, dull and insistent and impossible to reason with. He identified it after a moment.  
Hunger.  
He had shaped worlds where hunger was a fundamental law, had written it into whole species and watched what they built to escape it, and he had never once experienced it. He  
had also built worlds where hunger did not exist at all, simply to see what such worlds would become. Worlds with no light. Worlds with no darkness. Worlds that were only ocean,  
and worlds that were only land. He had built every world he could conceive of, and he had walked through more of them than he could name, and not one of them had ever asked  
him for anything.  
This was a demand, made by his own body, to his own attention, with no interest whatsoever in his opinion on the matter.  
Heaviness settled after that, weighting his limbs and pulling steadily at his eyelids. Tiredness. Also new. A sensation he ha d not encountered across millions upon millions of years.  
He had slept before, but never out of necessity. He had chosen to sleep, closed his eyes, and opened them again without knowi ng whether a single second or a thousand years or an  
entire epoch had passed in the real world, because none of it had ever held any meaning for him.  
He turned his awareness outward from habit, and it stopped short.  
There was the room. Wooden ceiling above him. Warm air, smoke, the smell of damp wool and something cooking beyond these wall s. Two heartbeats near him, one fast and one  
slow. A door somewhere, and past that door, a world he could no longer see.  
He reached again, carefully, and the reach ended a handspan past his own skin and simply refused to continue. There was no wa ll stopping it. There was no ceiling on his perception.  
There was only the plain fact that he now perceived by means of a body, and this body's means extended precisely as far as a body's means extend.  
Existence was still out there. Every layer of it, every world he had watched turning simultaneously and in full detail, all o f it continuing without him. He could no longer see any of it.  
He was aware of a room, and the two people in it, and the weight of his own arm.  
Something in him should have objected to that, but nothing did.  
Instead there was a small warm feeling with no clear origin, sitting somewhere beneath the hunger and the tiredness, something he had never really felt before. Contentment,  
possibly. He turned it over and found he had no prior instance to compare it against, no framework that could fit this situation.  
Boundaries in every direction, closing around him with. The feeling of being restricted which is something he never felt.  
Two people were in the room with him, and neither of them was afraid.  
He would have to learn all of this. Every part of it. Starting now, from the beginning, with nothing.  
He would explore these worlds, and he would enjoy his life.  
Mateo opened his eyes slowly.  
A wooden ceiling. Uneven beams crossing above him, dark with smoke and age, one of them slightly warped where the wood had dried unevenly. The room was small.  
The wooden house sat on a gentle rise closer to the middle of the village. It was neither small nor grand, but comfortably sized, with two stories and a broad front porch that  
wrapped around one side of the building. The exterior was built from warm cedar planks that had weathered to a rich honey -brown color over the years. Large windows reflected the  
surrounding trees and filled the interior with natural light.  
He lifted one hand into his field of vision.  
Tiny. Five small fingers, uncooperative, trembling faintly with the effort of being held up.  
He stared at it for a long moment.  
"...I did it."  
Voices moved nearby, warm and low, pressing against ears that could not yet organize sound into meaning.  
Lucía held him carefully against her shoulder, adjusting his blanket with the attentiveness of someone who had not slept properly in days. Rafael stood close, watching more than he  
spoke, arms crossed, brow drawn.  
"He's too quiet," Lucía murmured.  
"He keeps looking at things like he understands," Rafael said.  
Mateo closed his eyes briefly.  
Because I do.  
"It worked," he said quietly, and what came out was a soft, formless sound that meant nothing at all to the room.  
Lucía blinked and glanced down at him. "What is it my child?"  
"Nothing" he thought.  
He exhaled slowly, and the breath went out of him with far more effort than it had ever taken to unmake a universe.  
Good.  
Something cool rested against his chest. A thin cord, looped and knotted twice so it would not slip, and hanging from it a small silver pendant shaped into a four-pointed star, its  
edges already worn smooth.  
Lucía had bought it weeks before he was born, from a traveling vendor whose face she could no longer recall, and she would tell that story for years.  
Mateo felt the shape of it against his skin.  
He went very still.  
Far beyond the small room, the worlds continued to turn, unaware that something had been watching them from above and had now come into reach. And far beneath everything, a  
sealed realm had begun to break open, not in destruction, but in release.  
For a brief moment, Mateo paused.  
Nothing had made a sound. He had not seen anything. The air simply felt off. Not heavier and not lighter, only slightly out of place, as though something had shifted and the world  
had not finished correcting for it.  
His gaze drifted faintly toward nothing.  
Then it was gone.
