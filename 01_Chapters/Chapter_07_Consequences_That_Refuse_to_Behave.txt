The elders did not return to their meeting.
At least, not immediately.
What had been a routine discussion about minor disputes and training matters had, in a matter of minutes, unraveled into something far less structured. The room—once controlled and orderly, the kind of quiet space where even small sounds carried meaning—now held an atmosphere that was distinctly… unsettled.
"He walked in."
One of the elders spoke first, his tone low and deliberate, as though repeating the words might somehow make the situation more reasonable.
"Yes," another replied.
"And none of you stopped him."
No one answered right away.
Because that was the problem.

"…He didn't act like someone who needed to be stopped," a third elder said at last, his brows furrowed. "He spoke as if he was already permitted to be there."
"That doesn't make sense," someone else muttered. "He's a child."
The first elder leaned back slightly, exhaling.
"…Then why did it feel like we were the ones being questioned?"
Silence followed.

At the far end of the room, where light from the window barely reached, the older man who had remained quiet until now finally spoke.
"…Because we were."
They turned toward him.
He did not elaborate.

"…That boy," another elder said, "is Rafael's son."
"That explains nothing."
"It explains enough," the older man replied calmly. "If what we heard about his training is true, then it's no surprise he doesn't behave like the others."
"…Behaving differently is one thing," another elder said. "Walking into restricted meetings and speaking as if he belongs there is something else entirely."
A brief pause settled over the room, heavier this time.

"…Should we discipline him?" someone asked.

The room quieted again.

The question lingered longer than expected.

"…For what?" the older man said.
Another elder frowned. "For entering without permission, obviously."
"…And what would you say was his intent?"
That stopped them.

"…He said he was testing something," one of them replied after a moment.
"…And?"
"…And he proved," another added reluctantly, "…that we didn't notice him entering, and that our discussion could be overheard."
The older man nodded once.
"…Then he revealed a flaw."
"…By breaking rules."
"…By exposing weaknesses," the man corrected.

Silence lingered once more.

"…That does not mean we allow it to continue," one elder said firmly.
"No," the older one agreed.
A short pause followed before he added, "…but it also does not mean we misunderstand what just happened."

Far from the meeting hall, where the village paths curved back into familiar patterns of movement and sound, Mateo stretched lightly beneath the shade of a tree as though nothing of consequence had occurred earlier that day. The dirt beneath him was warm now, marked by footsteps and scattered impressions from prior training.

"You're going to get in trouble."
Diego said this with the certainty of someone who believed it completely—and the excitement of someone who very much wanted to be present when it happened.
Mateo didn't open his eyes.
"…Possibly."
"That's your response?"
"…It's accurate."

Tomas stood nearby, arms crossed, his expression still carrying the faint traces of something unsettled—something he had not fully put into words yet.
"…You don't seem concerned."
Mateo opened one eye slightly.
"…Should I be?"
Tomas stared at him for a moment.
"…Yes."

A brief pause.
Then Mateo closed his eye again.
"…I'll consider it."

Diego groaned.
"He's not going to consider it."
"That is correct," Mateo said.

Tomas exhaled slowly, as if searching for a way to approach something that resisted normal explanation.
"…What you did," Tomas began, "should have gone differently."
Mateo tilted his head slightly but didn't otherwise move.
"…In what way."
"You entered a restricted area, spoke to the elders as if you had authority, then left."
"…Yes."
"…That's not normal."
Mateo considered this.
"…Correct."

Tomas narrowed his eyes.
"…Then why do it?"

For a brief moment, Mateo didn't answer.
Not immediately.

Then he sat up slowly and turned toward Tomas, his posture shifting just enough to return to that calm, measured presence.
"…If a system functions properly," he said, "then it should be able to withstand unexpected input."
Diego blinked.
"…What?"
Mateo continued without looking at him.
"…If it cannot, then the system is flawed. If no one tests it…" He paused briefly, letting the thought settle. "…then the flaw remains."

Tomas watched him closely now, something in his expression tightening—not disagreement, but something close to reluctant understanding.
"…And you decided to test it."
"…Yes."
"…By walking into an elder meeting."
"…Yes."

Another pause stretched between them.

"…You could have been punished."
"That outcome was considered."
"…And you still did it."
Mateo nodded once.
"…Yes."

Tomas exhaled slowly, trying to align that explanation with the action. For a moment, it almost worked.

Then Mateo added, without changing his tone:
"…Also, it was funnier this way."

Silence.

Diego immediately lost whatever restraint he had been holding.
"…I KNEW IT."

Tomas stared at him.
"…You just gave a full explanation about systems and structure—"
"…That part was correct."
"—and then said you did it because it was funny."
Mateo looked at him calmly.
"…Both are true."

Tomas blinked.
Slowly.

	…This doesn't make sense.
	Why does it make sense.

"…You're a problem," Tomas said again.
Mateo nodded faintly.
"…Yes."

By the next morning, the story had changed.
Slightly.
Then completely.

"He walked in and challenged them."
"No, he tested them."
"I heard he was invited."
"He wasn't invited."
"I heard he corrected them."
"He embarrassed them."

Along the dirt paths and small gathering areas, the story moved faster than accuracy, reshaped by each retelling.
Mateo listened from a distance.

	Narratives are unstable.

"…They're saying you're some kind of hidden genius now," Diego said, far too pleased with that development.
Mateo glanced at him.
"…They were saying that yesterday."
"…Yeah, but now it's louder."

	That's worse.

Tomas stood beside him, watching the reactions around them even if he wasn't directly engaging.
"…They're going to keep pushing," he said.
Mateo didn't respond right away.

	Yes.

"…Then we adjust," Mateo said simply.
"…Adjust how."
Mateo looked ahead.
"…By becoming less predictable."

Diego frowned.
"…I thought you said everyone is predictable."
Mateo paused briefly, then replied,
"…That doesn't include me."

Later that afternoon, Mateo noticed something else.
Someone different.

Not from the group before.
Not from the village.

Someone watching.
Not openly.
But carefully.

	…Different.

The presence wasn't aggressive.
But it wasn't casual either.
It lingered longer than necessary, its attention fixed rather than drifting.

	Word is spreading beyond expectation.

Mateo exhaled slowly.

	That complicates things.

Then, after only a brief pause—

	…Interesting.

That evening, Mateo walked alone again, the forest pressing quietly around the edges of the village as the last traces of daylight faded. The familiar movement and noise softened into something slower, less structured.
The pendant rested lightly in his hand.

It pulsed.
Faint.
But more frequently now.

	Something is responding… or forming.

He turned it slowly, watching the fractured pattern shift under low light.

	Fragments aligning…

For a moment, that same image surfaced again.
Fire.
Metal.
Hands shaping something unrefined into something precise.

Gone.

Mateo tightened his grip slightly.
"…Not yet."

The thought formed quietly.

	But soon.

Somewhere behind him, Diego was already shouting about something completely unrelated.
Tomas sighed.
And the village, for all its quiet simplicity—

No longer felt entirely small.
