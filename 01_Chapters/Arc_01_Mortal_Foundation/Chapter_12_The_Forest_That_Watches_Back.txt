The forest did not greet Mateo when he entered. It simply… continued. That, in itself, was enough to make most cautious.
For Mateo, it was relaxing.
He moved without urgency, his steps light but deliberate, his body naturally adjusting to every irregularity in the terrain without conscious effort. Where roots twisted beneath the ground, his ankle corrected before imbalance could form. Where the soil softened unexpectedly, his toes flexed, redistributing weight through the ball of his foot rather than sinking into it. There was no wasted motion. And more importantly—no need to rush.
But even here—
There were traces of disturbance.
Not obvious. Not recent.
But present.
Broken bark at unnatural angles. Shallow depressions in the ground where weight had settled briefly, then lifted without hesitation. A branch cut cleanly rather than worn or snapped.
Mateo did not pause.
 Different movement pattern. Not local wildlife.
He continued.

By midday, Mateo had already stopped taking the forest seriously. He found a decent patch of sunlight breaking through the dense canopy and lay back against a slightly elevated mound of roots, one arm resting behind his head. Above him, the sky fractured into pieces through layered branches. The wind shifted lazily. Something small scurried nearby.
Mateo closed his eyes.
“…This is better.”

He didn’t sleep. Not really. His breathing slowed, his pulse evened, but his awareness stretched outward instead of inward. Its eyes locked onto Mateo.
