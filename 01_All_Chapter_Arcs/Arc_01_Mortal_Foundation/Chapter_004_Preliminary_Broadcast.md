# Chapter 4 - Preliminary Broadcast

Chapter 4 — Training, Trouble, and Something That Should Not Laugh  

The sun had not fully risen when Tomas arrived.  
The forest still held most of its shadows, and long strips of dim light slipped unevenly through the canopy. Some beams reached the ground cleanly and landed in warm  
pools that would shift as the angle changed. Others fragmented on the way down, leaving certain patches dimmer than they should have been.  
The training yard, if it could be called that, was nothing more than a widened patch of dirt carved out by use rather than design. No one had planned it or leveled it or  
posted a marker declaring that training happened here. It had emerged the way paths emerge, through repetition pressing the same ground in the same patterns until  
the earth conceded.  
The area near the eastern roots where Mateo usually practiced had been pressed so often that the dirt was nearly as solid as stone, while the patches closer to the edges  
still gave slightly underfoot. The differences were subtle enough that most people would ignore them completely.  
Mateo did not ignore them.  
He had mapped every variation in this ground over the past year. He knew where the soil compressed cleanly and where it shifted, where hidden stones created  
resistance, where roots beneath the surface produced ridges that altered the angle of a planted foot by fractions of a degree. He had not changed anything about the  
yard. He had simply understood it until its imperfections stopped being obstacles.  
Tomas did not call out or announce himself. He stood at the edge of the yard instead, his wooden saber resting against his shoulder, watching the quiet movements of  
the boy already there.  
Mateo noticed immediately, and continued the motion regardless. The ground held slightly differently than it had on the step before. Not wrong, only not identical. A  
patch compressed during yesterday's session had recovered overnight, and the resistance it offered was fractionally less than his muscle memory expected.  
He adjusted without thinking. The correction was invisible.  
Only then did he glance over his shoulder.  
"You're early," Mateo said calmly.  
Tomas didn't move. "You're later than you should be."  
Mateo glanced toward the sky, where the sun had only just begun to rise through the trees. The light was still more grey than gold, the kind that makes everything look  
slightly less real than it will in an hour.  
"The sun isn't even fully up."  
"Exactly."  
A brief silence followed.  
Somewhere beyond the yard a dull thud echoed through the village, someone already training, or possibly arguing in a manner that required physical reinforcement.  
Neither of them acknowledged it.  
Closer by, the first sounds of the village waking layered into the background. A door opening. Footsteps on a porch. The distant clatter of someone setting up a cooking  
station with the resigned efficiency of a person who had stopped pretending to enjoy it years ago.  
Mateo sighed, rolling his shoulders as if preparing for something unnecessarily repetitive.  
This is going to be exhausting. Not because the training will be difficult. Because Tomas has arrived with the specific energy of someone who spent the night thinking about  
something and decided today is the day he addresses it. That energy always produces longer sessions.  
"What are we doing?" he finally asked.  
Tomas stepped forward and lowered the saber into a ready position. His stance settled smoothly, no wasted motion, no hesitation, his footing distributing evenly across  
ground that had never once been level.  
"No holding back."  
Mateo blinked slowly.  
We have had this conversation several times, and each time the definition of "no holding back" shifts to accommodate whatever Tomas observed during the previous session.  
It has become a moving target, which, given Tomas's persistence, is probably the point.  
"Define that," he said.  
"Stop correcting only halfway," Tomas answered immediately. Then, after a beat: "And whatever you did at the end yesterday. Do it on purpose this time."  
Mateo studied him more closely.  
The change was subtle but present. Less impatience than before, less of the need to prove something immediately. In its place was focus, an intent that had taken shape  
overnight out of the vague frustration of their previous sessions.  
He is trying to understand it rather than rush through it. He spent the night analyzing yesterday and arrived with a specific objective rather than a general wish to improve.  
That is a meaningful shift.  
That is good.  
Annoying, but good.  
"You won't like the result," Mateo said after a short pause.  
Tomas didn't hesitate. "...Try me."  
Mateo exhaled once, then stepped fully into position. "Fine."  

Training began differently that morning.  
Tomas slowed everything down. Instead of pressing forward as he had before, he broke the movement into pieces: stance, breath, alignment, then strike. Each part  
deliberate, repeated until it felt natural before he continued to the next. It was the pace of someone who had realized that speed is the reward for precision rather than a  
substitute for it.  
"Like this," Tomas said, demonstrating a controlled swing before resetting. The saber moved through a clean arc, tighter than his usual style, the transition between  
extension and recovery smoother than it had been a week ago. "Again."  
Mateo watched closely.  
He is isolating the structure of each movement instead of forcing the outcome. The swing's arc has tightened by roughly three centimeters since our last session and his  
recovery has shortened by nearly a full beat. He has been practicing this on his own.  
"...Fine," Mateo said, stepping forward.  

They moved together now. Step, pivot, strike. The rhythm formed quickly, each repetition smoothing out inefficiency. Dust marked their steps in shallow arcs across the  
worn ground, the firmer soil holding the impressions while the softer patches released them almost at once.  
This time, Tomas wasn't trying to win.  
He was watching. Waiting.  
"There."  
Tomas stopped mid-motion. Mateo halted as well, his hand still raised. "What."  
"You shifted too early. Changed your footing too," Tomas said, pointing toward the impression Mateo's lead foot had left in the dirt. The print sat at a different angle  
than the one beside it, rotated a fraction outward, the weight favoring the outer edge. "Before I committed."  
A brief silence passed.  
He caught that. The adjustment came during his preparatory phase, before his weight committed to the strike. He noticed because he was watching my feet rather than my  
hands, which means he has stopped reacting to the visible attack and started reading the preparation beneath it.  
Mateo lowered his stance slightly, letting his weight settle more evenly. "...Yes."  
Tomas nodded slowly. "As if you already knew what I was going to do."  
Mateo didn't respond immediately.  
Because I did. The weight shift in your right hip telegraphs the angle of your swing three-tenths of a second before your arm moves. Your breathing compresses before a  
committed strike, which separates real attacks from feints. Your left foot rotates inward when you intend a follow-up, which tells me whether to expect one motion or two.  
Your body announced all of it before your mind gave the order.  
"You're predictable," he said instead.  
Tomas frowned. "Am I really that predictable?"  
Mateo tilted his head slightly. "Everyone is."  
"Maybe to you, you monster," Tomas said, a chuckle breaking through the frustration, the specific laugh of someone annoyed enough to be honest and amused enough  
not to mind.  
Then why does no one else notice? he thought, rolling his eyes.  
Before the silence could stretch too far, a loud voice cut across the yard.  
"HEY! If everyone is predictable, predict this!"  
Neither of them turned right away.  
"Something loud and unnecessary," Mateo replied.  
"...Okay, that's fair," Diego admitted, already halfway through stepping on, and then blaming, a rock that had not moved at all. His foot had found it with the unerring  
accuracy of someone capable of locating the single obstacle in an otherwise clear path.  
Tomas glanced over. Diego's case against the ground was thorough, impassioned, and entirely one-sided. He gestured at the rock. He pointed at his foot. He looked  
around for witnesses. The rock offered no defense and showed no remorse.  
"...He doesn't bother you?" Tomas asked.  
"...No," Mateo said calmly. "...I just ignore him. At least he doesn't overthink things."  
Tomas exhaled, something close to amusement crossing his face, the reluctant acknowledgment that Mateo had in a handful of words identified the precise quality that  
made Diego simultaneously infuriating and invaluable.  
"...I see why he likes you."  
What does that even mean? Diego likes everyone. He likes food. He likes rocks, despite the current dispute. His approval is not selective enough to be meaningful data.  
He chose not to investigate the comment further.  

Training resumed, and Mateo allowed more. Not evenly and not completely, but enough resistance to be meaningful and enough variation to be educational, enough to  
leave a gap Tomas could see and reach toward without the reaching feeling pointless.  
He is finding weaknesses that were invisible inside a continuous sequence, learning the corrections one at a time and then integrating them. That is not intuition. That is  
method. And method, unlike talent, does not plateau.  
"Again," Tomas said.  
"You've said that ten times."  
"Then we'll make it eleven."  
"You're persistent," Mateo replied.  
"You say that like it's a flaw."  
"It depends on the outcome."  
Another exchange followed, and then another. The dust had been disturbed so many times that it hung in a fine haze, catching the morning light that had finally  
committed to arriving and turning the space between them almost golden.  
Then Tomas overextended. Only slightly, a fraction beyond where his balance should have held, but once it happened it carried through the rest of the motion and  
compounded with every degree that followed. His shoulder moved past the line of his hip. His weight committed forward beyond clean recovery. The saber's arc  
widened by centimeters he could not reclaim.  
Mateo reacted, and something opened.  
His awareness narrowed. Not everything slowed; only his perception. The world continued at its normal pace, but the information inside it arrived with a clarity that had  
nothing in common with ordinary observation. A filter had been removed, and what lay beneath it was briefly accessible.  
The shift in Tomas's weight, one point three degrees past his balance point. The blade descending along an arc that would reach Mateo's left shoulder in four-tenths of  
a second. The exact moment the strike would stop being a strike and become an obligation Tomas's body could not withdraw from.  
The space between movement and outcome was no longer something to estimate. It was legible. The future existed not as possibility but as geometry, and geometry  
can be read, provided you know the language.  
He knew the language. He had written a great deal of it.  
Again.  
The pendant beneath his clothing grew warm.  
Options unfolded, not as thoughts but as paths branching out of the current moment into different consequences. Then they collapsed, all but one, the cleanest and  
most aligned with the principles he had spent years refining.  
He moved.  
His hand cut across Tomas's motion, and the quality of it had changed. Not faster. More certain. The path had been decided before the overextension occurred and had  
simply been waiting for permission, and it carried none of the hesitation that separates a choice from a reflex.  
The strike deflected cleanly, breaking Tomas's balance enough to force him back a step. It required almost no force, only precision and timing and contact placed at the  
exact point where his structure was weakest. The saber passed harmlessly aside. Tomas staggered, not from impact but from the sudden absence of resistance where  
resistance should have been.  

Mateo stopped immediately. The dust hanging in the air seemed to pause with him, suspended in the golden light as though waiting for permission to keep falling.  
He looked down at his own hand. It looked the same as it always had, fingers and palm and the calluses training had built beneath his knuckles. Nothing visible had  
changed.  
But something had moved through that hand, and it had not asked him first.  
That was not me.  
That was what I used to be, finding a gap in the wall and putting one hand through it.  
The pendant's warmth was already retreating toward its baseline, a window closing on a schedule that had nothing to do with his consent. He noted the timing. He  
noted that he had noted it. Beneath both observations sat a simpler fact: for the length of half a breath, he had not been the one deciding.  
Tomas was still standing where the deflection had pushed him, saber hanging loose at his side, breathing slightly elevated.  
He did not ask what had happened.  
"That wasn't a correction," he said.  
Mateo looked up.  
"You've been correcting me all morning. Every time I make a mistake, you show me the mistake." Tomas's voice was level, which was somehow worse. "That wasn't that.  
That was you stopping me."  
A pause stretched between them.  
"...Forget it," Mateo said.  
"No."  
The word landed flatly, without heat, and Tomas didn't move to continue the session or step back from it either. He simply let the disagreement stand in the open, which  
was the most effective thing he could have done and which he had almost certainly not planned.  
"...I'm not going to explain it."  
"I didn't ask you to." Tomas rolled his shoulder, testing it, finding nothing wrong. "I'm telling you I noticed. That's all."  
Then he reset his stance, because that was what Tomas did with things he could not resolve, and waited.  
Mateo stepped back into position.  
He will file this, the way he files everything, and eventually he will have enough of them to build a shape out of.  
I should be more careful.  
I have been thinking that quite a lot lately.  

They stepped aside eventually to recover.  
Diego had progressed significantly during this time. Not in training. In eating.  
He had acquired, through means Mateo chose not to investigate, a collection of food arranged on a flat stone beside the yard with the attention of someone curating an  
exhibition. A rice wrap. Something fried that smelled considerably better than it looked. A small clay cup of broth.  
"Where did you even get that?" Mateo asked.  
"I brought it," Diego said proudly.  
"You brought food... to watch us train?"  
"Obviously."  
There is an efficiency here I may have underestimated. He has optimized his morning to include entertainment, nutrition, and social interaction without expending any  
physical effort whatsoever. From a systems perspective, this is the most rational use of time anyone in this yard has demonstrated today.  
"You didn't bring enough."  
Diego froze mid-bite. "...For who?"  
Mateo gestured. Toward Tomas, wiping sweat from his forehead with his sleeve. Toward Inés, who had arrived quietly at some point during the session and now stood  
at the yard's edge with her arms folded, watching with the stillness of someone who had been there longer than anyone realized. And toward himself.  
Diego looked down at the small amount of food. Then back up.  
"...Wow."  
"Yes."  
"You're all terrible people."  

That night the village quieted the way it always did, not completely, but enough that sound traveled farther than intended. The transition from day to night was never a  
clean line here. It was a gradual dimming, a slow compression of activity into smaller and smaller spaces until only the most persistent sounds remained.  
Somewhere in the distance, someone was still training, the rhythmic thud of a strike against a practice post, steady and unhurried, the sound of a person who trained  
not because they had been told to but because stopping felt wrong. Elsewhere, a quiet argument continued between two voices Mateo could not identify, the words  
indistinct, the tone carrying the texture of a disagreement that had gone on long enough to lose its original subject.  
The forest remained present. Its canopy stretched over the village in a dark lattice that broke the sky into fragments, and the sounds it produced, the creak of wood, the  
rustle of leaves, the occasional call of something that hunted after dark, ran beneath the village's own noises as a second conversation.  
Mateo lay still, letting his body rest. The pendant sat against his chest, warm the way it had been for weeks, a low-grade presence he had stopped noticing.  
Except it was not quite what it had been that morning. Whatever opened during the exchange had not fully closed afterward. It had narrowed. It had not sealed.  
Sleep came quickly anyway.  
But what followed was not sleep.  

Color replaced darkness.  
Not gradually, and not with the slow fade of a dream assembling itself out of memory and sensation. It happened immediately, as though someone had thrown open a  
door into a room that had already been lit before he arrived.  
Bright, unnatural bursts of light expanded outward, sitting halfway between controlled creation and excessive presentation. The colors didn't move the way light moves.  
They expanded in structured layers, folding as they spread, each one arriving with a precision that suggested design rather than spontaneity. Purples bled into golds that  
sharpened into whites, and the whites fractured into prismatic edges that held exactly long enough to be noticed.  
It was, Mateo realized, a performance. Something was showing off.  
The darkness reorganized itself into geometry, flat planes assembling beneath his feet and curved surfaces arching overhead. A stage being built in real time by  
someone who wanted the audience to watch the construction and be impressed by it.  
Mateo blinked. "...What is this."  
A platform formed beneath his feet, smooth and faintly glowing. It didn't rise. It simply existed where nothing had been a moment earlier, as though the concept of a  
floor had been introduced to this space and the space had reluctantly complied.  
Then something dropped from above.  
With a soft pop, the specific and slightly ridiculous sound of something arriving in a space not designed to accommodate arrivals, a small figure landed in front of him.  

It stood upright.  
Its ears were too large for its head, pointed and slightly asymmetrical, as though they had grown independently and never agreed on a final shape. Its body was  
compact, covered in short fur that might have been grey or silver depending on light that refused to hold still long enough to settle the question. Its eyes were large and  
round, carrying the specific luminance of something fully aware of its own importance.  
Its presence was far too confident for its size.  
A microphone was held in one paw.  
"...Welcome!" it announced, with the practiced projection of someone who had rehearsed the moment extensively, spreading its free paw outward and presenting the  
surrounding void as though it were a gift.  
Then it paused. Looked around at the incomplete, flickering, still-assembling space. Then directly at Mateo.  
"...Wait."  
Mateo stared back.  
What is that. It is small. It is holding a microphone. It arrived with a sound effect. It appears to have prepared a speech. None of these observations assemble into anything  
coherent.  
The creature narrowed its eyes. "...You're not supposed to be here yet."  
"I could say the same," Mateo replied.  
A pause. The creature's expression landed somewhere between offended and amused, in the territory where small creatures with large ears and excessive confidence  
tend to reside.  
"...This is a preliminary broadcast," it said stiffly, drawing itself up to its full height, which changed nothing about the power dynamic but appeared to make it feel better.  
"Please disregard."  
Mateo glanced at the microphone. "...You're holding a device specifically designed to be noticed."  
"...Presentation matters."  
"...Clearly."  
The creature tapped the microphone against its paw. Once. Twice. "...Testing. Testing."  
Nothing echoed. The sound left the microphone, traveled roughly six inches, and was absorbed by the surrounding space as though the space had decided it was not  
yet prepared to support acoustics.  
The creature frowned. "...We'll fix that."  
"Is the we in that sentence referring to you and someone else," Mateo asked, "or you and the microphone?"  
"...That is not your concern."  
"It became my concern when I arrived in a space I did not choose to enter, occupied by a creature I did not expect to meet, who is holding equipment that does not  
function."  
The creature stared at him for a long moment. Then it sighed, and the sound was far too deep and far too weary for something its size, as though the exhale had been  
borrowed from a much larger being.  
"...You're going to be a problem later, aren't you?"  
Mateo considered it. Based on the available evidence, it was a reasonable prediction.  
"That seems evident."  
The creature muttered to itself, turning partially away at a volume clearly intended to be overheard. "...Entry filters unstable... access controls broken... unauthorized  
consciousness in the staging area..." It glanced back. "...This is what happens when you rush a calibration sequence."  
Mateo folded his arms. "Who are you?"  
The creature straightened and adjusted the microphone in its paw with the deliberate care of someone arranging a prop for maximum impact.  
"I am the Realm Spirit."  
"...Of what."  
"...Classified."  
"...Of course it is."  
The creature stared at him. "...You're very calm about this."  
"I've seen worse."  
That was not entirely accurate. Mateo had, in fact, been the worse that other beings had seen. But it served its purpose. Whatever reaction the creature had expected its  
appearance to provoke, it would not be finding it here.  
A pause. Then the creature sighed again.  
"...You're going to be a problem later, aren't you."  
"You already asked that."  
"I was hoping the answer had changed."  
"It has not."  
The world flickered, not collapsing, only failing to settle. The geometry around them shimmered at its edges, the colors destabilizing before reestablishing themselves  
with noticeably less conviction.  
"...This isn't fully open yet anyway," the creature said, waving a paw meant to look dismissive and carrying the quiet urgency of someone who knew the infrastructure  
beneath them was less stable than it looked. "Come back later."  
"I didn't ask to be here."  
"Too bad."  
Everything collapsed instantly. Not dramatically, and not with the violence of a space being destroyed. It simply stopped. The colors, the geometry, the platform, the  
creature, all of it ceasing in the same instant.  

Mateo's eyes opened.  
The night returned. Still. Quiet. The ceiling of his room above him, wooden beams, uneven, familiar, and the sounds of the village at rest filtering through the walls.  
He remained unmoving, staring upward.  
That was not a dream. Dreams are assembled out of memory. That space was not assembled. It was built, externally, by something that was not me. The creature existed  
independently of my awareness and was surprised to find me there, which means the space was not generated by my mind but accessed by it.  
The pendant was the bridge. It opened this morning during the exchange with Tomas and it did not fully close afterward. Tonight the remaining gap was sufficient.  
The pendant rested in his hand, warm.  
He turned it slowly. The pulse that had become familiar over the past weeks felt different tonight. Not stronger, not faster. More directed, as though energy that had  
previously spread in every direction at once had begun orienting toward something specific.  
Fragments. Forming something.  
He exhaled quietly and closed his eyes again.  

I will deal with it later. The space is incomplete; the creature confirmed as much. The entry was accidental rather than deliberate. There is nothing to act on yet.  
A small pause followed.  
Then:  
That thing was ridiculous. It had a microphone. It attempted a sound check in a space that could not support sound. And it sighed at me twice, as though my existence were  
a personal inconvenience it had not budgeted for.  
A faint smile appeared on Mateo's face.  
I have questions.  
It was not willing to answer them.  
That is fine.  
I am patient.  
Then, stillness.  
Not empty. Not silent.  
Waiting.
