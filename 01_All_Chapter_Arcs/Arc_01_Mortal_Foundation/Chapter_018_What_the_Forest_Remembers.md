# Chapter 18 - What the Forest Remembers

Chapter 18 — What the Forest Remembers  

He went east for two hours, and the hawk did not come back.  
That was informative in itself. Whatever its patrol line was, it did not run tight enough to catch a boy walking beneath the canopy at a normal pace, which  
meant the line was oriented toward something other than the ground. It was watching for things in the air, or things large enough to disturb the canopy, or  
something else entirely that he had no framework for and would not get one by guessing.  
So he stopped guessing and started reading what was actually in front of him, which was the ground.  
His posture stayed loose, because tension spent energy and energy was a resource. His attention did not. It had gone wide during his rest in the clearing,  
spread out across everything within range; now it narrowed to a cone ahead of him, tracking terrain and canopy and the ambient patterns together, with the  
specific focus of someone who had recently been reminded that this forest contained things he could not confront.  
He began collecting as he moved.  
The collecting was not incidental and it was not random. His path bent between clusters of growth in small deviations that avoided the obvious routes, and  
every deviation followed a logic connecting what he could see above the ground to what he could feel beneath it.  
Some plants grew normally, rooted in soil that felt stable, their growth consistent with the light and water available. Common. Useful. Unremarkable. The  
kind of material old man Chen sold at triple markup while lecturing the buyer about storage technique.  
Others did not.  
A cluster of broad-leafed plants at the base of an old oak caught him — not by appearance, which was ordinary, but by vitality. The leaves ran greener than  
everything around them. The stems were thicker. When he exposed a portion of the root system, carefully, it went deeper than the species had any reason to  
go, reaching down toward something the plant clearly wanted more of.  
He crouched and put his palm flat on the soil.  
The sensation arrived immediately. Warmth, and not temperature. A current running through the earth beneath the plants, feeding them. It was faint and it  
was consistent, and the plants had arranged themselves around it with the precision of organisms that could not think but could respond.  
The concentration in this soil is higher than the surrounding area, and it is not higher by accident. They are not healthier because of light or water. They are  
healthier because of what is underneath them.  
He harvested several specimens, cutting rather than pulling, preserving the roots so the cluster would regenerate, taking only what had reached full  
maturity. The quality was obvious in the hand. The leaves held their structure. The stems resisted the knife with a resilience that meant the energy they had  
absorbed had been built into the tissue rather than simply passing through it.  
This is what Chen's stock should be. He is not a poor merchant. He is buying from the wrong ground and has never once been told.  
And the woman at the stall, who arranged her bundles by interaction properties and separated the ones that would spoil each other, and who had  
understood more about her material through forty years of handling it than most alchemists learned from texts — she was working with the same  
disadvantage. Everything in the village came from village soil. Stable soil. Even soil. Soil that produced consistent, moderate, entirely reliable results and  
would never produce anything else.  
The thought connected to the mixture that had failed behind his house, the one whose layers had refused to merge despite correct proportions and correct  
timing, because the binding agent and the primary compounds had come from ground with different signatures and could not be made to integrate.  
Here, everything fed from the same current carried the same signature.  
It was never a technique problem. Technique is what you do after the material is already determined. The material is determined by the ground, and the ground  
is determined by whatever is moving beneath it.  
Every alchemist in the village is solving the last step of the problem and calling it the whole problem.  
He filed it. The understanding was not complete — he could feel the currents and not see them, identify their effects and not their structure, register that  
they varied without being able to map the variation. But the relationship between ground, quality, and compatibility had moved from theory into something  
he had confirmed with his own hands, and that was worth the walk on its own.  

An hour further east, the ground changed underneath him.  
Not abruptly. The transition took perhaps a hundred meters, a gradual shift in what arrived through the soles of his shoes. The supportive firmness of the  
first stretch thinned out. The ground softened. The ambient flow, which had been smooth and consistent, developed a fine unevenness that made his  
breathing adjust before he had decided to adjust it.  
The vegetation changed with it. Broad leaves gave way to ferns and low dense ground cover that preferred shade and moisture. The soil held more water,  
and the air carried a dampness that settled on his skin. Even the trees were different — bark smoother, roots spreading wide and shallow instead of driving  
down, as though there were less below worth reaching for.  
His steps adjusted without instruction. Each footfall sank slightly deeper, and the soil gave up water under compression, and his pace slowed. Not from  
caution. From the increased demand on his balance.  
This ground disperses rather than concentrates. The energy runs outward instead of pooling, and everything growing here reflects that. Softer. Less stable. Less  
worth defending.  
Then something else surfaced, and it had nothing to do with energy.  
Vegetation disturbed recently. Not harvested — displaced. Root systems damaged and abandoned. Plants broken and left. Stems bent at angles that meant  
something had gone through without caring what it went through.  
Waste.  
He frowned very slightly.  
Not gathering. Moving. Quickly, and without attention to anything except the direction of travel. The damage is incidental. This is someone crossing this area to  
get somewhere else and not looking at any of it.  
The signs grew more frequent as he continued. Broken branches at varying heights. Disturbed soil. Once, in softer ground, most of a boot print.  

Same individual as the cuts near the boundary. He has come this way more than once, and he is not slowing down for anything.  

He stopped where the ground told him to.  
The energy beneath him, which had been dispersing through the soft terrain in every direction at once, gathered without warning. Not smoothly. Sharper  
than the stable zone had been, and denser, carrying a quality he had not encountered before — a pressure pushing upward through the soil rather than  
flowing along beneath it.  
He crouched and set both palms flat against the earth.  
Two currents. Different directions. They intersect here.  
The soil under his hands was warm, and the sun had not reached this stretch of ground in what looked like years. The two flows met at an angle and  
produced turbulence, and in the turbulence the ambient energy compressed into a small area far denser than anything around it.  
And growing in that compressed soil, feeding on the intersection, was something he did not recognize.  
Its leaves were nearly black-green, dark in a way that suggested extreme concentration in the tissue. The stem was rigid, thick for its height, and when he  
touched it, it resisted his fingers more like wood than herb. Where soil had eroded away from the base, the root system glowed faintly — not visibly, not in  
any way a person would see at noon or at dusk, but his awareness, extended through his fingertips, could feel a luminescence sitting at the boundary  
between the physical and something else. The plant had been so thoroughly saturated that its biology had begun expressing energy in ways botany did not  
account for.  
"...That won't work," he murmured.  
He studied it properly. The potency was extraordinary and so was the instability. The two currents feeding it carried different characteristics, and the plant  
had absorbed both without integrating either. Cutting it carelessly would release the stored energy in a burst that would take the useful compounds with it.  
Processing it without accounting for the doubled signature would reproduce exactly the failure that had ruined his preparation at home, scaled up by an  
order of magnitude.  
"...No separation. No isolation. No stability."  
He traced the pathways with his awareness, following the way the two currents entered the root system from different angles and met inside the stem.  
"...That's irritating."  
He looked around carefully. The forest moved — wind through the canopy, something crossing the undergrowth far enough away to be irrelevant. Nothing  
close. Nothing watching.  
Except, at the edge of his vision, a small plant barely a handspan tall, with leaves that oriented toward movement rather than light.  
It had not been there when he arrived. He was almost certain of that.  
Mateo sighed lightly.  
"...You're about to see something you probably shouldn't."  
The plant did not respond, obviously, because it was a plant.  
He could have sworn it leaned in.  

He worked quickly.  
The technique was not something the village had taught him. It was not something the village could have taught him, because it sat at an intersection of  
rune work and energy handling and spatial reasoning that no one there operated at.  
He did not ask himself where it came from. He had stopped asking months ago. It surfaced when he needed it, arriving from somewhere beneath his own  
awareness like a tool out of a drawer he did not remember filling — never complete, always in fragments, illuminating a principle and then receding and  
leaving the understanding behind without its origin.  
That is two things now.  
The thought arrived unwelcome and precisely on schedule. Two things he had declined to examine: why he had walked east this morning instead of  
following the pull, and where his hands learned what they knew.  
I have a functioning method for examining things. I have used it on everything I have encountered since I could hold my own head up. And I have now set it  
aside twice in one day, both times for questions about myself.  
He noted it. He did not act on it, which he was aware was the third instance.  
Then he put his hands on either side of the herb and extended his awareness into the soil, finding the two currents, tracing them to the exact point of  
intersection and the exact angle of collision. With the care of someone adjusting the anchor points of a formation — and the comparison was not accidental,  
and he noticed that too — he applied a controlled pulse that separated the two flows inside the root system.  
Not permanently. Not destructively. Long enough.  
The energy inside the plant reorganized. The doubled signature resolved into two isolated channels, each stable within its own path, and the volatility that  
would have destroyed the specimen on harvest settled into a temporary equilibrium.  
He cut cleanly. The stem separated. The root system, relieved of what it had been carrying, settled back into the soil to begin regenerating.  
Then the silence changed.  
Something reacted. Not nearby. Not in the forest around him at all. Far off, and beneath — a faint distortion in the ambient energy, a ripple, except that the  
ripple moved inward, toward the point where he had just adjusted the currents, the way attention moves toward a sound.  
It lasted less than a breath and was gone.  
Mateo's expression did not change.  
Something felt that. Distance significant. It traveled through the ground rather than the air, which means whatever noticed is connected to the same system I  
just put my hands into.  
I did not disturb this forest. I touched something that runs underneath it, and something at the other end of it turned to look.  
Against his chest, the pendant had gone briefly thin — the same flattening of its rhythm it had produced under the hawk's shadow — and then resumed.  
Twice now. It hides before I do.  
"...Better," he said quietly, which was true of the herb and true of nothing else.  
He stored everything: the specimen, two soil samples, the observations about intersections and energy-fed growth and the fact that the forest's energy  
system was not passive.  
Behind him, the small plant had retreated roughly two meters from where it had been.  
Mateo looked at it for a while.  

"...Well. That was not expected."  
He had been fairly certain plants could not move.  
He was becoming less certain by the hour, and he found that he did not mind.  

He moved on, and the forest resumed around him — birds returning to their calls, wind resuming its layered pass through the canopy, undergrowth rustling  
with small creatures that had paused during the silence and were cautiously getting on with things.  
But it did not behave the same way around him now.  
The ambient flow in his immediate vicinity ran differently than it had before he arrived. Not disrupted. Not disturbed. Adjusted, the way water adjusts around  
a stone that has been set in it — not fighting the stone, simply acknowledging that something is there.  
It has stopped treating me as weather and started treating me as terrain.  
He found that both interesting and mildly concerning, in roughly equal measure.  

The calm ended near water.  
The pond sat in a shallow depression ringed by twisted roots and smooth stone, a basin carved by seasons of collection and retreat. Its surface held the  
broken canopy light in fragments that shifted against each other as the water moved in response to currents too fine to see.  
The energy here was different again. Calmer than the intersection. Steadier than the dispersive ground. The water acted as a medium, taking ambient energy  
from the surrounding soil and holding it and circulating it slowly through the basin in patterns he could feel through the ground without touching the  
surface.  
He crouched at the margin, drawn down by something beneath it.  
"...It should be here."  
He worked his fingers into the soil where water met earth. The ground was dense there, compacted by moisture and time into something nearer clay, and it  
resisted him with the stubbornness of ground that had not been disturbed in a long while.  
Then his fingers found them.  
Golden-threaded roots, pulsing beneath the soil in a slow rhythmic luminescence he could feel through his fingertips as clearly as his own pulse. Thin, dense,  
woven through the clay in patterns that meant years of growth feeding on whatever the pond's circulation concentrated here.  
"...Found you."  
The growl arrived immediately. Low, deep, resonant enough that it reached him through his knees before it reached his ears.  
He did not turn.  
"...You again."  
The alpha stood at the clearing's edge. Alone this time. No pack, no formation, no younger wolves at the tree line. Just the alpha, and it looked larger than  
he remembered, and its focus had sharpened considerably since the morning.  
There was no confusion in its posture now.  
It did not follow my scent. I have been careful with my scent.  
He looked down at the golden roots under his hands, and then at the direction he had come from, and then at the wolf.  
It came from the west. From the intersection.  
It did not track me. It felt what I did to the ground, and it came to see what was standing in the middle of it.  
That reframed the entire morning, and it did so in a way that was not favorable. The wolf was not defending territory in the sense that animals defended  
territory, marking a perimeter and objecting to trespass. It had been holding this stretch of forest for longer than most of the trees, and the thing that made  
this stretch worth holding was underneath it, and Mateo had spent the afternoon putting his hands into it.  
I did not walk into its range. I walked into the reason it has a range.  
The alpha's weight shifted forward, and the shift carried no ambiguity at all.  
Mateo exhaled slowly and let his fingers withdraw from the soil.  
"...You chose incorrectly."  
He stood. Unhurried, the way he moved when a situation called for precision rather than speed, his weight settling through the chain — feet, ankles, knees,  
hips, spine, shoulders — each link engaging in sequence.  
His hands stayed at his sides. His breathing stayed even. His expression did not change.  
Something in the clearing did.  
The ambient energy, which had been circulating slowly through the basin, compressed. Not because he had manipulated it. Not because he had released his  
suppression or projected anything measurable.  
Because he had stopped being passive, and the forest, which had spent all day noticing him, felt the difference.  
So did the alpha. Its growl dropped lower. Its body sank, muscle gathering under the dense fur, energy concentrating instinctively through its frame in  
preparation for something its body had already committed to and its mind was still deciding on.  
Mateo looked at the wolf.  
The wolf looked at Mateo.  
"...Alright."  
And this time he did not intend to play.
