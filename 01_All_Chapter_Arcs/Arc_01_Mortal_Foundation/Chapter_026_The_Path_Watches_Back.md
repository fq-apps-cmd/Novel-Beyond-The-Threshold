# Chapter 26 - The Path Watches Back

Chapter 26 — The Path Watches Back  

The canyon had not been visible until Mateo was already inside it.  
The forest thinned gradually as he moved deeper — not in a clean boundary but in uneven withdrawals of growth, as though the trees had been retreating  
from something for a long time and had each chosen their own pace. Trunks shortened and thickened, their bark darker and more heavily scarred than the  
trees closer to the village. Branches twisted low and inward rather than reaching for the canopy, their growth patterns suggesting decades of adaptation to  
conditions that did not favor height. The undergrowth diminished — fewer ferns, fewer ground-cover plants, fewer of the living indicators that had  
characterized the earlier zones. What remained was sparse, hardy, and deeply rooted, as though the plants that survived here had done so by committing  
everything to their foundations and nothing to display.  
The ground hardened beneath his steps as soil gave way to fractured stone. The transition was not sudden — the soil thinned in layers, each step revealing  
more rock beneath it, until the earth became a veneer over a skeleton of stone that had been there long before anything had tried to grow on top of it. The  
rock was not uniform. It fractured in patterns that suggested geological stress — compression from below, expansion from the sides, the accumulated  
evidence of forces that had been acting on this terrain for longer than the forest had existed.  
Visibility worsened — not from obstruction, but from distortion. Angled rock faces caught and redirected light in ways that the flat, open forest floor never  
had. Overlapping ridges created shadows that did not correspond to the objects casting them. Narrow channels between stone formations folded light into  
strange patterns that made distance unreliable — a formation that appeared to be twenty meters away might be ten, or fifty, and the only way to confirm was  
to walk to it and discover which estimate the terrain had decided to honor.  
Mateo slowed slightly, his gaze tracking the inconsistencies with the focused attention of someone who recognized that visual information was no longer  
trustworthy and who was already shifting his primary navigation to other senses.  
"...This terrain doesn't want to be read cleanly," he said quietly.  
A small pause. His feet registered the stone beneath them — harder than the forest floor, carrying vibration differently, each step producing a faint resonance  
that traveled through the rock and returned information about density, depth, and the presence or absence of hollow spaces beneath the surface.  
"...That's deliberate," he added. "Or it became that over time."  
The distinction mattered. Deliberate distortion implied design — someone or something had shaped this canyon to confuse navigation. Natural distortion  
implied geology — the terrain's properties were incidental, produced by forces that had no interest in who walked through the result. The difference between  
the two would determine whether Mateo was entering a space that had been created to test visitors or a space that simply happened to be difficult.  
His awareness extended downward through his feet — the same vein-detection sensitivity that had been developing since his first steps into the forest. The  
energy beneath this stone was different from anything he had felt before. Not flowing, not pooling, not dispersing. Compressed. The stone itself seemed to  
hold energy the way a clenched fist held tension — not circulating it, not using it, simply containing it under pressure that had been building for a very long  
time.  
He filed the observation and continued.  
It was there he found the opening.  
Not obvious. Not marked. Not the kind of entrance that announced itself or that would reward casual observation. Just a fracture in the canyon wall — two  
slabs of stone pressed together imperfectly, separated over time by a gap narrow enough to escape notice from any angle except the one Mateo happened  
to be standing at.  
The gap was approximately thirty centimeters wide at its broadest point and tapered to nothing at both top and bottom. Dust had accumulated in the lower  
portion, undisturbed — no footprints, no drag marks, no evidence that anything had passed through recently. The stone on either side was weathered evenly,  
suggesting the fracture had existed for decades at minimum.  
Or to shelter something.  
Mateo turned sideways and stepped inside.  
"...Good enough."  
The interior was shallow at first — a natural cavity just large enough to sit without being seen from outside. The walls curved inward, creating a space that  
was wider at floor level than at head height, as though the stone had been compressed from above at some point in the geological past. The air shifted  
immediately upon entry: cooler by several degrees, still in a way that the canyon's air had not been, dense with the mineral weight of stone that had been  
holding moisture for centuries without releasing it.  
No sound from outside penetrated. No motion disturbed the dust on the floor. The space existed in a state of isolation so complete that it felt less like a cave  
and more like a pause — a place where time had slowed because nothing had given it a reason to continue at its normal pace.  
He breathed in once, slow. The air tasted of stone and age and something faintly metallic that he had learned to associate with concentrated ambient energy.  
"...Stable," he murmured. "...Or pretending to be."  
A place to recover. Or a place something had once used for the same purpose. The distinction, again, mattered — and would reveal itself with observation.  
Mateo lowered himself carefully against the inner wall, one hand resting near his side where the injury pulled tight under the bandage. The earlier alchemy —  
the structured layering of Veinbite Root, Emberleaf, Plume Moss, and Clearwater Bulb — had stabilized the wound. Sealed. Balanced. Controlled. But the  
damage was not gone. The alpha's spiritual residue still lingered in the shoulder wound, and the rib cut, while closed, protested any motion that required full  
engagement of his left side.  
"...Still inefficient," he muttered. "...But workable."  
He reached into his satchel again. This time, lower-tier herbs — the common preparations he had brought from the village, materials that old man Chen  
would have considered beneath his attention and that Mateo considered perfectly adequate for the task at hand.  
No spectacle. No complex weaving. No spatial manipulation or suspended matrices. Just precision — controlled heat from a small flame between his fingers,  
minimal energy loss through careful temperature management, structured layering over the existing treatment to reinforce what had already been done.  
"...No reason to waste resources on something simple," he said quietly. "If this requires more than this... I made a mistake earlier."  
Reinforcement, not reconstruction. Enough to maintain alignment. Enough to move without degradation.  
When finished, he leaned back and exhaled slowly. The warmth of the treatment settled into his tissues — the Emberleaf's residual heat working its way  

through the sealed wound, the stabilizing compounds holding everything in place. Not healed. But maintained. The difference between a blade that was  
sharp and a blade that would hold its edge long enough to matter.  
Silence returned.  
Not natural silence. Not empty. Not peaceful.  
Aware.  
Mateo's eyes opened again.  
"...That's the problem."  
He stood, letting his weight settle through his frame with the careful attention of someone whose body was operating at less than full capacity and who was  
compensating through precision rather than power.  
At first glance, the cave was empty — stone walls, stone floor, the narrow entrance behind him, dust, silence.  
Now — it wasn't.  
The awareness had not arrived. It had been revealed — the way something hidden became visible not because it moved but because the observer's  
perception adjusted. The cave was not watching him. The cave was occupied by something that was watching him, and the something was so deeply  
embedded in the stone's structure that distinguishing it from the cave itself required the specific kind of attention that most people did not possess.  
"...You don't feel like an empty space," he said softly. "You feel like something that's waiting to be noticed."  
The cloth caught his attention first.  
Faded, torn, half-buried in a thin layer of dust near the wall where the stone curved closest to the floor. It was small — perhaps the size of a hand — and its  
edges were frayed in a way that suggested tearing rather than cutting. The fabric was woven, not rough-spun — a quality that did not match the cave's  
apparent isolation.  
He crouched and lifted it carefully, his fingers registering the texture before his eyes processed the visual.  
It didn't fall apart.  
"...Preserved."  
He turned it slightly between his fingers. The fabric should have degraded. In an environment this damp, with this much mineral content in the air, untreated  
cloth would lose its structural integrity within months. This piece had been here for what appeared to be years — possibly decades — and the fibers still held  
their weave.  
"...Too preserved."  
That meant influence. Not recent — the dust covering it was undisturbed and consistent with the rest of the cave floor's accumulation. But something had  
been acting on this fabric since it was left here, maintaining its integrity against the conditions that should have destroyed it.  
"...Something kept this from ending normally."  
He set it aside and looked up.  
The wall behind it felt wrong — not visibly, not in any way that a casual glance would identify. The stone's surface appeared as irregular and natural as every  
other surface in the cave. But the irregularity didn't align with natural fracturing. The ridges were too evenly spaced. The lines between them were too  
consistent in depth. The form was attempting to hide intent — and succeeding, unless the observer knew what intent looked like when it disguised itself as  
geology.  
Mateo stepped closer, narrowing his eyes.  
"...Hiding something poorly," he murmured.  
A beat.  
"...Or hiding it from the wrong kind of inspection."  
He didn't see it immediately. The concealment was good — not the kind of work that announced itself, but the kind that relied on the assumption that no  
one would look this closely at a cave wall in a canyon that most people would never find. It was security through obscurity rather than security through  
strength.  
Then his gaze adjusted. Not his physical vision — his awareness. The same sensitivity that had been developing since his first steps into the forest, the  
perception that registered energy patterns through contact and proximity rather than through sight alone.  
"...There."  
A fragment, embedded into the stone, cracked and eroded nearly flat. Its surface had been worn by time and moisture until it was almost indistinguishable  
from the surrounding rock. Almost.  
A talisman.  
What remained of it.  
Its lines were nearly gone — reduced to faint intersections that no longer held active power, the channels that had once carried energy now dry and inert. Yet  
the structure beneath them still existed. The architecture of the formation was intact even though the formation itself had failed. Like a riverbed that  
remembered the shape of water long after the river had dried.  
Like a memory refusing to fully disappear.  
Sealing work. Old. Worn. Breaking. But not broken — not completely. The structure held because the design had been good enough that even degradation  
could not fully erase it. Whoever had placed this talisman had understood formation architecture at a level that Mateo recognized and respected.  
He studied it quietly.  
"...You held longer than you were supposed to," he said softly. "...Or something kept you going."  
He lifted his hand. Paused. The decision to touch a degraded talisman was not trivial — the act of contact could trigger whatever residual function remained,  
and residual functions in old formations were unpredictable by definition.  
"...Let's see which."  
Then he touched it.  
Nothing.  
For half a breath.  
Then something shifted — not the talisman, not the stone, not anything physical. The space itself changed. The ambient energy in the cave, which had been  
pooled passively in the stone walls, moved — not outward, not inward, but through, as though a channel that had been blocked for decades had just been  
cleared and the accumulated pressure behind it was now finding its way to wherever it had originally been directed.  
"...There it is."  
The fragment collapsed instantly, crumbling into dust beneath his fingertip. The structure that had held for so long — maintaining itself through sheer  
architectural integrity — had been the anchor. Not the seal. The anchor. The point around which the rest of the formation organized itself. And Mateo's touch  

had not broken it. It had released it — communicated to the talisman's residual function that the contact it had been waiting for had arrived, and that its vigil  
was complete.  
The wall responded.  
A line appeared — thin, not carved but separating, the stone dividing along a seam that had been invisible moments before. Then another. Then many. The  
lines spread across the wall's surface in patterns that were too geometric to be geological — precise angles, deliberate intersections, the unmistakable  
signature of formation work rendered in stone rather than ink.  
Mateo stepped back calmly, watching.  
"...So you were the anchor."  
Hairline fractures spread outward, branching in patterns that followed the formation lines — each crack tracing a channel, each channel connecting to the  
next, the entire network revealing itself like a diagram being drawn in real time across the stone's surface.  
The entire section trembled — not violently, but deliberately. The vibration was controlled, measured, the kind of movement that occurred when a structure  
was releasing stored energy in a managed sequence rather than an uncontrolled collapse.  
A low sound followed. Not collapse. Not breaking.  
Memory.  
The sound of stone remembering what it had been built to do.  
"...You weren't sealing something in," Mateo said quietly, his voice carrying the specific tone of someone revising an assessment in real time. "...You were  
holding the structure in place."  
Something held for too long — maintaining a configuration that had served its purpose decades ago — finally recognizing that it no longer needed to  
remain.  
The air shifted. Pressure expanded outward, brushing past Mateo's skin in a wave that carried the concentrated staleness of air that had been trapped behind  
stone for longer than he had been alive.  
He didn't resist it.  
"...Release phase," he murmured.  
The structure let go.  
Mateo took another step back.  
"...There it is."  
The runes revealed themselves — not written on the stone's surface, but uncovered from within it. Old formation lines spread faintly across the exposed rock,  
their patterns describing functions that Mateo's understanding of formation architecture allowed him to read like text.  
Isolation. Concealment. Deferral.  
Three functions. The first two were straightforward — the formation had been designed to isolate whatever lay behind the wall from detection and to conceal  
the wall itself from casual inspection. The third was less common.  
Deferral.  
Mateo's gaze traced the rune patterns.  
"...Incomplete," he said. "...Either degraded... or intentionally unfinished."  
The deferral function suggested that the formation had been designed to delay — not permanently seal, but postpone. Whatever lay behind this wall was  
meant to be found. Eventually. By someone. The formation's purpose was not to prevent access but to control timing — to ensure that the contents remained  
hidden until the conditions for their discovery were met.  
Conditions that, apparently, included someone capable of recognizing a degraded talisman for what it was and touching it with awareness rather than force.  
The wall fractured inward. Stone collapsed into hollow space behind it — not chaotically, but in sections, each piece falling in sequence as the formation's  
release progressed through its designed stages. The collapse was architectural. Controlled. The work of someone who had planned for this moment even as  
they sealed the wall shut.  
A passage.  
Shaped. Deliberate.  
"...So this was never meant to be permanent," he murmured. "...Just hidden."  
A final section broke. And the opening cleared.  
Cold air flowed out — older than the cave's air, denser, carrying the specific quality of atmosphere that had been sealed for so long that it had developed its  
own character. The scent was mineral and deep, with an undertone that Mateo could not immediately identify but that his vein-detection awareness  
registered as significant — concentrated energy, compressed by enclosure, waiting.  
"...That's not empty."  
A beat.  
"...That's depth."  
He stood at the threshold. Looked inside. The darkness beyond was not the absence of light. It was the presence of space — vast, structured, patient.  
No hesitation.  
"...That explains it."  
He stepped forward.  
Silence returned. Not natural silence, not empty or peaceful, but aware. The faint cold drifting from below brushed against his skin in uneven pulses — not  
rhythmic like breathing, not random like wind, but responsive. Each pulse arrived at a different interval, as though the space was adjusting to his presence in  
real time.  
"It's not air movement," Mateo said quietly, pausing. "...It's structural response."  
It wasn't wind. It wasn't breath. It was the space itself reacting to the weight of a new presence within it — movement without motion, change without action.  
The labyrinth was not alive. But it was not inert.  
Mateo stepped fully inside.  
The moment his weight settled, the space changed — not visibly, but fundamentally. The quality of the air shifted. The temperature dropped another degree.  
The ambient energy, which had been concentrated in the cave above, existed here at a different density entirely — not pooled, but structured. Organized.  
Moving through the stone in patterns that Mateo could feel through his feet with a clarity that exceeded anything the forest floor had provided.  
The rough stone beneath his fingers didn't smooth in shape, but in structure. When he pressed his palm lightly against the wall, the surface felt the same —  
irregular, weathered, cold. But the energy within it was arranged. Channels existed beneath the surface — not natural vein-like flows, but carved pathways.  
Formation work embedded so deeply in the stone that it had become indistinguishable from the rock's own composition.  

"...Constructed first," he said softly. "Then aged to look natural."  
A small pause.  
"...Good work."  
Whoever had built this had understood something that most formation architects did not — that the best concealment was not hiding something behind a  
wall, but making the wall itself part of the concealment. The stone was the formation. The formation was the stone. They had been integrated so thoroughly  
that separating them would require destroying both.  
Constructed. Hidden beneath time.  
Mateo slowed — controlled, not hesitant. Each step placed deliberately. Each shift measured. The passage descended at a shallow angle, the floor smooth  
enough to walk on but carrying subtle variations in texture that his feet registered as information rather than inconvenience.  
His shoulder tightened slightly with each step. The wound still present. The alpha's spiritual residue still interfering with full recovery.  
He welcomed it.  
"...Stay there," he murmured to the pain. "...Don't let me get sloppy."  
Good. It kept him anchored. Pain was data. Data was awareness. And awareness, in a space that was testing him, was the only currency that mattered.  
Three steps into the passage proper, he stopped.  
Nothing moved. Nothing attacked. Nothing changed. The stone walls held their silence. The air continued its responsive pulsing. The formation energy  
embedded in the walls maintained its structured patterns.  
And yet —  
"...You're already testing," he said.  
The statement was not addressed to anyone. It was addressed to the space — to the system that he could feel operating around him, the architecture of  
intent that had been woven into every surface and every molecule of air within this passage.  
Mateo let his focus widen. Not searching — absorbing. Allowing information to arrive rather than hunting for it. The technique was the same one he used  
when entering a new zone in the forest — passive reception rather than active scanning, because active scanning revealed what you were looking for while  
passive reception revealed what was actually there.  
What was actually there did not match what appeared to be there.  
"...You don't line up," he said quietly.  
The space didn't align. The passage ahead looked straight — a clean, direct descent into deeper stone, the walls parallel, the floor even, the geometry simple  
and inviting. It was the kind of path that a tired, injured person would follow without question because following it required no effort and no thought.  
Too straight. Too simple. Too convenient.  
"...You're pretending to be simple," he said under his breath.  
He stepped back. Nothing changed. The straight path remained, patient, inviting, offering the path of least resistance with the specific generosity of  
something that wanted compliance rather than awareness.  
"...So it's not the path that responds. It's the observer."  
He rotated slightly. Stepped sideways — not along the path but perpendicular to it, moving into space that the straight path's geometry suggested was wall.  
The air wavered.  
"...There."  
The straight path flickered — vanished for a fraction of a second, then returned. The flicker was brief enough that someone moving forward along the path  
would never have noticed it. It was visible only from an angle that the path itself did not offer — an angle that required the observer to step outside the  
expected trajectory and look at the space from a perspective it had not been designed to accommodate.  
Mateo smiled faintly.  
"...There you are."  
He moved sideways again. The space resisted — not physically, not with force, but with suggestion. A subtle pressure that existed below the threshold of  
conscious perception, gently encouraging him to return to the straight path. To follow the obvious route. To comply.  
"...You prefer compliance," he muttered.  
A quiet suggestion pressed against his awareness — not words, not images, but the energetic equivalent of a hand on his shoulder, guiding him forward.  
This way is easier.  
Mateo let it settle. Let the suggestion register fully, acknowledging its presence without accepting its premise.  
"...Of course it is," he said softly. "That's the point."  
Then ignored it completely.  
The distortion grew clearer as he continued moving laterally. The straight path was not a path at all — it was a projection. An illusion maintained by the  
formation work in the walls, designed to present a false geometry that directed visitors along a predetermined route. The real passage existed beside it —  
offset, misaligned, hidden not behind concealment but behind assumption.  
"...Not hidden," he said. "...Misaligned. You're hiding the real entry behind the expectation that the obvious entry is the only one."  
No response. The space did not confirm or deny. It simply existed, maintaining its illusion for anyone still looking at it from the expected angle.  
"...Of course not."  
He stepped fully into the misaligned space.  
The world shifted.  
Not violently. Not dramatically. But completely. The straight path — the one that had been so inviting, so obvious, so designed to be followed — unraveled.  
Not broken. Replaced. The geometry reorganized around Mateo's new position, the walls adjusting, the floor settling into a different angle, the entire space  
reconfiguring itself to accommodate the observer who had chosen correctly.  
"...Correction," Mateo said quietly. "The path doesn't test navigation. It tests perception. The straight route was the wrong answer presented as the right one.  
The misaligned space was the right answer presented as nothing at all."  
"...Better."  
Behind him, the entrance vanished. The space where he had entered simply ceased to connect to the space he now occupied — not sealed, not collapsed, but  
disconnected. The passage behind him still existed. He simply could no longer reach it from here.  
Mateo didn't turn.  
"...No return," he noted calmly. "...Progression confirmed."  
The path descended. Steeper now. The air grew denser — not with pressure, but with complexity. Layers of energy moved through the stone around him in  
patterns that his vein-detection awareness could feel but not yet map. Currents. Structures. The architecture of something that had been built with purpose  

and maintained with patience.  
"...Not environment," he murmured. "...Behavior."  
The space was not a location. It was a system. And systems, as Mateo had understood since long before he had the vocabulary to express it, could be read.  
He crouched.  
"...Let's see what you're actually doing."  
The floor wasn't flat — or rather, it appeared flat but carried variations that his palms detected when he hovered them just above the surface. Pressure  
differentials. Subtle zones where the energy density increased or decreased in patterns too regular to be geological.  
"...Don't touch," he told himself, his hand hovering a centimeter above the stone.  
The space shifted faintly beneath his palm — responding to the proximity of his awareness the way the forest floor had responded to his footsteps. Not to his  
weight. To his attention.  
"...Pressure lines."  
Invisible threads of structured energy, woven through the floor in a grid that was not visible to the eye but that was perceptible to awareness that operated at  
the level where energy and structure intersected.  
"A test of interaction," he said. "...Not resistance. The pressure lines aren't obstacles. They're sensors. The floor is reading how I engage with it — whether I  
step on the lines, between them, or through them. Each response produces a different result."  
He stood and stepped forward — not onto the ground as it appeared, but between the pressure lines, placing his foot in the gaps where the energy density  
was lowest. His weight settled within a gap — a space approximately the width of his foot that existed between two invisible threads.  
"...Correct."  
No reaction. The space accepted his step without resistance, without alarm, without the subtle pressure that had accompanied the straight-path illusion.  
He continued. Step by step. Each one placed with the deliberate precision of someone walking through a minefield made of intent rather than explosives.  
"...So brute force fails," he said. "Walk across the pressure lines and the space responds — probably by redirecting you back to the false path, or by sealing  
the passage, or by producing consequences that scale with the degree of violation."  
Step by step.  
"...Awareness passes. The space permits what it recognizes as comprehension."  
"...You're testing awareness."  
The space pulsed. Or didn't. The distinction between the two was so fine that certainty was not possible — only the sense that something had acknowledged  
his observation without confirming it.  
He smiled slightly.  
"...Good."  
Time sharpened around him as he moved deeper. Not faster — more precise. Each moment carrying more information than the one before it, the space  
increasing its resolution the way a lens increased magnification, revealing details that had been present all along but that only became visible at this depth of  
engagement.  
"...You're increasing resolution," he said. "The deeper I go, the more you reveal. Not because you're opening up — because I'm learning to see."  
Each moment clearer. More precise. The formation work in the walls becoming readable. The energy patterns in the floor becoming navigable. The system's  
logic becoming comprehensible — not fully, not completely, but enough to continue.  
He reached the end of the passage.  
The space opened.  
Three paths.  
"...Finally," he murmured.  
They were different. Unaligned. Uneven. Each one leading in a different direction, each one carrying a different energy signature, each one presenting a  
different proposition to the person standing before them.  
Mateo stood still, studying them. His awareness extended into each one — not deeply, not intrusively, but enough to read the surface characteristics that  
each path offered.  
"...Obvious misdirection."  
Left path: stable. Too stable. The energy was even, the air calm, the descent gentle. It felt comfortable — inviting in the way that the straight path above had  
been inviting. A space designed to be chosen by someone who wanted ease.  
"...Too comfortable."  
Center path: direct. Too direct. The descent was steep, the energy concentrated, the atmosphere carrying the specific weight of a space that promised rapid  
progress. It felt efficient — appealing to someone who valued speed over caution.  
"...Too convenient."  
Right path — wrong.  
The energy was uneven. The air carried contradictions — warm in places, cold in others, the ambient pressure fluctuating without pattern. The descent was  
irregular, the walls narrowing and widening unpredictably. Everything about it suggested instability, unreliability, the kind of space that sensible people  
avoided.  
He focused on it.  
"...There it is."  
Not dangerous. Not unstable. Not any of the things its presentation suggested.  
Incorrect on purpose.  
The right path had been designed to feel wrong — to trigger the instinct that said avoid this, choose something else, go where it's safe. It was the labyrinth's  
final filter — the test that separated people who trusted their comfort from people who trusted their comprehension.  
The left path was a trap dressed as safety. The center path was a trap dressed as efficiency. The right path was the answer dressed as a mistake.  
Intentional.  
Mateo stepped forward.  
"...Then we proceed correctly."  
He turned right.  
The other paths vanished — dissolving into the stone walls as though they had never existed, their illusions collapsing the moment a selection was made. The  
labyrinth did not maintain options that had been rejected. It committed to the choice as fully as the chooser did.  
"...Selection confirmed."  

The air shifted deeper. Colder. More complex. The formation energy in the walls intensified — not threatening, but attentive. The system was no longer  
testing whether Mateo could perceive. It was observing how he proceeded.  
"...Now you're paying attention."  
The structure responded. Not to his presence — it had been responding to his presence since he entered. To his choice. The act of selecting the correct path  
had changed his status within the system from visitor to participant, and the labyrinth was adjusting its behavior accordingly.  
And somewhere deeper — far below the passage he now walked, in spaces that his awareness could sense but not yet reach — something noticed.  
Not the formation. Not the tests. Not the architecture that had been built to filter and evaluate.  
Something else.  
Something that the labyrinth had been built around.  
Or built to contain.  
Mateo continued walking.  
"...You're watching now," he said softly.  
Unhurried. Aware. Not guided. Not blocked.  
Observed.  
"...Good."  
And for the first time, the labyrinth did not feel like something to solve.  
It felt like something waiting to see how he would.  
Mateo exhaled once.  
"...Then watch."  
He kept walking.  
And deeper within — the labyrinth began to respond.
