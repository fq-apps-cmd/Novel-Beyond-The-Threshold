Mateo didn't stop running immediately.
He slowed.
Then forced himself to slow further.
The forest stretched around him in shadows and broken terrain, his steps no longer clean but controlled through strain rather than efficiency. Each landing sent a dull echo of pain through his side, the deeper cut along his ribs pulling with every shift of his body. His shoulder throbbed from the bite, muscles tightening reflexively, refusing to move as cleanly as they had moments before.

	Too much damage to keep moving like that.

He exhaled slowly, forcing his breathing into rhythm rather than letting it fracture under pressure. His eyes flicked across the terrain—roots, stone, shadow, elevation, scent.
The wolves were still there.
Not in sight.
But not far.
The alpha would not abandon this lightly.
"…So I need to stop first," he muttered quietly.
He angled downhill, feet adjusting against loose rock until the ground shifted from forest floor to something older—compressed stone, partially exposed, half-buried by time. The terrain narrowed slightly, forcing denser growth along the sides while opening just enough space for something larger than a simple animal trail.
Then he saw it.
A break in the rock.
Not obvious.
But wrong.
The entrance wasn't a clean cave opening but a distorted section of stone, partially collapsed inward, as if something had once forced its way through and the world had only partially corrected the damage.
Mateo didn't hesitate.
He stepped inside.

The air changed immediately.
Cooler.
Still.
The scent of damp earth gave way to something quieter—stone, age, and silence that wasn't empty but contained.
The sound of the forest dimmed behind him.

	Good.

Mateo leaned back against the inner wall and allowed himself to drop into a seated position, one leg extended, the other bent just enough to stabilize his center. His breathing slowed again as he brought his focus inward, away from external threat and into something more immediate.
Pain mapping.
The wound along his ribs was shallow—but long enough to bleed consistently. The shoulder bite was worse. Teeth had torn muscle, not completely, but enough to disrupt strength and movement.
"…That's annoying."
He closed his eyes.
Then reached inward.

The cube.

His hand moved beneath his clothing, fingers brushing the small stabilized object. The moment he opened it, the internal space unfolded—quiet, contained, perfectly separated from the interference that had plagued the herbs earlier.
"…Good."
He didn't rush.
That would be a mistake.
Instead, he laid the herbs out carefully on a flat section of stone before him, observing—not just their form, but their reactions to the space, to him, to each other.
Even separated, they shifted subtly.
Not aggressively.
But not entirely passive either.
Still interacting. Just less directly.

Mateo exhaled once.
"…Let's see what actually works."

He reached first for the Lifebloom Verdant Core.
The moment his fingers brushed it, the soft golden motes surrounding it responded, drifting upward slightly before settling again. The energy it held was steady—not aggressive, not reactive—just… present.
Healing.
Safe.
Mostly.

Next—Azure Lung Moss.
The faint, moisture-rich aura it carried aligned immediately with the Verdant Core, the interaction subtle but clean.

	Good pairing.

Then—
He paused.

The Emberveil Bloom pulsed faintly.
Not violently.
But enough.
The floating embers shifted slightly toward the other herbs, not in aggression, but as if probing space.
Mateo narrowed his eyes.
"…No."

He set it aside.
Farther.
Separately.

"Later."

This was not the time for risky enhancement.
This was survival.

He reached for the Starvein Pulse Vine next, fingers hovering just above it before making contact. The faint rhythm within it synced slightly with his touch, its internal motion aligning just enough to feel… cooperative.
"…You'll help."

Mateo moved slowly now, not assembling anything formal, not crafting a perfect pill, but building something simpler—something functional.

He crushed the Lifebloom Verdant Core between his fingers.

Not violently.
Carefully.

The soft light within it spread across his hand, golden motes breaking apart into something usable. He mixed it with strands of Azure Lung Moss, pressing them together until the textures merged, moisture forming naturally as the moss released its stored properties.

Then—
A small piece of Starvein Pulse Vine.

Not much.
Just enough.

The effect was immediate.
The mixture shifted, stabilizing more fully, the conflicting tendencies smoothing into something closer to balance.

	Bridge established.

Mateo nodded slightly.
"…That's enough."

He didn't refine it into a pill.
Didn't need to.

Instead, he pressed the mixture directly along the wound at his ribs.

Pain flared immediately.

Not sharp—
Deep.

His muscles tensed instinctively, rejecting the intrusion for half a breath before the Verdant aspect took hold. The warmth spread outward, not aggressively but steadily, knitting surface tissue first, then slowing as it reached deeper layers.
The Azure component carried it.
Evenly.
Smoothly.
The Starvein influence—
That was different.

His breathing hitched once.

The energy didn't just heal.
It aligned.

Minor distortions in his internal flow corrected as the mixture settled, forcing his body to accept structure where pain had disrupted it. Not perfect.
Not even close.
But better.

His shoulder—
That required more.

Mateo studied it for a moment, blood still running in thin lines along his arm.
"…That one's annoying."

He adjusted his approach.
This time, adding a trace—

A tiny fragment of Frostglass Heart Lotus.

Cooling.
Stabilizing.

The mixture changed again.
Less aggressive.
More controlled.

He applied it more carefully along the bite.

The effect was immediate.
The heat from the injury dropped, pressure easing as the Frost element balanced the Verdant regrowth. The muscle still resisted, still damaged—but the progression slowed correctly instead of forcing unnatural repair.

Mateo leaned back.
"…Better."

Not fixed.
Not healed.

Functional.

He sat there for several breaths, letting his body adjust. The internal changes were subtle, but present—his circulation had stabilized, his breathing no longer fought against damage, and the sharp edge of pain had dulled into something manageable.

	Good enough.

Only then did he look deeper into the cave.

It wasn't natural.
That much was obvious now.
The outer portion had been, perhaps—but the deeper stretch carried intention. The walls shifted from rough stone into a more structured irregularity, patterns that almost resembled erosion until observed just long enough to realize they were not random.

Form.

Hidden.

Mateo stood slowly, testing his weight.
His side pulled.
His shoulder resisted.

"…Still usable."

He stepped forward.
Carefully.

The air grew heavier the deeper he went. Not physically oppressive, but dense with something old—something that had remained untouched for a long time.
Dust lay along sections of the floor, but not evenly.
Some areas—
Disturbed.

Mateo crouched slightly, fingers brushing across one section of ground.
"…Recent."

Not by beasts.
Not by natural movement.

Something else.

He stood again, continuing forward until the back wall of the cave came into full view.
At first glance—
Nothing.

Then—
Structure.

A section of stone slightly misaligned, its surface carrying unnatural breaks, edges too clean beneath layered wear. It wasn't obvious unless you were looking for it.
Mateo reached out.
His fingers traced along the cracks, following lines that didn't behave like natural fracture. Where others would have dismissed it as age, he saw intention.

"…You were hidden."

His touch paused.

Then shifted slightly.

There—

A fragment embedded deeper than the rest.

A talisman.

Old.
Worn.
But intact.

Mateo studied it briefly, eyes narrowing as he felt the structure behind it—not the symbol itself, but what it connected to.
"…Not decoration."

He pulled it free.

Nothing happened.

Then—
Everything did.

The wall shifted—not loudly, not dramatically, but with quiet certainty. Stone moved where it should not have, revealing a narrow opening where none had existed.
The air that came from within was colder.
Older.

Mateo stared.
"…That's inconvenient."

He should rest.
Recover more.
Prepare.

Instead—
He stepped forward.

Because if the world was already changing around him—
Then waiting would not stop it.

And somewhere beyond that newly opened path—
Something was already waiting in return.
