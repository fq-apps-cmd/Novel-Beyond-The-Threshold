The cave breathed quietly around him.
Mateo stood just beyond the opened passage, the older air brushing faintly against his senses, carrying with it the weight of something hidden and waiting. His side still ached, his shoulder tightened with each subtle shift, and yet his attention lingered not on the pain—but on the space ahead.
"…He would like this," Mateo muttered.
The thought came easily.
Too easily.
Tomas would have stepped in without hesitation, boots striking the ground just to test it, forcing whatever was hidden here to reveal itself. He would already be thinking about how to break it, how to push it, how to prove something against it.
Probably the wrong part first.
Mateo's mouth twitched faintly.
"…Then complain about it afterward."
The image lingered—and with it came something heavier.
A memory.

The training ground felt too quiet. Not empty—just… wrong. Tomas stood at its center, feet planted firmly against packed earth, shoulders squared as he drew a slow breath through his nose. Dust settled lightly around him, disturbed moments earlier by movements that never quite felt right anymore.
"…Again," he muttered.
The ground responded. A sharp stomp sent earth surging upward beneath him, rising in jagged segments before wrapping along his legs. The stone followed his motion as his fingers curled, pulling invisible structure inward. The earth obeyed—but late.
Then he moved.
The first time—too heavy.
The second—too forced.
The third—
He slipped slightly.

Another memory layered over it.

Younger.
Smaller.
Tomas stood near the edge of a shallow quarry outside the village, watching men drive tools into stone. His father worked among them, not fast—but consistent. Each strike measured. Each movement controlled.
"Watch the ground," his father had said without turning. "It tells you where it will break before it breaks."
Tomas frowned. "It's just rock."
His father paused, then tapped the stone lightly with the end of his tool.
A crack formed exactly where he struck.
"…It's never just rock."

Another shift.

A night.
Dim light. A cramped space.
His mother adjusting old training bindings along his arms, tightening them carefully.
"You don't have to prove anything to us," she said softly.
Tomas didn't answer.
"We're proud of you already."
"I know," he replied.
But his voice said something else.

The memory fractured—returning him to the training ground.

"…Still wrong," Tomas muttered.
A voice behind him responded calmly. "Your timing is off."
Mateo stepped forward.
He had seen this before.
Not just the technique.
The pattern.

Tomas wasn't wrong.
He was disconnected.

Mateo watched as Tomas stomped again, drawing the earth upward in layered motion. Heavy. Solid. Impressive.
And completely inefficient.
The flow of energy fractured at every transition. The ground responded, but not with him—after him.
Mateo stepped aside.
Tapped once.
Everything collapsed.

It continued.
Again.
Again.
Again.

Each failure wasn't random.
It was consistent.

	Too much force.
	Not enough listening.

Mateo didn't say that.
Not yet.

Eventually—
"…You're holding back."
That part always came.

They argued.
They reset.

Tomas crouched again, forming his defensive shell, compressing stone inward until it formed a tight structure around him.
Mateo stared at it.
"…You turned yourself into a rock."
"…That's not the point."

Mateo walked forward.
Knocked.
Listened.
Tapped points along it.
"…Here. And here. And there."

He didn't break it with strength.
He broke it with understanding.

The structure gave way.

Tomas rolled out and lay there.
"…That's cheating."
Mateo snorted lightly.

Then—
The part Mateo remembered more clearly than he expected.

"…I don't get it."
Tomas' voice had changed.
Less anger.
More… weight.

Mateo stayed quiet.
Tomas pushed himself up, pulling his knees slightly inward, staring at his hands as if they should already know how to move properly.
"I train every day. I don't skip. I don't slack. I fix what I can. I repeat until it works. And it still… doesn't."
Mateo watched him.
Really watched him.

Fragments aligned in his mind.
The way Tomas' stance leaned too far forward under pressure.
The way his energy surged instead of flowed.
The way he compensated instead of corrected.

	Not weak.
	Not wrong.
	Misaligned.

Tomas continued quietly. "…My family is depending on me."
That part felt heavier this time.
Mateo tilted his head slightly. "Explain."
Tomas hesitated.
Then continued anyway.
"They're not fighters. Not really. My father and uncles—they work the mountain veins outside the village. Stone extraction. Mineral work. Tools. Materials. They don't fight—they endure."
Mateo didn't interrupt.
"They've put everything into this path. Time. Money. What they have." Tomas looked down again. "They say it's for me. That I should grow however I can. That they're proud already."
A pause.
"…But that's not enough."
His fingers curled slowly.
"I want to prove it meant something. That I didn't just take everything they gave me and… stop here."
Another pause.
"…I want to become someone they can look at and know it was worth it."

Mateo didn't answer immediately.
Because for a moment—
He understood.
Not fully.
But enough.

So instead, he crouched down awkwardly beside him, hugging his knees and tracing circles into the dirt like someone who had somehow failed a very simple problem.
"…I don't get it either," Mateo said.
Tomas blinked. "What?"
"I train. I fix things. I repeat them until they work. And sometimes…" He drew another circle. "…it feels like it should be easier than it is."
Tomas stared at him for a moment.
"…You're messing with me."
Mateo shook his head slightly. "Not this time."

Silence stretched.

And Mateo watched something else.
Not Tomas' frustration.
Not his strength.

His structure.

The way his body wanted to move.
But didn't.

	Too tight here… drifting there…

The thought came naturally.
Without effort.
Without permission.

Mateo blinked.

That wasn't new.
But it was clearer this time.

"…You're close," he said.
Tomas didn't believe him.
Mateo didn't correct him.

Far away, the mission board began gathering a crowd. Diego leaned forward, squinting as if reading something incredibly complicated.
"…We can get rewards," he muttered.

Back on the training ground—
Tomas stomped again.

This time—
The earth didn't resist as harshly.

Not clean.
Not correct.
But closer.

Mateo didn't acknowledge it aloud.
But internally—

	Good.

That night—
The real work would begin.
