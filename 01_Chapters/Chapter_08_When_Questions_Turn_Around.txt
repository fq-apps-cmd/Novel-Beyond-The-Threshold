"You let him walk out."
The statement carried irritation, but none of the elders chose to answer it directly. Instead, they sat again in a loose circle, the low table between them unchanged, but the arrangement around it no longer the same. The windows had been shifted, seating adjusted, angles reconsidered.
Subtle corrections.
But deliberate.
The flaw he had pointed out had already been addressed.
That, perhaps, was the most irritating part.

"…The issue is not that he came in," one elder said slowly. "The issue is how he behaved once he did."
"He behaved as if he belonged there," another replied.
"…Which he did not."
"…And yet none of us stopped him."
Silence followed.

At the far side of the room, an older man sat quietly with his arms folded, his posture relaxed but solid, like something that had endured more than the others without needing to prove it. His presence did not dominate the space, yet it carried weight all the same.
Mateo's great-grandfather.

"…You're all focusing on the wrong thing," he said at last.
The room turned toward him.

"The boy didn't force his way in," he continued. "He walked through an open door, spoke clearly, and exposed something we overlooked."
"…You're defending him?" one elder asked.
The old man shook his head slightly. "I'm reminding you to think."

He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees.
"Do you know what happens when someone steps into Core Formation?"
That shifted the conversation.
"…They stabilize their foundation," one elder said.
"They begin refining their core."
"They solidify their path."
The old man nodded.
"…And at Peak Core Formation?"
Silence lingered longer.
"…Pressure," another elder said finally. "…Their presence alone starts affecting the environment."
The old man nodded again.

"Every realm has ten layers," he said. "From zero—the moment you step into it—to nine, where you reach its limit. Most never move beyond the middle layers. Fewer still reach the peak."
His gaze drifted briefly toward the doorway, as if measuring something unseen.
"And when someone reaches too high… too fast… what happens?"
"…They ascend," someone answered.
"Or are forced to," another added quietly.
The old man leaned back.
"…Exactly."

A pause settled.
"…The higher the realm," he continued, "the less time you are allowed to remain in it. Even in higher worlds, the strongest rarely last beyond a few thousand years—two, maybe five, depending on their nature and the methods they use to extend it."
The elders listened more carefully now.
"…Because the stronger you become," he said quietly, "…the less the world can tolerate you."
Another silence followed.

"…And you think that applies here?" one elder asked cautiously.
The old man's expression didn't change much, but something faint shifted beneath it.
"I think the boy does not behave like someone who fears limits."

Later that day, Mateo was summoned.
Not urgently.
Not forcefully.
But formally.

"…You're going," Tomas said, watching him.
"…Yes."
"…You're not concerned."
Mateo considered briefly.
"…Should I be?"
"…Yes."
Mateo nodded once.
"…I'll keep that in mind."
Diego leaned in immediately. "…He's not going to keep it in mind."
"…That is correct," Mateo said.

The hall felt different this time.
More controlled.
More intentional.
The air was quieter, the layout adjusted, even the way light fell across the table slightly altered.
Mateo noticed immediately.
	They adjusted.
	Good.

He stepped inside without hesitation. This time, the door closed behind him.

One of the elders leaned forward.
"You entered this room without permission yesterday," he said. "Explain."
Mateo stood calmly.
"…Are you asking for an explanation," he said, "or for justification?"
A pause followed.
"…Both."
Mateo nodded.
"…The door was open."
"…That is not sufficient."
Mateo tilted his head.
"…Then the door should not have been open."
A flicker of irritation passed through the room.
"…You are deflecting."
"…No," Mateo replied. "…I am identifying the first failure."
A few elders shifted.
"…And the second?"
Mateo's gaze moved across them.
"…You noticed me too late."

Silence.

"…You are avoiding responsibility," one elder said sharply.
Mateo paused, then asked,
"…What responsibility did I violate?"
"…You—"
The elder stopped.

Mateo continued, tone unchanged.
"…If the rule is 'no entry,' then the responsibility to enforce it lies with those who created it. If it is not enforced…"
A brief pause.
"…then is it truly a rule?"

This time, the silence changed.
It was no longer irritation.
It was thought.

"…Enough."
The main elder spoke again, firmer now.
"You disrupted a meeting, challenged authority, and caused confusion."
Mateo nodded once.
"…Correct."
"…Why?"

A brief pause followed.

Mateo looked at them.
"…Do you want the effective answer," he asked calmly, "…or the honest one?"

Tomas, at the edge of the room, closed his eyes briefly.
	…This is going to go badly.

"…Both," the elder said.

Mateo considered.
Then answered.
"Effective: your meeting structure had weaknesses. Those weaknesses are now corrected."
A pause.
"Honest: I wanted to see what would happen."

Another pause.

"…And?" the elder pressed.

Mateo tilted his head.
"…It was interesting."

A few elders suppressed amusement.
Others did not.

One elder leaned back slightly.
"…You turned our questioning into your own," he said. "Was that intentional?"
Mateo considered.
Then nodded.
"…Yes."
"…Why?"
Mateo glanced around the room.
"…You were already uncertain," he said. "I asked questions that aligned with that."
"…You manipulated the situation."
"…I clarified it."

That drew a stronger reaction.

Before it escalated—
"…He's not wrong."

The voice came from the side.
Mateo's great-grandfather.
He lifted one hand slightly.
Thumb raised.

The room paused.

An elder exhaled sharply.
"…You're encouraging him."
The old man shrugged.
"…I'm acknowledging efficiency."

Mateo glanced at him briefly.
	…Useful.

As the meeting began to settle—
Mateo turned toward the door.
Then paused.

	…One more test.

"…Before I leave," he said calmly, "there is something else."
The elders looked back.

"Your seating formation is improved," Mateo said, "but still incomplete."
"…Explain."
Mateo gestured slightly.
"You've corrected sound direction, but not visual exposure."
Several elders turned instinctively.
"…If someone stands at that angle outside," he continued, "…movement inside the room is still visible."

Tomas stiffened.
	…He's doing it again.

One elder rose immediately and checked.
"…He's right."
Another shook his head.
"…Why do we keep listening to him?"

Mateo paused at the doorway.
Then said calmly,
"…Because I am correct."

And left.

Outside, the air felt less structured again—sound returning, the distant noise of the village drifting back into place.
Tomas followed immediately.
"…You just did it again."
"…Yes."
"…Why?"
Mateo paused.
Then straightened slightly.
"…Systems must be tested continuously," he said. "If they are not, they degrade."

Tomas listened.
For a moment—it made sense again.

Then Mateo added,
"…Also, I was still curious."
"…About what?"
Mateo resumed walking.
"…How many times it would work."

Diego burst out laughing.
"…You're unbelievable."

Tomas remained still for a moment.
	He speaks like someone who understands everything…
	…then acts like none of it matters.

He shook his head and followed.
"…You're not normal."
Mateo didn't look back.
"…That has never been the goal."

That night, Mateo stood beneath the open sky again, the forest stretching quietly beyond the edges of the village.
The pendant pulsed.
Stronger now.

	Something is aligning faster.

He closed his hand around it.

	Fragments… returning.

For a brief moment—
He felt something deeper.

Not this world.
Not this life.
Something older.
Waiting.

Mateo exhaled slowly.
"…Good."

Because for the first time—

The mystery wasn't distant.

It was beginning to respond.
