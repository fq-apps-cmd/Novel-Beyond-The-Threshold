"You adjusted again."
The statement was delivered evenly, without accusation, yet it carried an unmistakable certainty. Mateo did not look up immediately. He remained in position, his weight slightly forward as he completed the motion he had been practicing, letting the stance settle before easing out of it.
Only then did he lift his gaze.
"…Is that a problem?"
Inés stood a short distance away with her arms folded, her attention fixed on him in a way that most others had learned not to maintain for long. She did not look intimidated, nor curious—only focused.
"You correct your stance in the middle of the movement, then pull back before finishing it," she replied. "You've done it every time."
Mateo exhaled quietly, his expression unchanged.
	…She's consistent. That's unfortunate.
"You're imagining things."
"No," she said calmly. "I'm not."
Before the conversation could continue, another voice interrupted them.
"Then show her properly."
Both of them turned.
Tomas stood at the edge of the yard, his posture straight, his wooden saber resting along his shoulder. His expression was neither aggressive nor casual—it was measured, like someone who had already reached a conclusion and was now waiting for confirmation.
Mateo watched him for a moment.
"…And you are?"
"Tomas Luján."
Diego, who had somehow appeared without Mateo noticing, leaned in just enough to whisper, "He trains with people from outside the village."
Mateo glanced at him briefly.
"…That explains the confidence."
Tomas ignored the quiet exchange and stepped forward, closing the distance between them with steady steps. His gaze did not waver.
"I watched your movements," he said. "They were wrong."
A short pause followed.
Mateo tilted his head slightly.
"…And yet you want to fight me."
"Yes."
	…Consistent logic. I can respect that.
"Why?" Mateo asked.
The question seemed to catch Tomas off guard. For a brief moment, he simply stared, as though the answer was obvious enough not to require explanation.
"…To improve."
Mateo nodded faintly.
"…Then improve."
Tomas' eyes narrowed slightly.
"You're avoiding the point."
	Yes.
"…Fight me," Tomas repeated, this time more firmly.
Mateo closed his eyes briefly, as if weighing whether this interaction was worth continuing.
	If I refuse, it escalates. If I accept, it accelerates.
After a moment, he sighed.
"…Fine."

They stepped into the open space without ceremony. There was no audience called, no signal given. Yet word had already begun to spread, and others gathered quietly around them.
Tomas lowered his saber into a ready stance, his posture grounded and stable. There was no excess tension in his shoulders, no unnecessary movement—everything was controlled.
Mateo noticed immediately.
	Better foundation than most here.
"You're not using a weapon?" Tomas asked.
Mateo shook his head.
"…Not necessary."
There was a flicker of something in Tomas' expression—something sharper than irritation.
"…We'll see."
He moved first.
The strike was clean, fast, and direct. It came without warning, cutting straight toward Mateo's centerline. There was no wasted motion.
Mateo shifted to the side with minimal effort, the blade passing close enough to feel but not close enough to connect.
	Efficient. But still too direct.
Tomas adjusted immediately, pivoting into a second strike. This one carried more intent, more pressure.
Mateo redirected it with a subtle movement of his arm, altering the angle just enough to make the strike ineffective.
"Is that all?" Tomas pressed.
Mateo said nothing.
	No need.
The exchange continued, each movement building on the last. Tomas grew faster with each step, pushing his own pace, testing limits. But Mateo remained just beyond reach—not retreating, not yielding, simply… adjusting.
At first, it looked like avoidance.
Then it became something else.
"…You're not moving away," Tomas said suddenly.
"…No," Mateo replied.
	I'm moving around you.
The next exchange was shorter.
Tomas stepped in with another strike, slightly overextending.
Mateo reacted instantly.
He stepped forward—not away—and guided the motion of the saber just enough to throw off its alignment. The imbalance was small, but enough.
He tapped Tomas lightly on the shoulder.
"…You're open."
Silence fell across the yard.
Tomas stared at him for a moment, then stepped back slowly.
"…Again."
Mateo blinked.
	…Persistent.
"…Fine."

They repeated the exchange.
Again.
And again.
Each time, Tomas adjusted, refining his movements, correcting small mistakes. His posture improved, his strikes sharpened, his control deepened.
And each time, Mateo remained just ahead.
Not dominating.
Not overwhelming.
Just… slightly out of reach.
By the fifth exchange, the atmosphere had changed.
Diego was no longer smiling.
Inés had gone completely silent.
Even Rafael, watching from further back, had narrowed his gaze.
"…Something's off," Rafael muttered quietly.
Lucía, standing beside him, didn't respond at first. Her attention was fixed not on the strikes or the movements—but on Mateo himself.
"…Yes," she said softly. "…It is."

The sixth exchange began.
Tomas stepped in faster this time, pushing harder, compressing his movement into a tighter arc. The saber came closer than before.
Mateo moved to counter—
And then something shifted.
Just for a moment.
The warmth at his chest returned.
	…Again.
The pendant pulsed faintly beneath his clothes, unseen by anyone else. At the same time, something surfaced within him—not a memory exactly, but something near it.
Fragments.
Broken pieces of something vast.
His body moved.
Not consciously.
Not entirely.
The motion was different.
Older.
Refined in a way that did not belong to this place.
The strike was deflected completely.
Not partially.
Not redirected.
Erased.
Tomas' balance broke instantly.
He staggered back, catching himself with visible effort.
Mateo had already stopped moving.
Silence settled over the yard.
"…That wasn't the same," Tomas said quietly.
Mateo looked at his hand.
	No… it wasn't.
"…Forget it," he replied.
But Tomas didn't look convinced.

Later that evening, Mateo sat inside the house, surrounded by bundles of herbs Lucía had gathered earlier that day. The faint scent of dried leaves and roots filled the air as he examined them carefully.
	Last time was unstable.
He picked up a small piece, turning it slightly as he observed its structure.
	Balance was off. Reaction too fast.
He began again.
Slowly.
Carefully.
Adjusting proportions.
Controlling the process rather than forcing it.
Lucía watched from a distance this time, saying nothing. Her gaze remained fixed on his movements, her expression more focused than before.
The mixture changed gradually.
Not violently, like last time.
Not chaotically.
But steadily.
A faint glow emerged—barely visible, but present.
Mateo narrowed his eyes slightly.
	…There.
The process completed without disruption.
No smoke.
No explosion.
Just a small, faintly glowing liquid resting in a simple container.
Lucía approached slowly.
"…You succeeded."
Mateo studied it silently.
	Barely. But correctly.
"…It works."
Lucía crouched beside him, her gaze shifting between the mixture and his face.
"…You're not experimenting randomly," she said quietly. "…You understand what you're doing."
Mateo didn't respond.
	I always have.
"…Where did you learn this?"
A brief pause followed.
"…I watched you."
Lucía held his gaze for a moment longer, then exhaled softly.
"…You're going to cause trouble."
Mateo looked back down at the mixture.
	That seems unavoidable.
"…Probably."

That night, the village had fallen quiet.
Mateo stepped outside, the cool air settling around him as he looked upward toward the sky. The stars stretched endlessly above, distant and unmoving—simple in appearance, but layered with something he couldn't quite grasp.
The pendant rested in his hand again.
For the first time, it felt… active.
He turned it slowly, watching the fractured star pattern reflect faint light.
	Fragments.
The thought lingered longer this time.
	Something broke.
A faint sensation stirred within him—not memory, not recognition, but something close to both.
	Something fell.
His gaze lifted toward the sky.
The feeling deepened slightly.
	…From above.
Mateo frowned.
"…That doesn't make sense."
And yet—
He couldn't dismiss it.
Something about the name. The pendant. The reactions of others.
None of it fit together.
But none of it felt random either.
He exhaled slowly, closing his eyes.
	There's something here I'm not seeing yet.
A pause.
	That's fine. I'll figure it out.
He always did.
