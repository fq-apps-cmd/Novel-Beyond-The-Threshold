The alpha did not hesitate. It moved first—not with wild aggression, but with certainty. Its body lowered slightly, muscles coiling beneath metallic fur that caught fractured sunlight in muted flashes, and the space between it and Mateo closed in an instant.
Mateo stepped, not backward, but forward and to the side. Its jaws snapped through empty air where his shoulder had been, the force of the missed bite sending a gust past him strong enough to ripple the pond’s surface behind him. His right foot slid across stone, toes gripping just enough to hold friction, his ankle adjusting before imbalance could form.
Fast.
Not fast enough.
Mateo’s thoughts remained steady. He had long since reached the ninth level of the Body Tempering Realm, the final stage of the upper tier. His muscles, bones, blood, and organs had all been tempered far beyond what most in the village could achieve. Yet everyone around him still believed he stood somewhere in the middle levels—around the fourth or fifth at best.
That misunderstanding had always been useful.
Now, it didn’t matter.
A second wolf came from the flank, lower and quicker, not aiming to kill but to disrupt. Mateo dropped his center immediately. His left hand intercepted just beneath the jaw, pushing it off line by a fraction. Teeth scraped across his sleeve. His right foot planted hard—force rising cleanly through calf, knee, hip, core—before releasing through a short, brutal strike into the beast’s shoulder.
The wolf staggered sideways.
Water erupted as the alpha followed.
“…You don’t like warning shots, do you?” Mateo said quietly.
The response came immediately.
A claw cut toward his ribs.
He slipped—almost perfectly.
Not enough.
The edge caught him.
Pain flared.
He stepped through it instead of retreating.
The pack spread wider.
No longer probing.
Hunting.
Mateo moved first. He angled outward, forcing them to adjust. One lunged—he dipped under it, palm cutting into the exposed underside. Another snapped—he twisted, elbow striking across its muzzle. A third came blind from behind—
He felt it.
For a moment—
Everything separated.
Movement. Timing. Angle. Continuation.
He chose before the attack completed.
He stepped into it.
The claws missed his centerline. His knee drove upward into the beast’s chest, not with brute force, but with perfect transmission. The impact broke its momentum and sent it rolling.
The alpha slowed.
Adjusted.
“That’s better,” Mateo muttered.
Now the fight changed.
The alpha stopped attacking directly. It guided. Pressured. Cut off space. The other wolves moved in response—not independently—but as extensions of the same decision.
Smart.
Very smart.
Mateo shifted deeper into uneven terrain. Roots twisted close. Footing tightened. One wolf overcommitted—he redirected it into a trunk. Another struck low—he angled off, driving a short strike into its ribs. The pack flowed again.
The alpha came in.
This time, Mateo blocked.
Not fully.
The impact drove through his forearm, forcing his stance to collapse half an inch before he corrected it.
Too dense.
This was not an ordinary beast.
Upper-tier Body Tempering in raw physical cohesion.
Close to the threshold.
Good.
He stepped forward again.
The exchange compressed—motion, breath, impact collapsing into one continuous chain.
Mateo stopped holding back.
Not his realm.
But his completion.
His next strike carried no wasted force. His foot drove into the ground. The force rose without interruption. His spine held perfect alignment. His arm released at the exact last moment.
The wolf it struck lifted from the ground and crashed sideways.
Another came—he moved before it committed.
Predicting.
Not reacting.
The pressure increased.
Then—
Broke.
The alpha struck again.
This time—
Too late.
Claws tore deep into his side.
He hit the ground.
Breath gone.
Pain spread.
The world tightened—
Then steadied.
“…Good,” he said.
He stood again.
Slower.
Now the hunt shifted.

Mateo ran.
Not in retreat.
In control.
He led the terrain.
A fallen trunk—he vaulted and twisted mid-air.
A narrow gap—he cut sharply through it.
Loose stone—he kicked it behind him, forcing disruption.
The wolves followed.
But less smoothly.
“This is the problem with you,” he muttered. “You learn too quickly.”

Something else watched.
An Earthbreaker Boar moved through the underbrush, its massive form layered in mineral-like armor. It paused, snorted once, then shifted away—not fearing the wolves, but unwilling to claim this conflict.
Above, a Veilthread Serpent coiled silently across a branch, observing through stillness. It did not interfere.
Further off, a Farseer Stag stood at the edge of perception, its antlers humming faintly as if calculating patterns instead of reacting to them.
The forest was not chaotic.
It was structured.
Layered.
Hierarchical.

Then—
Mateo misstepped.
Just once.
The wound slowed him.
The delay existed.
The alpha took it.
Its jaws closed around his shoulder.
Pain exploded.
Mateo twisted free.
Blood spread.
Enough.

He stopped running.
Turned.
Faced it.
The forest quieted.
Mateo lowered into stance.
Not lower.
More complete.
His breathing slowed—not to recover, but to align.
The alpha hesitated.
Mateo smiled.
“…There you are.”

The next clash was different.
No wasted testing.
The alpha lunged low—feinting.
Mateo stepped inside the second line before it formed. His forearm rode the jawline. His palm struck under the neck. The wolf twisted.
Claws followed.
Mateo shifted.
Not away.
Through.
His foot rooted. Force rose—not from power, but from continuity.
He found it.
The gap.
The fraction of collapse between decision and motion.
His fist drove forward.
Fully connected.
The strike landed.
Deep.
The alpha staggered.

Around them—
Everything changed.
The serpent released the branch.
The boar stilled.
The stag withdrew.
And beneath them—
Something moved.

Not above.
Not around.
Below.
Deep.
Ancient.
For a brief moment, the forest held a pressure that did not belong to predators, prey, or hierarchy.
It was something else.
Something that did not hunt.
Something that did not chase.
Something that simply was.
Then it settled.
But the memory remained.
The wolves felt it.
The alpha stepped back.
Not in fear.
In recognition.

Mateo exhaled slowly.
This fight had reached its limit.
He could end it.
Probably.
But not here.
Not like this.

So he changed something else.

He closed his eyes.
Not to withdraw.
To see.

For years, he had been at the peak of Body Tempering Realm, Level 9.
That peak—
Had been incomplete.

He felt it now.
Every inconsistency.
Every small inefficiency hidden beneath control.
The delay in his injured shoulder.
The imbalance under uneven terrain.
The slight loss of continuity through the chain of motion.

He corrected it.

Not with force.
Not with breakthrough.
With understanding.

His breathing shifted first.
Then his center.
Then the connection between movement.
Muscle.
Tendon.
Bone.
Blood.
Meridian.

They stopped acting separately.

They aligned.

The change was silent.
No energy burst.
No visible force.

But it was absolute.

His body no longer needed constant correction.
It self-stabilized.
Every movement flowed without contradiction.
Every response completed itself.

The final stage of Body Tempering Realm, Level 9—
Completed.

The alpha felt it.
Stepped back.

Mateo opened his eyes.
“…Much better.”

He did not pursue.
The wolves withdrew.

The forest slowly breathed again.

Mateo stood alone.
Blood on his body.
Calm in his posture.

He felt the next realm.
Qi Gathering Realm.
It was there.
Close.
Reachable.

He did not take it.

A flawed foundation—even at this stage—was unacceptable.
Completion came first.
Transition came later.

“Later,” he said.

He looked deeper into the forest.
Toward what had moved.
Toward what had not yet fully awakened.

This time—
When he stepped forward—
He carried a body that no longer fought itself.

And something deeper in the forest—
Not the wolves.
Not the serpent.
Not the stag—

Noticed.
