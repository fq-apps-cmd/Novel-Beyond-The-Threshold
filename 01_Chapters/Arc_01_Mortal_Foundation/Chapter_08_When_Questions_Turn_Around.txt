“You let him walk out.”
The statement carried irritation, but none of the elders chose to answer it directly. Instead, they sat again in a loose circle, the low table between them unchanged, but the arrangement around it no longer the same. The windows had been shifted, seating adjusted, angles reconsidered. The air itself felt more contained now, as though the room had been instructed to behave more precisely than before.
Subtle corrections.
But deliberate.
The flaw he had pointed out had already been addressed.
That, perhaps, was the most irritating part.

“…The issue is not that he came in,” one elder said slowly. “The issue is how he behaved once he did.”
“He behaved as if he belonged there,” another replied.
“…Which he did not.”
“…And yet none of us stopped him.”
Silence followed.
Not uncertain—just uncomfortable.

“…He didn’t hesitate,” one elder added. “No pause. No adjustment. No indication that he expected resistance.”
“…No deference,” another said.
“…No,” someone else corrected. “…no recognition of hierarchy.”
That phrasing settled worse.
One elder’s fingers tapped once against the table before stilling again.
“…And we allowed it.”

At the far side of the room, an older man sat quietly with his arms folded, his posture relaxed but solid, like something that had endured more than the others without needing to prove it. His presence did not dominate the space, yet it carried weight all the same.
Mateo’s great-grandfather.
He had not spoken before.
He chose now.

“…You’re all focusing on the wrong thing,” he said at last.
The room turned toward him.
“The boy didn’t force his way in,” he continued. “He walked through an open door, spoke clearly, and exposed something we overlooked.”
“…You’re defending him?” one elder asked.
The old man shook his head slightly.
“I’m reminding you to think.”

He leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees.
“Do you know what happens when someone steps into Core Formation?”
That shifted the room immediately.
“…They stabilize their foundation,” one elder said.
“They begin refining their core.”
“They solidify their path.”
The old man nodded.
“…And at Peak Core Formation?”
Silence lingered longer this time.
“…Pressure,” another elder said finally. “…their presence alone starts affecting the environment.”
The old man nodded again.
The faint ambient stillness of the room seemed to settle more tightly as he spoke, as though even the air responded to the subject.

“Every realm has ten layers,” he said. “From zero—the moment you step into it—to nine, where you reach its limit. Most never move beyond the middle layers. Fewer still reach the peak.”
His gaze drifted briefly toward the doorway.
“And when someone reaches too high… too fast… what happens?”
“…They ascend,” someone answered.
“Or are forced to,” another added quietly.
The old man leaned back.
“…Exactly.”

A pause followed.
Still.
Measured.

“…The higher the realm,” he continued, “the less time you are allowed to remain in it. Even in higher worlds, the strongest rarely last beyond a few thousand years—two, maybe five, depending on their nature and the methods they use to extend it.”
The elders listened more carefully now.
“…Because the stronger you become,” he said quietly, “…the less the world can tolerate you.”
The words lingered longer than expected.

“…And you think that applies here?” one elder asked cautiously.
The old man’s expression didn’t change much, but something beneath it shifted—slightly more focused now.
“I think the boy does not behave like someone who fears limits.”
“…He’s nowhere near that level,” another elder said.
“…No,” the old man agreed.
A pause.
“…But the way he evaluates things…”
He let that trail.
It was enough.

“…Then what do we do?” someone pressed.
“Restrict him,” one said immediately.
“Observe him,” another countered.
“Correct him early.”
“Control him before this spreads.”
That last one carried weight.

The old man’s gaze moved slightly.
“…Control requires understanding.”
“…Then we enforce first,” another replied.
“…And if enforcement fails again?”
No one answered immediately.
Because now—
that scenario felt possible.

A quieter elder near the window spoke.
“…He adapts faster than we react.”
They turned.
“…We corrected the room,” he continued quietly. “…He already found another flaw before leaving.”
A subtle tightening passed through the group.

“…Then we stay ahead,” one elder said.
“…You assume that’s possible,” the quieter one replied.
That did not sit well.

The old man spoke again.
“…We adjust the structure.”
A pause.
“…And we watch the boy.”

Later that day, Mateo was summoned.
Not urgently.
Not forcefully.
But formally.
The request carried weight—not pressure, but expectation. Enough that ignoring it would cost more than attending.

“…You’re going,” Tomas said, watching him.
“…Yes.”
“…You’re not concerned.”
Mateo considered briefly.
“…Should I be?”
“…Yes.”
Mateo nodded once.
“…I’ll keep that in mind.”
Diego leaned in immediately.
“…He’s not going to keep it in mind.”
“…That is correct,” Mateo said.

=======================
The hall felt different this time.
More controlled.
More intentional.
The air was quieter, the layout adjusted, even the way light fell across the table slightly altered. The structure now held more deliberately—less casual, more enforced.
Mateo noticed immediately.
 They adjusted.
 Good.

He stepped inside without hesitation.
This time—the door closed behind him.
That was new.
 Deliberate containment.
He noted it.
Stored it.

One of the elders leaned forward.
“You entered this room without permission yesterday,” he said. “Explain.”
Mateo stood calmly.
“…Are you asking for an explanation,” he said, “or for justification?”
A pause.
“…Both.”
Mateo nodded.
“…The door was open.”
“…That is not sufficient.”
Mateo tilted his head.
“…Then the door should not have been open.”
A flicker of irritation.
“…You are deflecting.”
“…No,” Mateo replied. “…I am identifying the first failure.”
A few elders shifted.
The old man did not.

“…And the second?” one asked.
Mateo’s gaze moved across them.
“…You noticed me too late.”

Silence.
This time—
it held better.
But not completely.

“…You are avoiding responsibility,” one elder said sharply.
Mateo paused.
Then asked:
“…What responsibility did I violate?”
The elder began—
Stopped.

Mateo continued.
“…If the rule is ‘no entry,’ then the responsibility to enforce it lies with those who created it. If it is not enforced…”
A slight pause.
“…then is it truly a rule?”

The room shifted.
Subtly.
This wasn’t irritation anymore.
It was evaluation.

=======================
“…Enough.”
The lead elder spoke again.
“You disrupted a meeting, challenged authority, and caused confusion.”
Mateo nodded.
“…Correct.”
“…Why?”
A pause.
Mateo looked at them.
“…Do you want the effective answer,” he asked, “…or the honest one?”
Tomas, at the edge of the room:
 …This is going to go badly.
“…Both,” the elder said.

Mateo replied immediately.
“Effective: your meeting structure had weaknesses. Those weaknesses are now corrected.”
Pause.
“Honest: I wanted to see what would happen.”

“…and?” the elder pressed.
Mateo tilted his head.
“…It was interesting.”

A few elders suppressed reactions.
Others didn’t.
One almost smiled.
The tone fractured—slightly—but did not collapse.

=======================
One elder leaned back.
“…You redirected the discussion,” he said. “Was that intentional?”
Mateo nodded.
“…Yes.”
“…Why?”
“…You were already uncertain,” Mateo replied. “I aligned with that.”
“…You manipulated the situation.”
Pause.
“…I clarified it.”

That drew tension—
Before—
“…He’s not wrong.”

The great-grandfather spoke.
Thumb raised slightly.

The room paused.

“…You’re encouraging him.”
“…I’m acknowledging efficiency.”

Mateo glanced once.
 …Useful.

Mateo turned toward the door.
Paused.
 …One more test.

They focused instantly.

“Your seating formation is improved,” Mateo said, “but still incomplete.”
“…Explain.”
“You’ve corrected sound direction,” Mateo said, “but not visual exposure.”
Movement across the room.
“…If someone stands at that angle outside…”
Pause.
“…movement is still visible.”

Tomas:
 …He’s doing it again.

An elder checked.
Stopped.
“…He’s right.”

“…Why do we keep listening to him?”

Mateo paused.
“…Because I am correct.”

And left.

 He understands everything…
 then acts like none of it matters.

Mateo stood beneath the open sky.
The forest extended outward—present, quiet, layered.
The pendant pulsed.
Stronger now.
More consistent.

 Something is aligning faster.

He closed his hand.
 Fragments… returning.

For a moment—
Something deeper stirred.
Not this world.
Not this life.
Something older.
Waiting.

Mateo exhaled slowly.
“…Good.”

Because for the first time—
The mystery wasn’t distant.

It was beginning to respond.
