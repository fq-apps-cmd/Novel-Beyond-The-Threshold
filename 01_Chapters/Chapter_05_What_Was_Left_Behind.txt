The change in the village was no longer subtle.
At first, it had been quiet—low conversations between those who trained, disagreements settled through speculation rather than certainty. People would pause mid-discussion, lean closer, and speak just a little softer, as if uncertainty demanded restraint.
Now, that restraint was gone.
Voices carried further across the dirt paths. Conversations didn't stop so much as shift, turning sharper, more deliberate. Even the usual morning rhythm of training had changed slightly—fewer careless movements, more attention directed outward rather than inward.
The expectations had grown heavier.
Mateo noticed not by what people said to him, but by what they stopped saying when he passed.
	…Predictable.
He stood in the yard as usual, repeating the same basic sequence Rafael had shown him long ago—stances, steps, controlled strikes. The ground beneath him had already been marked by repeated practice, shallow grooves in the dirt showing where weight had shifted time and time again.
Every movement remained deliberately simple.
Unrefined where refinement would have made a difference.
It no longer mattered.
They were watching anyway.

"They're here again," Diego muttered as he stepped up beside him, holding something wrapped poorly in cloth that was almost certainly food.
Mateo didn't stop moving.
"…They never left."
That was the problem.
At the far edge of the training area, where the packed dirt gave way to scattered roots and the forest began to press inward again, three figures stood. They weren't positioned casually—they had chosen that location deliberately, just outside the natural flow of movement, where they could observe without being drawn into it.
They carried themselves differently.
Not only in posture, but in presence.
Their footing was stable. Their gaze focused. Their stillness intentional.
They weren't better in an absolute sense.
But they were practiced.
And they weren't here for the village.
They were here for him.

"So this is the place people keep talking about," one of them said, his tone light but edged with something less friendly. "I expected something more… structured."
Mateo finished his movement before turning.
He did not rush.
"…You're welcome to organize it."
A few quiet snickers came from those nearby, quickly suppressed but still present.
The newcomer smiled faintly. He didn't appear amused.
He also didn't appear offended.
"…You're Mateo."
"That seems to be the consensus."

Tomas stepped forward slightly, just enough to shift attention without escalating unnecessarily.
"He's already training," Tomas said calmly. "If you came to prove something, wait."
The outsider's gaze flicked to him, then back.
"…Or we can test it now."

Mateo exhaled once.
	This was inevitable.

He stepped forward before Tomas could move further.
"…Take turns. Then leave."
The outsider's smile sharpened slightly.
"…Confident."
	No.
Pause.
	Efficient.

The first strike came faster than what Mateo had seen from most within the village—not reckless like Diego's, not controlled like Tomas', but something between the two.
Trained.
Eager.
Dominant.

Mateo stepped aside, letting it pass just beyond reasonable distance.
	Faster than most here…
Pause.
	Still obvious.

The second strike followed immediately, angled lower to cut off movement rather than test reaction.
	Better.

Mateo shifted slightly, adjusting just enough to redirect the path without engaging fully. His balance changed subtly, alignment correcting ahead of impact rather than reacting to it.
The third strike came harder.
Too hard.

	There.

Mateo stepped forward instead of back, guiding the force away from its center and dissolving its momentum before it could stabilize.
Then—
He tapped the outsider's shoulder.

"You're open."

The outsider froze.
Only for a moment.
Then stepped back.
"…Again."

Mateo sighed quietly.
	Of course.

The second exchange broke rhythm almost immediately.
The outsider pushed harder.
Not testing anymore.
Proving.

There was a line between pressure and force.
He crossed it.

Mateo saw it as it happened.
	He's trying to prove something now.

The next strike was faster, sharper—
And misaligned.

Mateo moved to intercept.
But before he could fully redirect—

Tomas stepped in.

The wooden saber intercepted cleanly, shifting the strike enough to prevent it from landing properly.
The outsider stumbled.
Balance broke.

For a moment—
Everything tightened.

"…You interfered," the outsider said.
Tomas' voice didn't rise.
"You lost control."

	Correct.

The air shifted.

Mateo stepped forward, positioning himself between them—not obviously, not directly, but enough.
"…That's enough."

His tone did not demand.
It ended things anyway.

Silence lingered briefly.
Then—
The outsider exhaled, stepping back.
"…Fine. I've seen enough."
But his gaze lingered.
Sharpened.

	That's not finished.

As tension eased, Mateo turned slightly—
And noticed it.

Tomas' arm.
A shallow cut ran along his forearm, clean and controlled.
Small.
But unacceptable.

"…You're injured."
Tomas glanced down.
"…It's nothing."

	Incorrect.

"Sit."
Tomas frowned slightly.
"I said—"
"Sit."

A pause.
Then—he did.

Diego leaned in immediately.
"…This is new. You're giving orders now?"
"…No," Mateo said calmly.
Pause.
"…I'm fixing a mistake."

Mateo retrieved the container he had prepared earlier. The faint glow inside had stabilized since its creation, no longer fluctuating as it had the first time.
	This should be enough.

He applied a thin layer across the cut.
No flare.
No visible effect.

Just warmth.

Tomas' expression shifted.
"…It's working."
The bleeding slowed.
Then stopped.

Diego leaned even closer.
"…Since when can you do that?"
Mateo didn't look up.
"…Since recently."

	Better effectiveness than expected.

Lucía approached without being noticed.
She didn't speak immediately.
She watched.

"That wasn't practice anymore," she said quietly.
Mateo didn't deny it.
"…No."

Her gaze lingered longer than usual.
"…We'll talk later."

	That's not optional.

As the day settled into evening, Mateo found himself beside Rafael.
The older man sat near the edge of a low structure, sharpening a simple blade. The steady scrape of metal against stone carried softly through the air, blending with the distant sounds of the forest shifting into night.

"…You've drawn attention," Rafael said.
Mateo didn't respond right away.
"…It wasn't intentional."

Rafael let out a quiet snort.
"It never is."

After a moment, he continued.
"You know… this isn't the first time our family's had attention."

Mateo glanced toward him.
"…No?"

Rafael nodded slightly, his tone still casual—but deeper now.
"Before you were born—before I was—we had people come through here. Warriors. Craftsmen. People who didn't belong in a place like this."
He paused.
"…They came for him."

Mateo's focus sharpened.
"…Him?"

"Your ancestor," Rafael said. "We just called him Gramps."

Diego appeared again.
Without warning.
"…You had a Gramps like that and nobody told me?"

Mateo ignored him.
"…What was he."

Rafael smiled faintly.
"…Big."

Silence.

"Big?" Diego repeated.

Rafael nodded.
"Built like a wall. Worked like it too. He chopped trees like they insulted him and worked iron like it was clay."

Mateo absorbed the description.

"…A blacksmith."

"Yeah," Rafael said. "But not the kind you're thinking."
He turned the blade slightly.
"He'd take simple metal—ordinary, cheap—and turn it into something better. Stronger."

Mateo's thoughts sharpened.
	Refinement through understanding…

"He never explained it," Rafael continued. "Said metal was like people. You just had to listen before shaping it."

Diego frowned.
"…I don't think I've ever listened to metal."

"…That explains a lot," Mateo replied.

Rafael chuckled.
Then his tone changed.

"He didn't leave because he wanted to."

The air shifted.

Mateo looked at him directly.
"…What do you mean."

Rafael set the blade down.
"…He became too strong."

Silence followed.

"Not the kind you think of," Rafael added. "The kind that changes things just by being there. People couldn't breathe right. Animals wouldn't come near."

Mateo stilled.
	…Pressure.

"He tried to stay," Rafael said. "But eventually… he couldn't."
"…So he left."
"…Yeah."
A pause.
"…Because staying would have hurt everything."

Mateo looked down at the pendant.
	Fragments… falling…

"…And the family?"

Rafael shrugged lightly.
"…Things change when someone like that leaves."

	Decline after forced ascension.

"…And the name?"

"Estrella?" Rafael said.
"It's just a name."

	No, it isn't.

That night, Mateo stood outside the herb shop.
The edge of the village blended directly into the forest here. No walls. No clear separation. Just less light, thicker shadow, and a shift in the air that suggested stepping further meant stepping into something else entirely.
The shop was closed.
Locked.

"Didn't sell to you earlier, did they?" Diego whispered, appearing far too interested.
"…No."
"…So now we're here."

Mateo studied the door.
	I could leave.
Pause.
	…That would be inefficient.

"Stay quiet."
"…Wait, are we actually—"

Mateo was already moving.

The window latch was simple.
Poorly secured.

	Predictable.

Within moments, he was inside.

Diego followed.
"…This is a bad idea."
"…Yes."
"…We're still doing it?"
"…Yes."

Inside, Mateo moved with precision. He examined herbs, selected only what he needed, and avoided unnecessary disturbance.
	No wasted motion.

When they stepped back outside, Diego exhaled.
"…You just proved them right."
"…About what."
"That you don't belong where you're supposed to be."

Mateo paused briefly.
Then continued walking.

	That was never the plan anyway.

Back under the open sky, Mateo held the pendant again.

It pulsed.
Warmer now.

	Something is aligning.

The thought felt closer this time.
More defined.

	Not broken.
Pause.
	Becoming.

He closed his hand around it.

	…This world is only the beginning.

And for the first time—
That didn't feel distant.

It felt real.
