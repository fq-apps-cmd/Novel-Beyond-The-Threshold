The elders did not return to their meeting. At least, not immediately.
What had been a routine discussion about minor disputes and training matters had, in a matter of minutes, unraveled into something far less stable. The room—once controlled and orderly, the kind of quiet space where even small sounds carried meaning—now held an atmosphere that was distinctly… unsettled. Even the way silence settled between them felt wrong, as though the room had not fully resumed belonging to them after being disrupted.
"He just… walked in."
Elder Vargas spoke first, his tone low and deliberate, as though repeating the words might somehow make the situation more reasonable.
"Yes," Elder Liang replied.
"And none of you stopped him."
No one answered right away.
Because that was the problem.
Elder Hiro shifted in his seat, irritation tightening his expression. "He didn't force his way in. He didn't sneak. He opened the door and entered as if he had already decided we would allow it."
"That," Elder Park said quietly, "may be worse."
"…He didn't act like someone who needed permission," Elder Vargas said at last, his brows furrowed. "He spoke as if he was already permitted to be here."
"That doesn't make sense," Elder Liang muttered. "He's a child."
Elder Hiro leaned back slightly, exhaling.
"…Then why did it feel like we were the ones being questioned?"
Silence followed.
Not empty—just heavy.
Elder Park tapped his fingers once against the table, then stopped as if the sound itself felt misplaced.
"…I found myself wanting to answer him without really questioning it," he said slowly. "Without thinking about why."
"…So did I," Elder Vargas admitted.
"That's absurd," Elder Hiro said.
"…It is," Elder Park replied. "…and yet it happened."
At the far end of the room, where light from the window barely reached, Elder Shen remained unusually still. He had not spoken when Mateo entered. He had not interrupted when the others reacted. Even now, he said nothing—only watched, fingers loosely folded, gazing at what seemed the sky as though listening to the shape of the discussion rather than the words.
No one looked at him yet.
But more than one had already noticed.
"…That boy," Elder Liang said, "is Rafael's son."
"That explains nothing," Elder Hiro replied.
"It explains enough," Elder Marcos said calmly from the head of the table. He had been listening without speaking, letting the others expend their frustration before offering his own weight. "If what we heard about his training is true, then it's no surprise he does not behave like the others."
"…Behaving differently is one thing," Elder Vargas said. "Walking into restricted meetings and speaking as if he belongs there is something else entirely."
"…He didn't act like he belonged," Elder Park corrected. "…He acted as if that distinction didn't matter."
That drew a few more glances.
"…That is worse," Elder Liang said quietly.
"…Should we discipline him?" Elder Hiro asked.
The question settled across the room.
No one answered immediately.
Elder Marcos leaned forward slightly. "…If we do, what exactly are we punishing?"
"Unauthorized entry in the elders hall," Elder Hiro said firmly.
"…In the elders hall that we failed to secure," Elder Vargas added.
"…That is not the point."
"…Isn't it?"
Silence flickered again.
"…He said he was testing something," Elder Park said, recalling the moment carefully.
"…Yes."
"…And he proved," Elder Liang added reluctantly, "…that we didn't notice him entering, and that our discussion could be overheard."
"And it could have been anyone overhearing us as we were too relaxed and were not focused on our surroundings as much as we should be…" Elder Vargas said.
That drew a few glances.
Elder Shen, still near the window, finally looked up—not enough to claim the room, just enough to suggest he had not missed anything. His expression was unreadable.
Elder Marcos nodded once. "Then he revealed a flaw."
"…By breaking rules," Elder Hiro said.
"…By exposing weaknesses," Elder Marcos corrected.
A pause.
"…There is a difference."
"…That does not mean we allow it to continue," Elder Hiro said firmly.
"No," Elder Marcos agreed. "It does not."
A pause.
"But neither should we misunderstand what happened."
Elder Hiro leaned forward. "Then say it clearly."
Elder Marcos did not answer immediately. He let the room settle first, as if the weight of the conclusion mattered as much as the words themselves.
"We failed in sequence," he said at last. "We failed to notice him. Failed to secure the room. Failed to challenge his premise quickly enough. Then failed to recognize that our hesitation had already become an implicit permission."
That landed harder than accusation would have.
Elder Hiro crossed his arms. "You make it sound like he controlled the room."
Elder Marcos's gaze shifted toward him. "Did he not?"
No one answered.
Because if they spoke too quickly, they would be lying.
Elder Vargas exhaled sharply. "Then we correct it. Ensure the hall restrictions are working as expected. Reinforce privacy and limit access more carefully."
Elder Liang nodded. "And make it clear he is not permitted entry under any circumstance."
Elder Park frowned. "That handles the room. Not the boy."
That was where the conversation had truly been leading.
The real issue was Mateo.
Another pause.
Then— Elder Shen spoke.
"…No."
The word wasn't loud, but it cut through the room more cleanly than anything said so far.
Several heads turned.
He did not shift in his seat.
"Restrictions alone will not solve this," he said. "He did not enter because the room was open. He entered because he wanted to know whether the room meant what people believed it meant."
Elder Hiro narrowed his eyes. "You sound impressed."
Elder Shen's expression didn't change.
Elder Park frowned. "You think we should do nothing?"
"I think," Elder Shen replied, "that reacting too strongly tells him more than silence would."
That settled uneasily amongst all the elders.
Elder Marcos looked toward him. "Then what do you suggest?"
Elder Shen lowered his gaze once more, as if the answer had been obvious from the beginning.
"Observe him."
A brief silence followed.
Elder Hiro let out a short, humorless breath. "That sounds dangerously troublesome."
"Of course it is when dealing with Mateo," Elder Shen said.
He finally looked at them fully now.
"If he is only a troublesome child, he will eventually reveal it. If he is unusually perceptive, pressure will refine that. If he is something else…"
He let the sentence trail.
That was enough.
The room stilled more completely than before.
Elder Park frowned. "Something else?"
Elder Shen did not answer directly.
"Most children test limits because they resent them," he said. "He tests them because he wants to know how far he can push them."
No one interrupted.
Because that felt more dangerous than simple disobedience.
Elder Marcos leaned back slightly. "You've been watching him."
Elder Shen's expression remained neutral. "Enough."
Elder Vargas exhaled slowly. "And your conclusion?"
Elder Shen answered without hesitation.
"He seems far more intelligent and capable than he puts on."
"As if he is not where he should be."
This place limits his capabilities it seems, Elder Shen thought to himself.
That line lingered in the room longer than expected.
Not because they misunderstood it.
Because they understood it too well in too many directions.
Elder Park finally asked, "Then we do nothing?"
Elder Marcos shook his head. "No. We adjust."
He looked around the room once, letting each of them feel the answer rather than simply hear it.
"We reinforce the hall. We change how we meet. We stop assuming privacy exists simply because we prefer it. And we watch the boy."
Another pause.
"If he pushes again," Elder Hiro said, "do we stop him?"
Elder Shen answered this time.
"Yes."
A pause.
"Properly."
That ended the discussion more effectively than anything else had.
The meeting did not resume.
Not really.
Because what Mateo had disrupted was no longer the agenda.
It was the assumption beneath it.
When the others had gone, Elder Shen remained by the window.
The room was dim now, the light outside thinning slowly as afternoon shifted toward evening. Dust drifted through the narrow beam that reached the far wall, and for a time he watched it in silence, following the tiny particles as they rose, turned, vanished into shadow, then reappeared elsewhere.
He stood at last and moved toward the place where Mateo had stood.
Calmly.
Without imitation.
There was still nothing remarkable there. No residue of power. No spiritual distortion. No evidence that anything unusual had occupied the space except memory itself.
That, perhaps, was what bothered him most.
Most gifted children revealed themselves through excess. Too much force. Too much pride. Too much eagerness to be seen.
This one did not.
He pressed two fingers lightly against the tabletop Mateo had gestured toward earlier, as if testing whether contact alone might recover meaning. It did not.
"…You test structures before you accept them," he murmured quietly, not expecting an answer. "That is either intelligence…"
A pause.
"…or inheritance."
His gaze shifted toward the doorway.
Then farther than that, as though the path beyond it might still carry the shape of the boy who had walked through so easily.
Elder Shen's expression remained composed.
But his thoughts did not.
He had seen children with talent. Children with discipline. Children who wore arrogance like armor because they had not yet learned what the world did to those who mistook promise for inevitability.
Mateo did not feel like any of those.
He felt—
misplaced.
That thought returned again, and he allowed it this time.
Not where he should be.
Not because he was dangerous in the obvious sense.
Not because he was stronger than he should have been.
Because he moved through assumptions too easily.
And people like that either became useful very quickly—
or catastrophic and followed a path towards destruction.
Elder Shen turned from the window at last.
"…Let us see which one you are."
Then he left the hall, the room falling quiet behind him once more.
Far from the meeting hall, where the village paths curved back into familiar patterns of movement and sound, Mateo stretched lightly beneath the shade of a tree as though nothing of consequence had occurred earlier that day. The dirt beneath him was warm now, marked by footsteps and scattered impressions from prior training. Some prints held shape clearly. Others blurred at the edges, as though the ground itself could not fully decide how long to remember.
"You're going to get in trouble."
Diego said this with the certainty of someone who believed it completely—and the excitement of someone who very much wanted to be present when it happened.
Mateo didn't open his eyes and shrugged.
"…Possibly."
"That's your response?"
"…It's accurate."
Diego crossed his arms.
"…Is that not concerning?"
Mateo opened one eye slightly.
"…Being concerned would not improve the outcome."
Diego paused.
"…I don't like that that makes sense."
Tomas stood nearby, arms crossed, his expression still carrying the faint traces of something unsettled—something he had not fully put into words yet. His attention wasn't on the yard, or the people.
It was on Mateo.
"…You don't seem concerned at all."
Mateo opened his eye slightly further.
"…Should I be?"
Tomas stared at him for a moment.
"…Yes."
A brief pause.
Mateo closed his eye again.
"…I'll consider it."
Diego snorted.
"He's not going to consider it."
"That is correct," Mateo said.
Tomas exhaled slowly, as if searching for a way to approach something that resisted normal explanation.
"…What you did," Tomas began, "should have gone differently."
Mateo tilted his head slightly but didn't otherwise move.
"…In what way."
"You entered a restricted area, spoke to the elders as if you had authority, then left."
"…Yes."
"…That's not normal."
Mateo considered this.
"…Correct."
Tomas narrowed his eyes.
"…Then why do it?"
For a brief moment, Mateo didn't answer.
Not because he didn't have one—
But because he was deciding which answer was the best in this situation.
Then he sat up slowly and turned toward Tomas, his posture shifting just enough to return to that calm, measured presence. The space around him seemed to settle slightly as he did, as though movement aligned more cleanly when he chose to focus.
"…If a system functions properly," he said, "then it should be able to withstand unexpected input."
Diego blinked.
"…What?"
Mateo continued without looking at him.
"…If it cannot, then the system is flawed."
A pause.
"…If no one tests it…"
He let the thought linger, just long enough to solidify.
"…then the flaw remains."
Tomas watched him closely now, something in his expression tightening—not disagreement, but something closer to reluctant understanding.
"…And you decided to test it."
"…Yes."
"…By walking into an elder meeting."
"…Yes."
Another pause stretched between them.
Long enough to almost accept the logic.
"…You could have been punished."
"That outcome was considered."
"…And you still did it."
Mateo nodded once.
"…Yes."
Tomas exhaled slowly.
"…So you weighed the consequence…"
"…Yes."
"…And decided it was worth it."
"…Yes."
A short pause.
"…Why."
Mateo answered without hesitation this time.
"…Because the result was more useful than the cost."
Diego blinked again.
"…That's… actually terrifying."
Then Mateo added, without changing his tone:
"…Also, it was funnier this way."
Silence.
Diego immediately snapped forward.
"…I KNEW IT."
Tomas stared.
"…You just gave a full explanation about systems and structure—"
"…That part was correct."
"—and then said you did it because it was funny."
Mateo looked at him calmly.
"…Both are true."
Tomas blinked.
Slowly.
…This doesn't make sense.
Why does it make sense.
"…You're a problem," Tomas said again.
Mateo nodded faintly.
"…Yes."
… …
By the next morning, the story had changed.
Slightly.
Then completely.
"He walked in and challenged them."
"No, he tested them."
"I heard he was invited."
"He wasn't invited."
"I heard he corrected them."
"He embarrassed them."
"Wait—I heard he didn't even knock."
"That part's probably true."
Along the dirt paths and small gathering areas, the story moved faster than accuracy, reshaped by each retelling. Voices overlapped, shortened, exaggerated.
Details blurred.
Impact remained.
Mateo listened from a distance.
Narratives are unstable.
They didn't spread truth.
They spread stories.
"…They're saying you're some kind of hidden genius now," Diego said, far too pleased with that development.
Mateo glanced at him.
"…They were saying that yesterday."
"…Yeah, but now it's louder."
That's worse.
"…Louder means less accurate," Mateo added.
Diego frowned.
"…That doesn't stop it."
"…No," Mateo replied. "…It accelerates it."
Tomas stood beside him, watching the reactions around them even if he wasn't directly engaging.
"…They're going to keep pushing," he said.
Mateo didn't respond right away.
Yes.
"They want to see what you'll do next," Tomas added.
"…That's inefficient," Mateo replied.
"…It's inevitable."
A brief pause.
"…Then we adjust," Mateo said simply.
"…Adjust how."
Mateo looked ahead.
"…By becoming less predictable."
Diego tilted his head.
"…I thought you said everyone is predictable."
Mateo paused briefly.
"…That doesn't include me."
Diego stared.
"…That's incredibly unfair."
"…Correct."
… …
Later that afternoon, Mateo noticed something else.
Someone different.
Not from the group before.
Not from the village.
Someone watching.
Not openly.
But carefully.
…Different.
The presence wasn't aggressive.
But it wasn't casual either.
It lingered longer than necessary, its attention fixed rather than drifting.
Even at a distance, the stillness had intent behind it.
…Interesting.
… …..
That evening, Mateo walked alone again, the forest pressing quietly around the edges of the village as the last traces of daylight faded. The familiar movement and noise softened into something slower, less structured. The transition wasn't abrupt—but it was noticeable.
The pendant rested lightly in his hand.
It pulsed.
Faint.
But more frequently now.
Something is responding… or forming.
He turned it slowly, watching the fractured pattern shift under low light. The reflection didn't settle cleanly—it fractured across small angles before aligning again.
Fragments aligning…
For a moment, that same image surfaced again.
Fire.
Metal.
Hands shaping something unrefined into something precise.
The glow held steady.
Stronger this time.
Gone.
Mateo tightened his grip slightly.
"…Not yet."
The words were quiet.
But deliberate.
But soon.
Somewhere behind him, Diego was already shouting about something completely unrelated.
"…No, I'm telling you that tree moved."
"That's wind."
"It wasn't wind. That tree looked at me."
"Trees don't look at people."
"This one felt judgmental."
Tomas sighed.
"…I'm ignoring both of you."
And the village, for all its quiet simplicity—
Continued.
