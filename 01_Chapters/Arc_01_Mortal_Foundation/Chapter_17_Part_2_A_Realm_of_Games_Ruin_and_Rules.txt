But the explanation did not end with Mateo. Across the countless realms the announcement had touched—questions followed.

🌊 World of Endless Waters
Far beneath a shifting ocean sky, currents moved in deliberate spirals around a massive form. Blue scales, each the size of a shield, rippled with quiet power as a colossal python coiled around a submerged ruin. Its eyes opened—ancient, steady, aware.
The voice had reached here. The fireworks had passed through water without resistance. That alone was… unacceptable.
The python spoke, its voice vibrating through the ocean itself. “…If this is a realm… what governs entry?”
The water stilled.
Then—
The chihuahua appeared.
Not standing. Floating. As if casually treading water that did not exist. It wore a ridiculous lifeguard outfit for exactly half a second—whistle, red float, everything—before spinning lazily and returning to its robe and pipe.
“…Excellent question, large and dramatically scaled aquatic individual,” it said, blowing a bubble that drifted upward through water like it had forgotten how gravity worked.
A tiny pause.
“…Also, very good timing.”
The python did not react.
“…Entry is simple,” the spirit continued. “If you have a token, you enter. If you do not, you do not. If you are strong enough to cheat the system…” It squinted slightly. “…please don’t. It’s very inconvenient for bookkeeping.”
A pause.
“…Power will not determine entry.”
Another.
“…But it will determine what happens after.”
The python’s eyes narrowed. “…So the strong are not favored?”
The chihuahua grinned.
“…They are noticed.”
A beat.
“…Sometimes that’s worse.”

🌫️ Fragmented Ruin World
A lone cultivator stepped carefully across shifting stone platforms suspended in empty air. The fireworks had reflected across surfaces that weren’t aligned, crossing distances that shouldn’t connect.
He spoke quietly. “…If these worlds have rules… can they be broken?”
The chihuahua appeared sitting cross-legged midair beside him, chewing thoughtfully on something that did not exist.
“…Everything can be broken,” it said casually.
A beat.
“…But some things break back harder.”
The cultivator paused. “…So there is risk.”
“…Oh, absolutely,” the chihuahua replied. “There are entire worlds designed to punish overconfident people.”
A pause.
“…Those are my favorites.”
A tiny shift in tone—barely noticeable.
“…They’re very educational.”

🪨 Mountain Sect (Follow-up Reaction)
An elder finally spoke. “…What determines reward?”
The chihuahua reappeared, still balanced atop the floating sword—though now its posture had shifted subtly. The humor didn’t vanish, but it sharpened.
“…For those who walk the sword path,” it said, voice lowering slightly, “reward is not as simple as gain.”
The air grew still.
“…There are worlds within this opening that respond only to intent sharpened by discipline. Realms where sword spirits wander without masters, waiting for something worthy enough to resonate with. Places where fragments of will, left behind by those who cut through their limits, still linger—watching, rejecting, or acknowledging.”
The disciples didn’t move.
“…There are sword tombs,” it continued, “fields filled with blades of every grade and history. Weapons that remember the hands that held them. Some will tremble when approached. Some will reject you outright. A few…”
A small pause.
“…will test you.”
Its gaze drifted slightly.
“…And there are those who came before. Legends. Not alive in the way you understand, but not entirely gone either. Echoes of mastery bound into trials, waiting for someone to challenge them—not with strength alone…”
A slight pause.
“…but with comprehension.”
A longer one.
“…Those who fail are cut down… correctly.”
Silence took hold. The elder’s expression shifted—just slightly.
The chihuahua leaned back, the seriousness cracking again at the edges.
“…Context,” it added.
“…That is not helpful.”
“It is extremely helpful,” the spirit corrected. “You just don’t like it.”
A pause.
“…Some worlds reward survival. Some reward progress. Some reward technique. Some reward creativity. Some reward not dying in a particularly impressive way.”
A longer pause.
“…And some worlds reward things you will not understand until it is too late to regret them.”
The disciples went silent.

🌑 Veil of Endless Night (Follow-up Reaction)
One kneeling figure dared to ask, “My lord… should one such as us participate?”
Vael Noctis said nothing.
The chihuahua appeared behind him. Not in front. Not beside. Behind.
“…Oh, you absolutely should,” it said cheerfully.
Silence deepened.
“…People like you make things very interesting.”
A faint pause.
“…And occasionally very short-lived.”
Another pause—subtle.
“…But you already knew that.”
Vael did not move.
But the darkness around him folded slightly.

🌋 Flame World (Follow-up Reaction)
The small flame spirit spoke again. “…Will it hurt?”
The woman’s hand remained calm against its small form.
The chihuahua stretched back into its beach chair, now casually sipping from something glowing.
“…Yes,” it said immediately.
Then paused.
“…But only the parts you’re still attached to.”
A brief glance upward.
“…Which tends to be the important parts.”
The flame spirit wavered.
The woman did not.
“…Pain is refinement,” she said quietly.
The chihuahua blinked.
“…I was going to say ‘don’t worry about it,’ but that works too.”

🧠 Final Realm-Wide Clarification
The chihuahua’s voice spread again. Not forced. Just… present.
“Let me make one thing very clear,” it said.
“There is no single path in these realms.”
It blew a slow stream of bubbles.
At first, they were small. Drifting. Harmless.
Then they grew.
Not just in size—
in depth.
The bubbles expanded as they floated, their surfaces thickening like layers of glass. Light bent across them unnaturally, and within each one—images began to form.
Clear. Alive.
Across realms—people looked up.
Within one bubble—
A forest of enormous glowing herbs stretched endlessly, each leaf pulsing with energy, creatures moving silently beneath them, careful… watching.
Another—
A battlefield inside a storm, lightning crashing as cultivators raced across broken terrain, not fighting each other—but chasing something barely visible ahead of them.
Another—
A quiet forge world, where a lone blacksmith hammered slowly, each strike sending ripples through the air itself as entire weapons formed not from metal—but from intention.
Another—
A shifting maze, walls rearranging as figures ran, hid, laughed—and screamed, realizing the maze was watching them back.
Another—
A vast ocean where the water rose upward instead of down, creatures swimming through suspended currents, while something enormous moved beneath the surface.
The bubbles continued to grow. Multiplying. Layering. Filling the sky.
Some burst—not into nothing—but into fragments of possibility, scattering across space before fading.
Silence spread across realities. Not from fear. From… realization.
“…All of that…” someone whispered.
“…Those are the realms…?”
The chihuahua nodded, pipe still drifting bubbles lazily.
“Oh, that’s barely scratching the surface,” it said casually.
Another bubble drifted forward.
Inside it—cultivators laughed while racing across shifting platforms.
Another—a figure collapsed alone in darkness.
Another—a child sat peacefully beside a spirit.
“Some of you will enter and become weaker.”
“Some will lose everything and start again.”
“Some will dominate one world and fail completely in the next.”
“Some will find things you were never supposed to find.”
Another bubble formed. Distorted. Incomplete. Something inside it… breaking.
“Some—”
A pause.
“…will realize they were never meant to survive at all.”
One bubble shattered quietly.
As if it had simply… failed.
Silence stretched. Longer. Heavier.
But still—no one looked away.
Because the possibilities remained.
The chihuahua grinned.
“But don’t worry about all that right now.”
A clap.
“You have six months.”
“Train. Think. Panic.”
“Organize yourselves if you can.”
“And try not to die too early.”
A beat.
“…It makes the later parts less exciting.”
Then the presence withdrew. Not instantly. Gradually. Like something closing a door it never fully opened.
A faint mutter lingered—
“…honestly, I should have charged more for this…”

🌌 Transition Back to Mateo
The cave remained. Unchanged. Unaffected. And yet—not untouched.
Mateo stood there, still facing the passage.
Most of what had been said did not matter.
Not yet.
Worlds. Games. Competitors. Rules.
All distant. Future problems.
Though a faint trace of amusement lingered.
“…Still ridiculous,” he murmured quietly.
A pause.
“…Consistently ridiculous.”
What mattered now was directly in front of him.
The air below was colder. Denser. Waiting.
Mateo stepped forward.
And the deeper world began.
