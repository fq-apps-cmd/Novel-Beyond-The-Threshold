# Chapter 21 - The Cost of Absence

Chapter 21 — The Cost of Absence  

The intersection was four hours' walk from the boundary, and he covered it in five.  
The shoulder was the reason. The claw wounds along his ribs had closed clean under the transitional-zone preparation, and the seal was holding well enough  
that he had stopped thinking about them. The bite had not closed at all. Whatever the alpha's jaw had left behind sat in the tissue and refused every  
compound he owned, and the arm below it worked at something like two-thirds, which was survivable and which cost him a fraction of his balance on every  
uneven step.  
Five hours of uneven steps.  
By the time the light came up properly he had stopped correcting for it consciously and started letting the correction live in his hip, which was worse  
mechanically and cheaper in attention, and he noted the trade the way he noted everything and kept walking.  
He was carrying other things too.  
Somebody in the village told somebody outside it that I went into Chen's shop.  
He had turned that over most of the night and gotten nowhere useful. Diego knew. Diego had been there. Diego would not have told anyone, not because  
Diego was discreet — Diego was the least discreet person Mateo had ever met — but because Diego had personally participated and considered it the finest  
night of his life. He would tell the story eventually. He would tell it to everyone. He had not told it yet, because he was saving it.  
Tomas did not know.  
Which left the possibility that nobody had told anyone, and that whoever cut the door had simply been in the village long enough to learn which shop the  
strange boy had been refused at and which post he trained on, and had cut both to demonstrate that they knew.  
That version was worse. That version meant months.  
And the post was nine meters inside the fence line.  
He put it down and picked up the ground instead, which was at least a problem the day could act on.  
The forest changed as he climbed toward the intersection. The dispersive soft ground of the water zone gave back to something firmer, and then firmer than  
firm — compressed, the way earth compresses when something pushes upward through it. He could feel the two currents from three hundred meters out  
now, which he had not been able to do two days ago. Either they had strengthened or he had.  
He suspected the second and did not entirely trust the suspicion.  
The dark-leafed herb was gone.  
He stopped when he saw the space where it had been. The root system he had left in the soil to regenerate was still there, still faintly luminous at the  
boundary his awareness could reach, and it was regenerating — but the stem he had cut had not been the only stem. There had been three others within a  
few paces, smaller, less developed, that he had left because they were not mature and he had not needed them.  
They had been taken. Not cut. Pulled, roots and all, the soil torn open and left open.  
Waste.  
Same as the broken stems near the boundary. Same individual, or same instruction.  
He crouched at the torn ground and put a palm flat against it.  
The currents were wrong.  
Not badly. Not catastrophically. But the separation he had induced two days ago, the temporary equilibrium he had built to let one specimen come out of the  
ground without detonating, had not resolved the way he had expected. It had not collapsed back into the original dual flow. It had held — and then it had  
begun propagating, the way a fold propagates through cloth once the first crease is set.  
I separated two currents in a root system perhaps a handspan across.  
This is running for meters now.  
I did not do that. I started something that is doing that.  
He was still crouched with his hand in the dirt when the pack arrived.  

They came without any attempt at concealment, which was the first difference.  
The alpha came last, which was the second.  
It moved at the back of its own formation, favoring the right foreleg with the specific economy of an animal that had spent a day learning exactly how much  
weight the shoulder would take. Four wolves came ahead of it, spread wide, and they did not close. They took positions and held them, and the geometry  
they made was not the contracting semicircle of the previous day.  
It was a fence.  
They are not hunting me.  
They are keeping me here.  
Mateo straightened slowly and took his hand out of the soil.  
"...You came back for this," he said. "Not for me."  
The alpha's ears moved. Whether it understood the words was irrelevant; it understood the tone, and it understood that the boy had stopped touching the  
ground, and that was the entire content of the exchange.  
Then one of the younger wolves broke.  
Mateo saw why before it moved — the animal had been holding position on ground that had gone soft under it, the propagating separation reaching that  
far, and the instability had put a tremor through its legs that its nerves interpreted as threat. It came at him because standing still had become unbearable.  
It came badly.  
Mateo stepped inside the line, and his left arm did not come up in time.  
The correction had to route through his right side, which meant rotating into the strike instead of away from it, which meant the shoulder took the impact of  
the collision rather than the redirection. Pain went through the bite wound and out the other side of him, white and total, and for a fraction of a second his  

vision reduced to a tunnel with a wolf at the end of it.  
His right hand still worked.  
The strike went into the animal's chest below the ribs, short, no distance, the whole chain from planted foot to knuckle firing in the sequence that had  
stopped needing his attention two years ago. The wolf came off him and hit the ground and did not get up quickly.  
Mateo staggered backward and caught himself against a trunk and stayed there for a moment, breathing.  
That cost more than it should have.  
Because the left side is compromised, and because the ground under my rear foot moved during the transfer.  
The ground moved because of me.  
The alpha had not advanced. It watched him from the back of its formation with its ruined shoulder held off the earth, and there was nothing in the look that  
resembled triumph.  

The others arrived over the following minutes, and none of them came for him.  
To the south, something moved through the underbrush with the indifference of a thing that had never in its life needed to be careful about where it walked.  
It was enormous and low and layered in plates that were not hide and not shell but mineral, grown from the animal outward, each one fitted against the next  
with a precision that suggested a very long time and no hurry at all. The energy coming off it was dense and earthbound and carried the flat signature of  
something that had been standing on the same ground for longer than the village had existed.  
It stopped at the edge of the intersection zone. Snorted once, and the sound arrived through the soles of Mateo's feet before it reached his ears.  
Then it turned its head toward the torn soil where the herbs had been pulled, and held there.  
Above and to the left, along a branch, something that had been indistinguishable from bark resolved into a serpent perhaps four meters long, whose scales  
did not reflect light so much as decline to commit to it. Its outline blurred against whatever it lay on. Only the eyes stayed constant — two points of cold  
steady attention that were not directed at Mateo, or at the wolves, but downward.  
At the ground.  
Further out, at the extreme edge of what he could perceive, something tall and lean stood entirely still, and its antlers branched in patterns too regular to  
have grown that way. They did not put out energy. They took it in, gathering ambient information from a wide radius and processing it somewhere Mateo  
could not follow, and the whole animal had the posture of an instrument in the middle of a measurement.  
None of them looked at him.  
Mateo stood against his tree with his shoulder bleeding through a bandage and understood something that reorganized the entire week.  
The forest has a structure. The boar holds ground. The serpent watches. The stag calculates. The hawk patrols. The wolves hunt.  
None of those functions are pointed at me.  
They are all pointed at the same place, and I am standing in it.  

Then the ground moved.  
Not physically. Nothing shifted, nothing shook, no soil displaced. What moved was underneath the soil, in the layer he had been learning to feel through his  
feet for three days, and it moved the way something very large moves when it changes position without waking.  
Pressure came up through the earth. Not directed. Not aggressive. Not aware of him in any way he could identify, and that was the part that took the strength  
out of his legs — that it was not aware of him. It simply existed, embedded so deeply in the structure of the ground that feeling it was like feeling the weight  
of a mountain you happened to be standing on.  
His awareness went out to meet it before he decided to, and got a fraction of the way, and stopped.  
Not blocked. It simply ran out of capacity, the way a cup runs out of capacity.  
This is what the ripple went to.  
Two days ago I separated two currents in one root system and something the size of this forest turned over in its sleep.  
Every animal in the clearing had gone still. The wolves were not in formation anymore; they were frozen in place with the total immobility of creatures whose  
oldest instinct had just overridden every other consideration they possessed. The boar had locked. The serpent had begun withdrawing along its branch,  
upward and away.  
The stag was already gone.  
Against Mateo's chest, the pendant went thin.  
Third time. The hawk. The intersection. Now.  
It hides before I do. Every time.  
Then the pressure subsided. Not departing — subsiding, the way water pulls back without leaving the ocean. Whatever had shifted had not woken. It had  
turned over, adjusted, and settled.  
The clearing stayed silent for a long moment afterward.  
The alpha stepped back first.  
Not in fear. In the same recognition it had shown him at the pond, but wider — the acknowledgment of a creature encountering something it could not fight,  
negotiate with, or leave the territory of. It made a single low sound, and the pack came off their positions and went into the undergrowth, and the fence they  
had made around him dissolved.  
It looked at him once before it followed them.  
You did that, the look did not say, because wolves did not say things. But the timing of it was not accidental, and Mateo had spent his entire existence reading  
timing.  

He stayed where he was for some time after the forest emptied.  
The pain in his shoulder had gone from acute to structural, and he could feel every place his body was compensating for it, and standing there with  
adrenaline still in his system and the ground still humming faintly beneath him, he could feel something else.  
He could feel everything that was wrong with him.  
He had been at the ninth level of Body Tempering for longer than anyone knew. The final stage. The ceiling past which no amount of physical refinement  
produced further gain, reached at thirteen through four years of training that had never once been about strength and had always been about waste. Muscle,  
bone, blood, organ — all of it tempered past anything the village had a category for.  
The village believed he sat somewhere around the fourth level. Perhaps the fifth on a generous assessment. His suppression was thorough and his  
performance had been consistent for years, and the misunderstanding had been worth more to him than the truth ever could have been.  

Standing alone in a clearing that every predator in the district had just abandoned, it was worth nothing.  
And the peak, he understood now, had been incomplete.  
He could feel the flaws. Not because they were new — because the conditions had finally arrived that made them visible. Comfort had never revealed them.  
Repetition had never revealed them. It had taken five hours of walking on a compromised side, and a fight he had nearly lost to a young wolf that should not  
have touched him, and ground that moved under his rear foot during a transfer.  
The delay in his left shoulder was not only the wound. There was a misalignment in how force crossed that joint that had been there before the alpha ever bit  
him, and the injury had merely made it loud enough to hear.  
The compensation in his right ankle on uneven ground was correcting for an asymmetry in his hip rotation that he had built himself, years ago, and had been  
carrying ever since.  
And at the junction between his core and his lower spine, a fraction of every strike he had ever thrown had been dissipating into a gap too small to detect  
under any load the training yard could produce.  
Months, at least. Possibly since he was eleven.  
He closed his eyes.  
He did not force anything. There was no breakthrough available here and nothing to break through to. What he did was slower and less impressive and took  
most of an hour, and consisted of finding each imperfection and correcting the alignment that had produced it, and then correcting the secondary  
compensations that the first correction exposed, and then the ones after those.  
His breathing shifted first, the phases coming into register with the oscillation of his core rather than running a half-beat off it. Then his center settled a  
fraction lower and a fraction forward, into the position from which every standing posture cost marginally less to hold. Then the transitions — the joins  
between one motion and the next, where he had been spending force for years without ever seeing the invoice.  
Muscle. Tendon. Bone. Blood. The meridian pathways running through him like channels cut in stone, empty for now, carrying nothing, but aligned and  
waiting to.  
They stopped operating separately.  
There was no sound. No release of energy, no visible pressure, nothing that anything watching could have detected without touching him. The completion of  
Body Tempering was a physical event, appropriately enough, and physical events did not announce themselves.  
It was also absolute.  
When he opened his eyes, the shoulder still hurt exactly as much. But his body was no longer correcting itself continuously beneath the level of his attention.  
Each position found its own alignment. Each movement fed the next without the micro-adjustments that had been quietly taxing him since he was old  
enough to walk properly.  
Small margins. Compounded across a thousand exchanges, small margins were the entire difference.  
And past the margin, past the completion, he could feel the next thing.  
Qi Gathering sat on the other side of a membrane that had thinned to almost nothing during the correction. Not spiritual energy in the cultivated sense —  
raw ambient energy, the same current running under his feet, the same thing the wolves had been drinking passively for decades. It pressed against the  
boundary from outside. His finished system pressed back from inside.  
Crossing would have taken very little.  
A foundation with flaws in it does not stop having flaws when you build on top of it. It stops being reachable.  
I have just spent an hour finding three I have been carrying for years. There will be others. They will surface under conditions I have not met yet.  
Advancing now is faster. Advancing after this settles is clean.  
"...Later," he said.  

He came back down out of the intersection zone as the light started to go, and he did not take anything with him.  
The golden-threaded roots were still at the pond where he had left them the previous day, and he passed within forty meters of them and kept walking. Not  
from caution. He had simply revised what they were. They had looked, two days ago, like a resource growing in unusually rich ground.  
They looked now like part of something's circulatory system.  
The boar holds ground it does not use. The serpent watches ground it does not hunt. The stag measures.  
None of them are guarding territory. They are all doing the same job in different ways, and the job is the ground itself.  
The alpha did not come to the pond because I trespassed. It came because I put my hands in.  
And I put my hands in twice, and the second time something the size of the entire forest changed position.  
He stopped walking about a kilometer from the boundary, where the ground stopped humming and started being ordinary dirt, and stood for a while in the  
failing light, and let himself finish the thought he had been declining to finish since Chapter and circumstance had first put it in front of him.  
Two days ago I separated two currents to harvest one plant.  
The same afternoon, something felt it from far enough away that the sensation had to travel through the ground to reach me.  
That night, someone cut a doorway in a village six hours from here, in a shop I robbed when I was ten, and a training post nine meters inside the fence line,  
where my own footwork was still in the dirt.  
He had spent the whole of the previous night trying to determine who in the village had informed on him, because that was a solvable problem with a small  
number of candidates, and because solving it would have meant the answer lived inside the village where he could reach it.  
It had not once occurred to him to ask whether the two things were the same event.  
He examined that failure with the same detachment he applied to everything, and got the same result he had been getting for four days, which was  
nothing — no distress, no alarm, only the flat awareness that a functioning method had been available and he had declined to point it at himself.  
Fifth time.  
He stopped counting.  
Chen is sixty-eight years old and has spent forty of them learning which soil produces which grade of material, and he will never once be told why his door was  
cut.  
He will blame the wind. He has already blamed the wind for me.  
The village would be lighting its lamps around now. The patrols would be walking their new overlapping routes through gaps that Elder Kurosawa had closed  
while Mateo was standing in a clearing correcting his hip rotation. Tomas would be waking tomorrow feeling better than he had in weeks, and would credit  
the sleep, and would be wrong, and would not know he had been wrong, because Mateo had decided in the dark that an explanation cost more than a  
violation.  
He had been in the forest for all of it. Every part.  

That was the arrangement. It had been the arrangement since he was ten years old and worked out that pressure applied to the people around him was  
cheaper than pressure applied to him directly, and that the correct response was to stand still and let it accumulate somewhere else.  
It had worked.  
It was working now.  
Mateo turned toward the village and started walking, and behind him, far down and far under, something that had shifted in its sleep two days ago had still  
not settled all the way back.
