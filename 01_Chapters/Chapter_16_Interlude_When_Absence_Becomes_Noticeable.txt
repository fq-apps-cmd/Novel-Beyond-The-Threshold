The village did not realize Mateo was gone immediately. At first, nothing seemed out of place. The dirt roads carried the usual movement—footsteps pressed into hardened earth shaped by years of training rather than design. The paths curved naturally through the village, not in straight lines but in efficient arcs between homes, open training grounds, and scattered shops. No one had ever bothered paving them. They could have. They simply chose not to.
Stone paths and rune-laid streets existed in larger settlements, even in nearby regions, but here—where training spilled into walkways and fights began mid-conversation before ending without resentment—hard-packed soil worked better. It shifted under pressure, absorbed impact, and required no repair after conflict.
Which was frequent.
Near the center, a group of young trainees sparred in a loose circle, their movements rough but energetic. One overcommitted and was knocked flat.
"Too slow."
"Then stop letting me hit you."
He was already back on his feet.
No one intervened.
No one needed to.
Further along, a row of simple structures formed the trading path. The herb stall remained open, sharp scents drifting into the road—clean, bitter, precise. The woman behind it sorted bundles of roots and leaves with quiet efficiency.
She didn't look up when she spoke.
"…He hasn't passed today."
Across from her, the blacksmith paused mid-strike. His hammer hovered before falling again.
"…No."
A pause.
"…He usually does."
"…Yeah."
They said nothing else.
But they noticed.

Tomas noticed first.
Not because he was paying more attention—
But because he had started expecting Mateo.
"…He's late."
A voice nearby responded quickly.
"…Without explanation?"
Tomas glanced up briefly.
Diego.
They hadn't spoken much recently—but they weren't strangers either. Just… people who crossed paths often enough to recognize patterns.
"…Yes."
Diego studied him for a moment.
"…Then something is wrong."
Tomas didn't argue.
He was already moving.
Diego followed.
Neither commented on that.

They walked the path Mateo usually took. Past the training ground. Past the herb stall. Past the edge where the village stopped organizing the land and started surviving it.
They reached the forest line.
Tomas stopped.
His foot pressed into the earth—
And sank less than it should have.
He blinked.
Shifted.
The ground responded differently.
Not stronger. Not smoother.
But—
Connected.
A faint ripple moved through the soil beneath him—not outward, but through him, as though the earth had stopped lagging behind his intent.
Tomas stilled.
"…That's new."
Diego leaned slightly, staring down.
"…Did the ground just listen to you?"
"…No."
Tomas narrowed his eyes.
"…It followed."
The sensation vanished.
Tomas looked toward the forest.
"…He went in."
Diego blinked.
"…He what?"
Tomas exhaled quietly.
"…He said things would get interesting when he started going deeper."
Diego scratched his head.
"…Is he always like that?"
"…Yes."
"…Does it usually end well?"
"…No."
A pause.
"…So you're concerned."
"…Yes."

They didn't move forward.
Not yet.

The elders gathered once his absence was confirmed.
Not in panic.
Not in disorder.
That wasn't how things worked here.
"…He left without telling anyone."
"That is not surprising."
"…He is fourteen."
"…He is Mateo."
That ended it.
The great-grandfather sat quietly.
"…He went into the forest," Tomas said.
"I expected it," the old man replied.
Silence.
"…Should we retrieve him?"
"If he can be retrieved easily, he has not gone far enough to matter."
"…And if he has?"
A faint smile.
"…Then he is already solving it."
Diego leaned slightly toward Tomas.
"…That is both reassuring and deeply concerning."
"…Yes."

Outside—
The mission board had already become a problem.

Diego stood in front of it, arms crossed.
"…This system is flawed."
"…You've been here for five minutes."
"That's enough time."
Tomas stayed nearby.
Diego continued pointing.
"'Moderate difficulty'? That means nothing. Moderate for who? That guy?" He pointed randomly.
"…That's how descriptions work."
"No, that's how confusing systems are created."
He flipped a marker.
Nothing happened.
"…Unfortunate."
"…Diego, do not reorganize the mission board."
"I am improving it."
"You will get banned."
"…That would be unjust."
Pause.
"…I should test the limits first."
"…Don't."
Too late.
He was already rearranging things.
"You stacked high-reward missions."
"…Yes."
"That's not how difficulty works."
"…It could be."
Tomas rubbed his face.
"…Your family makes more sense now."
"…That's rude."
Diego shrugged.
"My family does make sense. Just not in a way you understand."
That was… new.
"They don't fight. They don't mine. They don't run shops."
"…Then what do they do?"
Diego smiled slightly.
"…We find things."
"Lost items. Broken tools. Rare herbs. Things people swear are gone."
A pause.
"…Sometimes things they didn't know they lost."
"…That sounds like nonsense."
"…It isn't."
"…That sounds illegal."
"…It sounds efficient."
"…You steal things."
"I prefer 'recovery specialist.'"
Tomas sighed.
"…Of course you do."
Diego grinned.
Then—
He glanced toward the forest again.
"…That Mateo kid you spar with…"
Tomas looked at him.
"…Yeah?"
"…He's the type to wander into something stupid, right?"
A pause.
"…Yes."
"…And dangerous."
"…Yes."
"…And valuable."
"…Yes."
Diego nodded.
"…Good to know."
He folded his arms.
"…And we're just standing here."
"…Yes."
"…That feels wrong."
Tomas didn't answer.
Because—
He agreed.

Far below the forest—
Something shifted.
Not awake.
But not still.

Across realms—
Something responded.

And then—
A voice appeared everywhere.

"…HELLOOOOO EVERYONE!!!"

Reality paused just enough to notice.
Then—
A chihuahua appeared.
Robes flowing. Microphone glowing. Presence overwhelming.
"…WELCOME TO THE MOST INCREDIBLE, SPECTACULAR REALM OPENING OF THE ERA!!!"
He spun.
Flourished.
Then suddenly—
Stopped.
His expression shifted.
"…You know…"
A sniff.
"…My master said he'd come back."
Pause.
He looked outward.
"…He did not come back."
Silence stretched.
"…He LEFT me."
His ears drooped.
Then—
He burst into exaggerated wailing.
"I WAS ABANDONED!!! LEFT ALONE!!! FORCED TO HOST A REALM OPENING BY MYSELF!!!"
"DO YOU KNOW HOW HARD IT IS TO HOST EVENTS ACROSS MULTIPLE REALMS BY YOURSELF?!"
Across realms—
Powerful beings stared.
"…Is… it crying?"
"…What are we watching?"
Then—
Instant stop.
The chihuahua straightened.
Sniffed once.
"…Anyway."
Clap.
Back to business.

"In six months—a new realm opens!"

High Realm of Flame
A woman stood at the center of a world of fire. Endless seas of flame surged around her, rising and falling like tides, obeying her presence without question. Flame spirits swirled nearby, small and large, flickering in patterns that resembled reverence more than instinct.
The fireworks reflected across the inferno.
She did not move.
One spirit drifted closer, its flames gathering and shaping—forming the outline of a small child. It reached toward her hesitantly, its form wavering with faint uncertainty.
"…Mother?"
Her expression softened.
"It's alright… my child," she said gently.
She knelt slightly, her hand brushing against the spirit's cheek, the flames bending under her touch rather than burning. The child-form steadied, flickering more clearly as her presence calmed it.
Her gaze lifted after a moment, returning to the sky.
"…No intrusion," she murmured.
A pause.
"…And yet not bound by any law I recognize."
The flames around her rose higher, not violently—but attentively, as though responding to her awareness.
"…Something has announced itself… without asking permission."

The sky distorted.
The air shimmered—
A chihuahua rose from a nearby beach chair, stretching like it had just finished sunbathing, sunglasses slipping down its face. It grabbed a glowing microphone like a drink.
"…Ah. Warm. Finally a realm that understands proper climate control," it said, glancing at the flames approvingly.
It leaned toward the flame spirit. "…Also, excellent formation. Very child-shaped. Strong effort. Ten out of ten."

Veil of Endless Night
In a realm where light struggled to exist, a figure stood wrapped entirely in black. Not cloth—absence. Darkness bent toward him, gathering around his form like something alive, something that recognized him as its center.
Behind him, dozens knelt in silence.
Unmoving.
Watching.
The fireworks tore briefly into the sky above them—strange bursts of color cutting through a world that rejected brightness, leaving lingering distortions as if the darkness itself refused to accept what it was seeing.
None of the kneeling figures moved.
Only one dared to speak.
"My lord…"
The figure did not turn.
"…Should we treat this as an intrusion?"
A long silence followed.
Not hesitation—
Consideration.
The darkness around him shifted subtly, as though listening.
"…No," he said at last, his voice low and controlled.
Another pause.
"…It does not enter."
His gaze lifted slightly—not toward the spectacle itself, but toward the structure through which it appeared, as if observing something beyond the visible layer.
"…It is… present, but not within."
A faint narrowing of his eyes.
"…Something that does not belong to any world… and yet exists in all of them."
The kneeling figure lowered their head further. "Then it cannot be struck."
"Correct."
Another moment passed.
"…Is it after us?"
The darkness folded inward slightly, tightening around him like a blade returning to its sheath.
"…No."
A pause.
"…Just observe."
He remained still, gaze unmoving.
"…Anything that exists beyond structure… is either misunderstood…"
A slight tilt of his head.
"…or far beyond our ability to challenge."

The fireworks faded slowly, leaving the world in its natural suffocating black.
But his gaze did not leave where they had been.
"…Anything that ignores boundaries… either does not understand them…"
A slight tilt of his head.
"…or has already outgrown them."
He said nothing else.
Behind him—
No one moved.

The shadows bent—
A ghostly chihuahua flickered into existence, barely outlined, its form phasing irregularly as if existence was optional.
"…Wow. Dramatic," it muttered, looking around. "…You all rehearsed this, didn't you?"
It leaned toward the figure slightly. "…Honestly, I respect the commitment. Very intimidating. Zero notes."

Lower Coastal Realm
A young cultivator stood knee-deep in shallow water, staring upward as shimmering lights appeared in the open sky above.
"…What… is that?"
An older fisherman nearby didn't answer immediately. He just watched.
"…It doesn't feel dangerous," the younger one said.
"…That's worse," the older man replied.

Remote Mountain Sect
A group of disciples gathered along a stone platform, their training interrupted as the strange lights filled the sky.
"…Is this an omen?"
"…Or an opportunity?"
"…Or a trap?"
An elder stepped forward, hands behind his back.
"…I must discuss this with the others and the Patriarch."

The sky shifted—
A chihuahua stood atop a floating sword, robes flowing perfectly as if it had cultivated for centuries.
It lifted its chin.
"…Yes. Observe closely," it said in a deep, overly serious tone.
A pause.
Then it broke character slightly.
"…I have absolutely no idea what I'm doing, but you all look very impressed, so I'm going to continue."

War-Torn Battlefield Realm
Two armies slowed.
Weapons lowered.
Not from fear—
From pressure.
The soldiers felt it—the change, space folding and shifting, something pressing across existence without entering it.
"…What is that feeling…?"
"…It's not killing intent…"
"…Then what is it…?"
A distant commander shouted:
"FORM RANKS!"
The tension snapped—

The pressure intensified—
Then—
A chihuahua dropped between both armies, wearing oversized general armor, the cloak dragging ridiculously behind it.
It raised one paw.
"…At ease! You're all doing great. Terrible coordination, but excellent enthusiasm," it said, nodding approvingly.
It waved.
Light spread instantly.
Wounds closed. Blood vanished. Broken bodies stabilized.
The battlefield fell silent.
"…Did we just get healed…?"
"…By… that thing?"
The chihuahua adjusted its oversized helmet.
"…Yes. You're welcome. Please remember me fondly and dramatically."

Fragmented Ruin World
A lone cultivator wandered through broken structures that floated out of alignment with each other.
When the fireworks appeared—
He laughed.
"…Finally."
No one heard him.
But his expression sharpened.
"…Something interesting."

Back to the Chihuahua
Then—
The chihuahua spun.
All versions vanished.
Replaced by—
A simple robe.
Sandals.
A pipe in its mouth—
Blowing slow floating bubbles through folded space.
"…WELCOME!!!"

Fireworks exploded—
…but not in one place.
Across countless worlds, across skies that had never shared the same rules, color, or light, the same spectacle appeared. Brilliant arcs of light burst outward in impossible symmetry, illuminating realms that had never once intersected.
And yet—
Something about it was wrong.
The fireworks were there.
And not there.
Reality itself seemed to bend around them, space folding and unfolding like thin paper being shifted just out of alignment. The light spread across distances that should not have existed, touching places without traveling between them. Some saw them clearly, others saw only faint impressions, as if witnessing an echo.
Many could tell immediately—
This was not an attack.
But it was not natural either.
Something was moving.
Not through space—
But through structure.
And for a brief moment… all of it aligned.

Reality complied.

"GLOBAL ANNOUNCEMENT!!! VERY EXPENSIVE!!! HIGH PRODUCTION VALUE!!!"
It gestured wildly.
"Everyone's included! No refunds! No excuses!"

"Treasure! Power! Glory! Bad decisions! Character growth!"
Then—
His gaze shifted.
Focused.
Through layers.
Through space.
Through time.

Into the cave.

Mateo stopped.
Turned.
Looked up.

The chihuahua froze.
"…That's not—"
Mateo stared.
Then—
Smiled.

The chihuahua slowly raised one paw.
And gave him the middle finger.

Silence.

Mateo burst out laughing.
"…Of course you would do that."

Back across reality—
The chihuahua recovered instantly.
"…ANYWAY!"
Tokens appeared everywhere.
"You want in? You get one!"
Grin.
"Prepare if you want."
"…Or don't."
Smile widened.
"I'll see you all soon."

Gone.

Silence returned.
Not fully.
Never fully.

Back in the village—
Tomas stood still.
The ground responded again.
Subtle.
Controlled.

He looked toward the forest.
"…Mateo…"

This time—
There was no doubt.

Something had changed.

And whatever it was—

He was already inside it.
