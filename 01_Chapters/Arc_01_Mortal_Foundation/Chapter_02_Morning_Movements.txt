Morning in the village did not begin quietly.
It began with movement.

Mateo stepped out onto the packed dirt path as the early light filtered through the dense forest canopy that stretched over much of the settlement. The air carried the scent of damp earth and tree bark, mixed faintly with herbs, smoke, and metal—signs of a place that did not separate daily life from cultivation.
The village itself was not large, nor was it designed with symmetry or display in mind. Homes were built from wood and reinforced with stone, shaped more by practicality than uniformity. Paths curved naturally between them, worn into the ground by years of repetition rather than intentionally constructed roads.
No one here had seen the need to replace them with stone.
They could have.
They simply didn’t.

A loud crack echoed nearby.
Mateo glanced to his right as two older trainees clashed near a clearing, their movements rough but purposeful. One stepped too far into the other's reach and was thrown into the dirt with a sharp exhale.
“You’re telegraphing every swing,” the victor said.
“Then stop reading them,” the other shot back, already pushing himself to his feet.
Neither seemed particularly bothered.

	Inefficient… but effective enough for this level.

Mateo continued walking without stopping. His pace was steady, his posture relaxed, though his gaze moved constantly—not in distraction, but in observation. Every motion around him was noted, evaluated, and categorized without conscious effort.
Further along, a modest line of shops marked what passed for a trade path in the village. The structures were simple but well-used, their entrances open, goods displayed with minimal formality.
The herb stall stood as it always did, bundles arranged neatly across a low table. The scent was sharper here—bitter greens, dried roots, and something faintly medicinal drifting into the path.
The woman behind it looked up as Mateo approached.
“You’re earlier than usual.”
“…Marginally.”
She snorted softly. “That’s still earlier.”
Mateo paused briefly, his gaze shifting across the display.
“You moved these,” he said, pointing to a small cluster of leaves set further from the others.
“They dry better apart.”
“…Correct,” Mateo replied.
She studied him for a moment. “…You going out again?”
A short pause followed.
“…Yes.”
She sighed faintly, though her expression carried no real resistance. Reaching beneath the table, she pulled out a small wrapped bundle and held it toward him.
“Take this. You’ll end up needing something you didn’t plan for.”
Mateo accepted it without checking.
“…Efficient.”
“That’s not a compliment.”
“It is.”

He resumed walking.
Behind him, the woman shook her head slightly, though a faint smile followed.

The ringing of metal carried across the path ahead. Mateo approached the blacksmith’s area—a wide, open structure rather than an enclosed shop. Thick beams supported a simple roof, leaving most of the space exposed to air and light.
The ground there was darker, scorched in places from repeated work.
A heavy strike echoed as the smith’s hammer came down against glowing metal, sparks scattering outward in brief arcs before vanishing.
Mateo watched for a moment.

	Alignment is slightly off. Force distribution uneven.

The smith paused mid-motion.
“…You’re staring again.”
Mateo didn’t look away. “…Yes.”
“…What is it this time?”
A brief silence passed.
“…Your last strike drifted.”
The smith stared at him for a long moment.
Then—
“…That’s not helpful.”
“…It was accurate.”
“…That doesn’t make it helpful.”

Mateo considered that.
“…Noted.”

He moved on.

As he passed further into the village, the forest grew denser between structures, the boundary between settlement and wilderness almost nonexistent. It was not uncommon for roots to break through the ground directly between homes or for small creatures to dart across paths without hesitation.
This place did not force nature away.
It adapted around it.

Which, Mateo found, was far more efficient.

“…You adjusted again.”
The voice cut through his thoughts.
Mateo slowed slightly, then stopped, completing the movement he had been running through mentally before turning partway toward the source.
“…Is that a problem?”
Inés stood a short distance away, arms folded, her posture perfectly balanced as always. Her gaze was sharp—not curious, not irritated—simply attentive.
“You correct midway through motion, then stop before finishing,” she said calmly. “You’ve done it repeatedly.”
Mateo exhaled quietly.

	…Observant.

“You’re imagining things.”
“No,” she replied. “I’m not.”
A brief silence settled between them.
Mateo shifted his stance slightly, as though testing whether continuing the discussion held value. It did not seem to.
Before he could dismiss it entirely, another voice entered.
“Then show her properly.”

Mateo turned.

Tomas stood at the edge of the path, a wooden saber resting against his shoulder. Unlike the others nearby, his posture wasn’t loose or reactive—it was measured, controlled in a way that suggested repetition rather than instinct.
Mateo studied him briefly.
“…And you are?”
“Tomas Luján.”
Diego appeared without warning beside Mateo.
“He trains with people from outside,” Diego whispered loudly.
Mateo glanced at him briefly.
“…That explains the posture.”
Tomas ignored the side comment and stepped forward, stopping just within a respectful but clear distance.
“I watched you,” he said. “You’re holding back.”
Mateo tilted his head slightly.
“…And that matters?”
“It does if you plan to improve.”

	Direct. Functional. Not inefficient.

Mateo considered refusing, if only because it would eliminate unnecessary attention. That outcome, however, would likely produce the same result—just slower and less controlled.
Accepting it would be faster.
“…Fine,” he said.
Tomas adjusted his stance immediately.
“…Good.”

They moved to an open section of the path, though it was less a designated training area and more an empty space where no one happened to be standing at the moment. A few nearby villagers shifted slightly to give room, but none left entirely.
Watching was normal.
Intervening was not.

Tomas moved first.

The strike was clean, controlled, and direct. He did not rush the motion, nor did he hesitate. The blade cut forward with clear intent, testing structure rather than overwhelming with force.
Mateo stepped slightly off-line.

	Predictable entry. Good execution. Limited variation.

The second strike followed quickly.
Then a third.

Mateo adjusted with minimal movement, redirecting rather than blocking, stepping in and out of range without disrupting his own balance.
At first glance, it appeared like avoidance.
At second—
It became something else.

“…You’re not retreating,” Tomas said mid-exchange.
“…No.”

	I’m moving around you.

The next opening appeared.

Mateo stepped forward.
Just slightly.

Tomas’ strike extended a fraction too far.

Mateo redirected the motion, shifting its path just enough to break alignment.
Then he tapped Tomas lightly on the shoulder.

“You’re open.”

Silence followed.

Tomas stepped back slowly.
“…Again.”
Mateo blinked once.

	…Persistent.

“…Fine.”

They repeated the exchange.
Again.
And again.

Each time, Tomas adjusted faster. His stance stabilized, his timing refined, his movement less reactive and more intentional.
And each time—
Mateo remained just ahead.

Not dramatically.
Not overwhelmingly.

Just enough.

By the fifth exchange, the tone around them had shifted.
The background conversation had quieted. Even those who weren’t watching directly had begun paying attention.
Something did not match expectation.

Diego, unusually, wasn’t talking.
Inés remained completely still.

“…Something’s off,” someone muttered from nearby.

Mateo ignored them.

Another exchange.
Another shift.

Tomas pushed further this time, tightening his movement, closing distance faster—

Mateo moved to counter—

And something changed.

The pendant beneath his clothing grew faintly warm.

	…Again.

A brief flicker of something surfaced.
Not memory.
Not fully.

But enough.

His body adjusted.
Not consciously.

Sharper.

Cleaner.

The strike broke completely.
Not redirected.
Removed from alignment entirely.

Tomas staggered back a step.
Mateo stopped.

Silence.

“That wasn’t the same,” Tomas said quietly.
Mateo looked down briefly at his hand.

	No… it wasn’t.

“…Forget it,” Mateo replied.

But Tomas did not look convinced.

And somewhere beyond the village paths, beyond the open training grounds and quiet shops—
The forest waited.
