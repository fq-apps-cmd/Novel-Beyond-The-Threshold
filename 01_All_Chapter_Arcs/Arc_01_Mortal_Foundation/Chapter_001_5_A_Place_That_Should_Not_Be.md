# Chapter 1.5 - A Place That Should Not Be

Chapter 1.5  
A Place That Should Not Be  
At the same time, far beyond the Mortal Realm and well past the boundaries that kept one world from bleeding into the next, something shifted.  
It was not loud. There was no explosion, no roar, no visible tear stitched across the dark, no light and no darkness either, nothing that could be pointed at,  
measured, or observed through any means an ordinary being might reach for. And yet something had unmistakably changed.  
What remained was a subtle absence.  
Not emptiness, and not void. Something considerably more precise than either.  
Everything around the region was still arranged for an occupant that was no longer in it. The angles held. The relationships held. Whatever had been there was  
vast, with weight and presence and continuity, and then, without warning and without wreckage, it had ceased.  
Whatever had been there had not been a world. Worlds leave debris. This had left an arrangement with nothing in the middle of it.  
Not destroyed. Not removed. Only no longer existing in relation to anything else.  
Even space did not rush in to fill the gap, and that was the part that should have alarmed someone immediately. Space always fills. It is the one thing it can be  
relied upon to do. Instead it stalled at the border of the region and stayed there, refusing the only behavior it had never once refused.  
The First to Arrive  
The place the disturbance chose was a stretch of the Spirit World that almost nothing bothered to visit anymore.  
It was a high plateau of cracked ochre flats, the ground split into wide irregular plates by ten thousand years of expansion and contraction, each seam packed with  
pale mineral dust that puffed upward at the smallest disturbance and hung far longer in the air than it should have. Standing stone spurs rose out of the flats at  
intervals, worn smooth and slightly translucent along their western faces where centuries of ambient spirit energy had polished them. The air was thin and dry  
enough to crack lips, and it carried a persistent taste of copper and of the moment just before a storm. Every breath left a chalky film across the back of the throat.  
The light came from a sky that never fully committed to a color. It sat somewhere between amber and bruised violet, without a visible sun, so that shadows fell in  
every direction at once and in no direction in particular. Beneath all of it ran a low continuous tone, less heard than felt, the hum of dense spirit energy moving  
through open ground and up through the soles of the feet.  
Then the sky twisted faintly.  
Not in color, and not in shape. In definition. For the length of a breath, the distance between two of the standing stones hesitated. The world was asked how far  
apart they were and could not immediately produce an answer.  
A ripple spread outward from no center at all. It moved through the ground rather than across it, and where it passed, the mineral dust in the seams lifted an inch  
and set back down out of order. The hum faltered, dropped, and caught again, and for one full second the plateau was quieter than it had been in an age.  
Then someone appeared.  
There was no movement to it, no descent, no arrival in any sense involving distance. He was simply there, standing on the flats where nothing had stood a moment  
before, and the plateau's dust settled around his boots without ever having been kicked up.  
A tall man in layered robes of deep azure, hands folded behind his back, hair bound high and severe, a thin scar running white through one eyebrow. From the  
instant he existed within that space, the land began to respond to him. Not shifting, not breaking, but settling, arranging itself around his presence and deferring  
to it. The floating dust that should have hung for minutes dropped straight down within a stride of him, abruptly and all at once. The ambient hum bent into a long  
subtle arc around his position and left a pocket of near-silence at his center.  
The air near his robes went cold. Not unpleasantly, and not sharply. It was the flat, dry, unlit cold of deep stone.  
His gaze fixed forward, on a stretch of air that looked exactly like every other stretch of air. His eyes narrowed.  
"…So it wasn't just me."  
Another Arrives  
A second figure stepped into existence beside him with the same total absence of ceremony.  
A woman, silver-haired, calm in a manner that felt deliberate rather than natural. She had decided a very long time ago that reacting was a habit she could afford  
to discard. Her robes stirred faintly despite the complete absence of wind, the hems lifting and falling in a rhythm that had nothing to do with the air around them.  
The dust nearest her did not settle at all. It drifted in careful orbits at knee height, permitted to come exactly that close and no closer.  
Light bent as it neared her, slowing and diffusing into a soft haze along the line of her shoulder before resuming its path afterward. Standing close to her, the taste  
of the air changed. The metallic edge sharpened into something cleaner and colder, thin and high and faintly sweet.  
"I felt it as well," she said quietly.  
Her voice did not travel. There was no crossing of distance, no faint delay, none of the flattening that the thin air imposed on every other sound out there. It simply  
existed at the place where it was heard.  
Her gaze swept across the expanse ahead of them, careful and unhurried. She was not searching for something visible. She was searching for something that  
should have been there and was not, and she already knew roughly where it should have been.  
"But this…"  
She paused, and the pause carried more weight than the sentence around it.  
"…this is different."  
More Gather  
Others began to arrive, one after another, each appearance identical to the last. No transition, no crossing of distance, no announcement. Only presence, where a  
moment before there had been none.  
Each arrival pressed on the plateau in its own way. The ground beneath one of them frosted in a slow white ring. Another brought the smell of scorched iron that  
clung to the roof of the mouth and would not clear. A third arrived and the hum of the flats jumped a full tone higher and stayed there, an itch behind the ears  
that no one commented on.  
A handful in total. Only those strong enough to have felt that something was wrong in the first place, which in practice meant only those strong enough that most  
worlds treated their arrival as a structural event rather than a social one.  
One of them, broad-shouldered and bare-armed, forearms marked with old burn scars gone silver, let out a low chuckle as he took in the barren flats and the  
entirely unremarkable air. The sound landed strangely. It was heavier than it had any right to be, and it pressed into the space around them rather than moving  

through it, so that everyone present felt it in the sternum a half-beat before they heard it.  
"So this is what called all of us here?" he said, spreading his hands wide enough to crack a shoulder. "I don't see anything. Just empty air."  
The man in blue did not answer immediately. His gaze had not moved once since arriving.  
"…Look again."  
The Investigation Begins  
The silver-haired woman stepped forward, slow and deliberate. Her sandals made no sound on the cracked plates, though the grit beneath them shifted visibly  
with every step.  
She extended her senses outward, not with force, which would have been crude, but carefully, letting her awareness unfold in controlled layers and testing each  
one before committing to the next.  
For a moment, there was nothing at all.  
Then she found a resistance. Not physical and not energetic; she would have recognized either instantly and been bored by both. This was conceptual. A refusal  
occurring one level beneath where refusals were supposed to happen.  
Her brows drew together.  
"…There's a discontinuity," she said. "A region where the laws simply thin out."  
As she spoke, the space ahead of her began, quietly, to misbehave.  
The pale dust drifting on the air slowed unnaturally as it crossed some invisible threshold, individual grains hanging motionless mid-flight while others skipped  
forward in small jerking increments, arriving at positions they had never traveled to. Light crossing the same stretch dulled. Not dimming, exactly, but losing  
coherence, so that the shadows of the standing stones went soft and doubled along their edges.  
The persistent hum of the flats stopped at that boundary, cut flat and clean. So did the smell. The copper and storm taste of the Spirit World reached a certain line  
in the air and simply ended, and past it the air had no character at all. Not clean, not stale, not cold or warm. Air with nothing to say about itself.  
The broad man scoffed and folded his arms across his chest, though he did not step any closer.  
"That's your imagination. Space doesn't just go missing."  
"…No," she replied softly, her gaze never leaving the emptiness. "It doesn't."  
That was precisely the problem.  
The First Test  
He stepped forward anyway, because curiosity had been overriding his instincts for several thousand years and had rarely cost him anything he missed.  
"Let's test that."  
His boots crunched over the plates, dust rising in slow amber curls around his ankles, and he extended a hand into the air ahead of him.  
For the first stretch, everything behaved. Ordinary air with its faint dry drag, the resistance of a world that acknowledged his arm was in it, the cool prickle of  
ambient energy against the hairs of his forearm. Space gave way exactly as space is supposed to give way.  
He pushed further, reinforcing the motion with a measured thread of power, enough to interact with the deeper layers of reality where the honest answers usually  
lived.  
Then he stopped.  
The dust that had been rising around his wrist did not come out the other side. It went in, and the small amber cloud he had stirred up simply ended at a line in  
the air, cut flat.  
"…What?"  
The First Unease  
His hand was still moving. He could feel the muscle and intent of it, could command it forward and watch the tendons shift in his forearm as it obeyed.  
He could no longer tell where it was.  
Not hidden, not resisted, not blocked. His perception simply failed to return anything meaningful. Nothing came back. No distance, no temperature, no boundary,  
no separation between his hand and the thing it was supposedly extending into, because there was no thing.  
Worse, and he noticed this second, was what his skin reported. Nothing. Not numbness, which would at least have been a sensation with a name. His hand had  
crossed out of the category of things that were somewhere, and the nerves in it had quietly stopped filing reports.  
"…There's nothing there."  
The others turned toward him. The man in blue took a step forward, and the frost ring around another cultivator's feet crept outward an inch as their concentration  
slipped.  
"What do you mean by nothing?"  
The broad man tried again, slower this time, more deliberate than he had been in a very long while. He watched his own wrist disappear at the flat line in the air  
and felt his pulse climb in his throat, which was a novelty he did not enjoy.  
"My hand…" He paused, jaw tightening until the muscle stood out. "…it isn't touching anything."  
"That's expected," someone muttered from behind them. "It's empty space."  
"No." His voice hardened, and the sound of it pressed into the plateau again, heavier than before. "It's not space."  
The silence that followed was total, and it was the kind that arrives only when everyone present has understood the same thing at the same moment and would  
strongly prefer not to be the one to say it aloud.  
Because if it were space, there would still be something to perceive. Space carries distance. It carries structure, orientation, relational existence, the simple fact that  
a thing here is a measurable amount of not-here away from a thing over there.  
This carried none of it.  
A Deeper Look  
The silver-haired woman moved closer, and this time she committed everything to it.  
The plateau went quiet around her, not metaphorically but actually. The hum in the ground stopped. The orbiting dust at her knees dropped and stayed down. The  
slow drift of ambient energy across the flats, the current that had run beneath everything out here since before any of them had names and had never once been  
asked to pause, held still and waited.  
In that stillness, the absence of the anomaly became enormous. Every sense they had was pointed at one region of the world, and every sense came back with  
nothing, and nothing had never been this loud before.  
Then her eyes widened, and for the first time in an era, something in her calm broke.  
"…This shouldn't exist."  
The Realization  
The man in blue turned toward her fully, and the pocket of silence around him widened with the movement.  

"Explain."  
She didn't look at him. She hadn't looked away from the region since she began.  
"We all perceive layered space," she said. "We understand distance beyond simple position. We can sense folds, shifts, the transitions between one world and the  
next. We have spent our entire existences learning to read those things."  
A pause.  
"This is not that."  
The broad man crossed his arms again, though the gesture had lost most of its conviction, and he was still flexing the fingers of one hand to confirm they had  
come back correctly.  
"…Then someone moved between layers. Someone strong enough to do it cleanly."  
She shook her head. "No." She turned to face him directly, and the light bent slowly around her shoulder as she moved. "That would leave relation. It would leave  
distance, and trace, and echo. There is always an echo, because moving through a structure means the structure was involved. Something is always left holding the  
door open behind you."  
Her voice lowered.  
"This leaves none."  
She looked forward again, out at the nothing, at the flat cut edge where the dust ended and the world stopped describing itself.  
"There is no coordinate here. Not a hidden one. Not a sealed one. None."  
A breath passed between them, and the thin air made it audible.  
"It is there. And it is not there."  
What They Could Not Accept  
The man in blue exhaled slowly, and the sound was the closest thing to unease he had permitted himself in several thousand years.  
"…Then whatever it was didn't pass through space."  
"Nor through layered traversal," she agreed, and her voice dropped further still. "It existed outside of position entirely, outside the entire question of where, and  
then intersected reality just enough, and just briefly enough, to leave behind this."  
The silence deepened, and out on the flats the standing stones threw their soft doubled shadows in every direction at once.  
These were beings who could perceive beyond worlds, who had stood at the summit of everything they had ever encountered and found the view unremarkable.  
Every one of them felt it.  
The Attempt  
Another figure stepped forward, thin and severe, robes trailing over the cracked plates, the air behind him warping with heat that had no source.  
"I will test it properly."  
Power surged outward from him in a single controlled wave. The plateau answered instantly. Dust blasted outward in a wide ring, the standing stones sang a low  
resonant note as the wave passed over them, and the seams in the ground flared with thin threads of white light. Space bent obediently to accommodate him.  
Reality adjusted. Law responded quickly and without complaint, as it always did for someone of his standing.  
His hand pressed forward into the region.  
And his power vanished.  
There was no impact, no resistance, no transfer of force into anything at all. It did not scatter, it did not rebound, and it did not dissipate. The ring of blasted dust  
arrived at the flat line in the air, and past that line there was no wave, no light, no sound of it continuing. It went in, and it stopped being.  
Behind them, the standing stones were still ringing. Ahead of them, nothing had happened at all, and the contrast made the back of everyone's neck go cold.  
He staggered back a half-step, which he had not done in living memory.  
"…What did you—"  
"No one touched you," the man in blue said, still perfectly calm.  
The thin figure flexed his hand slowly, turning it over, studying it. It was entirely ordinary. It had simply reported back that it had failed at something it should not  
have been able to fail at.  
"…My power didn't reach anything."  
That was substantially worse than failure. Failure meant a wall. Failure meant something on the far side that had refused him, and refusal could be studied, out-  
scaled, and eventually broken.  
This meant there had been nothing to reach.  
True Discomfort  
The broad man frowned, and for once he did not reach for a joke to bury it under. He became aware, distantly, that his mouth had gone dry, and that the copper  
taste of the Spirit World had come back into the air behind him, which only made the tasteless stretch ahead more obvious.  
"…I don't like this."  
Someone behind him spoke, barely above a whisper, and the thin air ate most of it.  
"What kind of existence ignores the structure of worlds entirely?"  
No one answered, because no one knew, and every one of them had spent their entire ascent under the comfortable assumption that they were the ones who  
knew things.  
The Warning  
"We should leave," the silver-haired woman said.  
"Without understanding it?" another challenged.  
"You won't understand it." She said it plainly, without insult. It was not a judgment of him. "Not today. Possibly not ever."  
A pause. The dust at her knees began to drift again, slowly, resuming its careful orbits.  
"And if you push further…" She looked at the region once more, at the not-space that had refused to answer any question put to it. "…I don't know what it will do.  
That is the part you should be listening to. I don't know."  
Back at the Disturbance  
The figures withdrew, one by one, in the same manner they had come. No departure, only the sudden absence of presence. The frost ring on the ground cracked  
and began to melt into the dust. The scorched-iron smell thinned. The hum of the flats slid back down to its old tone, one voice at a time, until it was again the  
sound nobody noticed.  
None of them hurried, because beings at their level did not hurry, and every one of them was aware that not hurrying had just become a decision rather than a  
default.  
The man in blue remained longest.  

He stood alone on the cracked plateau under that undecided amber sky, dust settling wrong around his boots, the taste of copper and coming rain on his tongue  
on one side of the air and absolutely nothing on the other. His gaze stayed fixed on the empty space, eyes narrowing, as though meaning could be forced out of  
something that had already made clear it did not intend to provide any.  
"…Whatever existed here…"  
The pause stretched out across the flats, and the wind that should have filled it did not arrive.  
"…was never part of any world to begin with."  
Then he turned, and was gone, and the region went back to quietly not existing.  
Mateo's Closing Thought  
Back in the Mortal Realm, Mateo sat with his back against the trunk of an old tree at the edge of the village, one knee drawn up, and felt something he had no  
name for yet.  
It was not seeing. Seeing had ended years ago, on the first night, when he reached outward from habit and found the reach stopped a handspan past his own skin  
and stayed there. He had not gotten it back, and he had stopped expecting to.  
This was closer to standing outside a room he used to sleep in. He could not see them. He could not hear them. He simply knew that the place had been entered,  
and that whoever had entered it had found nothing, and had not been able to accept that.  
The awareness lasted the length of a breath and then thinned out, the way everything from before thinned out now.  
The small silver star on its cord had gone cold against his chest, colder than the shade accounted for. He put two fingers against it without thinking about it, the  
way he had since he was old enough to have hands that obeyed him, and after a moment the cold went out of it.  
He rested his elbow against his knee.  
"…Alright."  
A pause.  
"…That answers nothing."  
Around him the village went on with its afternoon. Children shouting somewhere down the lane. Wind moving through the branches above him, dragging the  
smell of woodsmoke and turned earth along with it. A vendor's cart complaining over uneven stone.  
He stood, brushing bark and dust off the back of his clothes, and stretched until something in his shoulder popped in a way he still found novel.  
"…Guess I'll focus on things that actually matter right now."  
From inside the house came a voice with the specific tone of someone issuing a second warning.  
"Mateo! Get inside!"  
He sighed.  
"…Coming."  
A faint smile appeared as he started toward the door.  
"…From beyond existence, to getting yelled at to come eat."  
Another pause.  
"…Not bad."  
He had built worlds where hunger was a law and had never once felt it. Now he felt it every day, at roughly the same time, and had come to look forward to it.  
That still struck him as the strangest thing that had ever happened to him.
