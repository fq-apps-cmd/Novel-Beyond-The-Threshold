The sun had not fully risen when Tomas arrived.
The forest still held most of its shadows, long strips of dim light slipping unevenly through the canopy above. The training yard—if it could be called that—was nothing more than a widened patch of dirt carved out by use rather than design. The ground was uneven in places, marked by shallow grooves, scuffs, and compacted sections where repeated motion had shaped it over time.
Tomas did not call out or announce himself. Instead, he stood at the edge of the yard, his wooden saber resting lightly against his shoulder, silently observing the quiet movements of the boy already there.
Mateo noticed immediately.
He continued his motion regardless—stepping forward, shifting his weight with deliberate precision, and letting his stance settle naturally before coming to a stop. Only then did he glance over his shoulder.
"You're early," Mateo said calmly.
Tomas didn't move. His gaze remained steady. "You're later than you should be."
Mateo tilted his head slightly, glancing briefly toward the sky where the sun had only just begun to rise through the dense spread of trees.
"The sun isn't even fully up."
"Exactly."
A brief silence followed.
Somewhere beyond the yard, a dull thud echoed through the village—someone already training, or possibly arguing in a way that required reinforcement. The sound didn't draw either of their attention.
Mateo sighed quietly, rolling his shoulders as if preparing himself for something unnecessarily repetitive.
	…This is going to be exhausting.
"What are we doing?" he finally asked.
Tomas stepped forward, lowering the saber into a ready position. His stance settled smoothly—no wasted motion, no hesitation.
"No holding back."
Mateo blinked slowly.
	We've already had this conversation.
"Define that," he said.
"Stop correcting only halfway," Tomas answered immediately.
Mateo studied him more closely this time. The change was subtle, but present. There was less impatience than before, less of the need to prove something immediately. Instead, there was focus—intent that had taken shape overnight.
	He's trying to understand it… not rush through it.
	That's good.
	Annoying, but good.
"You won't like the result," Mateo said after a short pause.
Tomas didn't hesitate.
"…Try me."
Mateo exhaled once, then stepped fully into position.
"Fine."

Training began differently that morning.
Tomas slowed everything down.
Instead of pressing forward as he had before, he broke movement into pieces—stance, breath, alignment, then strike. Each part was deliberate, repeated until it felt natural before continuing.
"Like this," Tomas said, demonstrating a controlled swing, then resetting carefully. "Again."
Mateo watched closely, his eyes narrowing slightly.
	Controlled pace… reduced variation.
	He's isolating the movement instead of forcing the outcome.
"…Fine," Mateo said, stepping forward.
They moved together now—step, pivot, strike. The rhythm formed quickly, each repetition smoothing out inefficiency. Dust shifted underfoot, marking their steps in shallow arcs across the already worn ground.
This time, Tomas wasn't trying to win.
He was watching.
Waiting.

"There."
Tomas stopped suddenly mid-motion.
Mateo halted as well, his hand still raised from the previous movement.
"What."
"You changed your footing too early," Tomas said, pointing slightly downward. "Before I committed."
A brief silence passed.
	…He caught that.
Mateo lowered his stance.
"…Yes."
Tomas nodded slowly. "As if you already knew what I was going to do."
Mateo didn't respond immediately.
	Because I did.
"You're predictable," he said instead.
Tomas frowned. "I'm not."
Mateo tilted his head slightly.
"Everyone is."

Before the silence stretched too far, a loud voice cut across the yard.
"HEY! If everyone is predictable, predict this!"
Neither Mateo nor Tomas turned right away.
"Something loud and unnecessary," Mateo replied.
There was a pause.
"…Okay, that's fair," Diego admitted, already halfway through stepping on—and then blaming—a rock that had not moved at all.
Tomas glanced over, watching Diego argue with the ground as though the ground had initiated the conflict.
"…He doesn't bother you?"
Mateo finally looked over.
Diego had now committed to his position, explaining to the rock why it was responsible for everything that had just happened.
"…No," Mateo said calmly. "…He's consistent."
Tomas stared. "That's your standard?"
"At least he doesn't overthink."
Tomas exhaled quietly, something close to amusement flickering across his expression.
"…I see why he likes you."
Mateo blinked once.
	…What does that even mean?

Training resumed, but something had shifted.
It was no longer a test, nor even structured practice.
It had become… exchange.

Tomas adjusted continuously now. His stance refined, his timing tightened, his awareness broadened. He was no longer reacting to what Mateo did—he was beginning to anticipate possibility instead of action.
Mateo allowed more.
Not evenly.
Not completely.
But enough.

	He's improving faster than expected.

"Again," Tomas said.
"You've said that ten times."
"Then we'll make it eleven."
"Persistent," Mateo replied.
"You say that like it's a flaw."
"It depends on the outcome."

Another exchange.
Then another.

At one point, Tomas overextended—only slightly, just a fraction beyond where his balance should have held.
Mateo reacted.

This time, his movement changed.

His hand cut across Tomas' motion with sharper precision than before, faster—not in speed alone, but in certainty.
For an instant—
His awareness narrowed.

Not everything slowed.
Just his perception.

The shift in Tomas' weight.
The angle of the blade.
The space between movement and outcome.

	…Again.

Options unfolded.
Then collapsed.

Mateo moved.

The strike deflected cleanly, breaking Tomas' balance just enough to force him back a step.

Mateo stopped immediately.

Silence settled over the yard.

"That wasn't the same," Tomas said quietly.
Mateo looked down at his own hand.
	No… it wasn't.
"…Forget it."
But Tomas didn't look convinced.

They stepped aside briefly.
Recovering.

Diego had progressed significantly during this time.
Not in training.
In eating.

"Where did you even get that?" Mateo asked, eyeing the food in his hands.
"I brought it," Diego said proudly.
"You brought food… to watch us train?"
"Obviously."
Mateo stared at him.
	There is a strange efficiency to this approach that I may have underestimated.
"You didn't bring enough."
Diego froze mid-bite.
"…For who?"
Mateo gestured slightly—to Tomas, Inés, who had arrived quietly at some point, and himself.
Diego looked at the small amount of food.
Then back up.
"…Wow."
"Yes."
"You're all terrible people."
"Correct."

That night, the village quieted the way it always did—not completely, but enough that sound carried further than intended.
Somewhere in the distance, someone was still training.
Elsewhere, a quiet argument continued.
The forest remained present.
Watching.
Breathing.

Mateo lay still, allowing his body to rest.
Sleep came quickly.
But what followed—
Was not sleep.

Color replaced darkness.
Bright, unnatural bursts of light expanded outward, forming something that felt halfway between controlled creation and excessive presentation. Space twisted—not violently, but with intention.
Constructed.

Mateo blinked.
"…What is this."
A platform formed beneath his feet, smooth and faintly glowing.
Then—
Something dropped from above.
With a soft pop, a small figure landed in front of him.
It stood upright.
Ears too large.
Presence too confident.
A microphone held in one paw.

"…Welcome!" it announced.
Then paused.
Looked around.
Then directly at Mateo.

"…Wait."

Mateo stared back.
	What is that.
The creature narrowed its eyes.
"…You're not supposed to be here yet."
"I could say the same."
A pause.
"…This is a preliminary broadcast," the creature said stiffly. "Please disregard."
Mateo glanced at the microphone.
"…You're holding a device specifically designed to not be ignored."
"…Presentation matters."
"…Clearly."
The creature tapped the microphone.
"…Testing. Testing."
Nothing echoed.
It frowned.
"…We'll fix that."
Mateo folded his arms.
"Who are you?"
The creature straightened.
"I am the Realm Spirit."
"…Of what."
"…Classified."
"…Of course it is."

The creature stared at him.
"…You're very calm about this."
"I've seen worse."
A pause.
Then it sighed.
"…You're going to be a problem later."
Mateo shrugged.
"That seems consistent."
The creature muttered to itself.
"…Entry filters unstable… access controls broken…"
The world flickered.
"…This isn't fully open yet anyway," it said, waving a paw. "Come back later."
"I didn't ask to be here."
"Too bad."

Everything collapsed instantly.

Mateo's eyes opened.
The night returned.
Still.
Quiet.

He remained unmoving, staring upward.
	…That wasn't random.

The pendant rested in his hand.
Warm.

He turned it slowly.

	Fragments.
	Forming… something.

He exhaled quietly and closed his eyes again.
	I'll deal with it later.
A small pause followed.
Then—
	…That thing was ridiculous.

A faint smile appeared.
Then—
Stillness.
Not empty.
Not silent.

Waiting.
