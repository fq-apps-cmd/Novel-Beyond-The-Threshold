The cave breathed quietly around him. Mateo stood just beyond the opened passage, the older air brushing faintly against his senses, carrying with it the weight of something hidden and waiting. His side still ached, his shoulder tightened with each subtle shift, and yet his attention lingered not on the pain—but on the space ahead.
Then his body reminded him. A sharper pull through the ribs. A deeper burn in the shoulder.
Mateo exhaled once. “…Right.”
He shifted toward the cave wall, finding a shallow recess where the stone curved inward. The air here held steadier—less drift, less interference.
Good.
He crouched. Controlled.

The herbs came out first.
Starvein Pulse Vine. Emberveil Bloom. Frostglass Heart Lotus. Lifebloom Verdant Core. Thundertouch Reed.
They rested in his palm, quietly radiant.
Mateo stared at them. Then closed his hand. “…No.”
A small shake of his head. “…That’s excessive.”
He imagined it for a moment—
An alchemy elder watching. A long sigh.
“Child… you used a Lifebloom Verdant Core… to treat a scratch?”
Mateo exhaled lightly. “…They would actually stop breathing.”
He set them aside. Carefully. Respectfully.
“…Not wasting those.”

The second set came out instead. Simpler. Rougher. Correct for the task.
“Plume Moss.” He pressed it between his fingers, watching it collapse into a cool, damp residue. “…Good for stabilizing.”
“Veinbite Root.” Snap. The fibers split cleanly. “…Mild toxin. Restricts flow.” He tilted it slightly. “…If I don’t mess it up.”
“Emberleaf.” He rolled it slowly. “…Burns quickly.” He paused. “…Which means it can go very wrong very quickly.”
“Clearwater Bulb.” He lifted it to the light. “…Weak… but persistent.” A slight nod. “…That’ll hold everything together.”
He placed them on the stone. Looked once. “…That works.”

The fire came next.
A single spark. A breath.
Flame rose—then narrowed, then compressed.
Mateo tilted his head. “…Too wide.”
His fingers shifted slightly—not touching—guiding. The flame obeyed, not because of force, but because space itself tightened subtly around it.
The air near his hands bent. Just slightly. Barely enough to notice.
“…Right. Stay there.”

He began.
The Veinbite Root lifted first. Suspended—not by lifting, but by removing its weight from the immediate space beneath it. It hovered above the flame, turning slow and precise.
“…Break the poison… don’t destroy it.”
The root darkened—not burning, changing.
“…That’s it. Hold that line.”

Next—Emberleaf.
In. Out.
Too fast. Too slow.
Mateo frowned slightly. “…Closer.”
He adjusted the space again—compressing the heat in a narrow arc around the leaf rather than exposing it directly. The edges glowed—then stabilized.
“…There.”

Plume Moss came next. Crushed—but instead of falling, it unraveled.
Threads of pale moisture drifted upward, caught in the distortion Mateo had carved into the air itself. Not random. Not diffuse. Structured.
Mateo blinked once. “…Huh.” Then—a faint smirk. “…That’s convenient.”

He didn’t mix them.
He separated them.
The Veinbite Root stretched into dark strands. The Emberleaf into thin glowing filaments. The moss into soft stabilizing threads. Each piece—distinct.
“…Don’t fight. Just align.”

The Clearwater Bulb came last.
He pierced it.
The liquid didn’t spill. It curved. Flowed.
Thin streams weaving between the others, guiding, binding, stabilizing—without merging too quickly.
Mateo watched it carefully.
“…Water carries. Not controls.”

The structure formed.
Not solid. Not liquid. Something in between.
It rotated slowly, suspended mid-air—woven into a living matrix of energy, heat, moisture, and controlled toxicity.
Mateo’s eyes narrowed slightly.
“…If someone saw this…”
He paused, imagining—
An elder standing silently, watching.
Then—clutching their chest.
Falling sideways.
Mateo winced. “…Yeah. Definitely not showing that.”
A slight pause. “…They’d organize a meeting.”
Another pause. “…And then panic.”

He adjusted it again.
The threads tightened. Moved closer.
“…Too fast and it destabilizes.”
The poison thickened. The fire condensed. The water spread evenly. The moss settled everything into a narrow band of tolerance.
“…Now.”

He closed his fingers.
The entire structure collapsed—folded, compressed—into a dense, uneven paste resting in his palm.
The cave felt different for a moment.
Then—returned.
Mateo exhaled. “…Functional.”

He applied it to his ribs.
Pain followed immediately.
“…Yeah. That makes sense.”
Heat sealed the outer wound. Veinbite Root constricted deeper flow. Clearwater Bulb stabilized cell response. Plume Moss dampened excessive reaction.
Balanced. Barely.
“…That holds.”

The shoulder required more careful layering.
He applied it slowly. In stages.
Press. Wait. Adjust.
“…Don’t overdo it…”
He tightened the cloth around it.

The system settled.
His body responded immediately—not resisting, not compensating.
Integrating.

Mateo leaned back.
“…That’s better.”
A pause.
“…Still inefficient.”

Not the method.
The situation.

The high-tier herbs remained untouched.
Good.

Silence returned.
“…He would like this.”

Tomas would have stepped in already. Taken two steps too far. Stopped. Looked around too seriously—then immediately ruined it.
“Man… that fight was rough,” Tomas’ voice echoed through the memory. “I swear I almost died… at least thirteen times. Possibly more. I lost count after the third time I thought I definitely died.”
Mateo smirked faintly.
“And that stupid wolf—no, I’m not done talking about it—that thing was cheating,” Tomas continued. “I don’t care what anyone says, it was cheating. You saw it. It stepped wrong on purpose.”
A pause.
“…We should go back and beat it up.”
Another pause.
“…Or throw something at it.”
A longer pause.
“…Do we have any of those stink herbs left? The ones that made Diego gag?”
Mateo’s smirk deepened slightly.
“…Or we throw something worse.”
A beat.
“…I’m just saying, if a wolf wants to act like that, it loses the right to complain if we throw poop at it.”
Silence.

Mateo could practically see it—Tomas standing there, completely serious, absolutely convinced this was a valid tactical adjustment.
“…Wrong priorities,” Mateo muttered under his breath.

The memory settled.
Shifted.

From noise—
to stillness.
Then—
it deepened.
Not from the forest.
From the village.
From a night that had passed unnoticed—
except by him.


The memory came.
Not from the forest.
From the village.
From a night that had passed without notice to anyone—
except him.

Tomas’ house had been quiet.
Too quiet for someone who trained as much as he did.
Not peaceful quiet.
Not restful.
The kind that came from complete exhaustion—where the body shuts down because it has no other choice.

Mateo stood outside the window for a long moment, watching.
Not entering.
Not yet.

“…You push too hard,” he murmured under his breath, though no one was there to hear him. “And you still manage to do it inefficiently.”
He tilted his head slightly, studying Tomas through the narrow opening.
“…That’s actually impressive.”

Even from a distance, Mateo could feel it.
Not clearly—not the way he once could.
But enough.

The flow was there.
Strong.
Stable.
Built on a solid foundation.

But forced.

Energy moved through Tomas like water through channels carved too rigidly—paths shaped by will and habit rather than natural alignment.
They worked.
But not well.

Too much pressure in the joints.
Too much force in the turns.
Too little continuity between movement and intention.

“…You’re fighting yourself,” Mateo said quietly. “Half your effort is going into resistance you don’t even realize is there.”
He crossed his arms slightly, still leaning outside the window.
“…Five times the effort…”
A small pause.
“…for half the result.”

He waited.
Not for silence—there was already silence.
For rhythm.

Tomas’ breathing slowed.
Deepened.
Evened out.

Good.

Mateo moved.

The window didn’t creak.
The air didn’t shift.
The floor didn’t acknowledge him.

He stepped inside as if he had always been part of the room.

Tomas didn’t wake.

Mateo approached slowly, stopping just beside the bed.
He studied him again—closer now.
More detail.
More clarity.

“…Too rigid,” he said, almost conversationally. “You’re holding tension where you don’t need it.”
A small pause.
“…If you keep this up, you’re going to plateau early.”

He crouched slightly.
Reached into his sleeve.

The needles were simple.
Thin.
Unremarkable.

But Mateo held one up for a second, turning it slightly between his fingers.
“…You’re lucky I’m bored,” he muttered.
“…Or you’d have to fix this yourself.”

He didn’t rush.
Didn’t guess.

He waited.

Listening.

Not to sound.
To structure.

There—
A slight misalignment near the core.
A subtle restriction along a secondary path.
Too much pressure feeding into the wrong channel.

“…Found it.”

The first needle slid in.

Tomas’ body reacted immediately.
A sharp tightening through his core—
his breathing hitching slightly—
then release.

Not pain.
Adjustment.

Mateo watched carefully.
“…Good. At least your body knows what to do when it’s given a chance.”

Second needle.
A little lower.
Adjusting flow, not forcing it.

Third.
Rotated just slightly.

Each placement deliberate.
Measured.

Not opening meridians.
Not forcing breakthroughs.

Just correcting.

Subtly.

The energy shifted.
Barely.

A blocked point eased.
A pressured line softened.
A current that had been forcing its way through resistance… found a smoother path.

“…Better,” Mateo said quietly.
But he didn’t stop.

He leaned in a bit closer, frowning slightly.
“…Still uneven.”

He changed approach.

Two fingers pressed lightly against Tomas’ wrist.

Not force.
Guidance.

A soft pulse.
Almost nothing.

“…Follow it,” Mateo murmured. “Stop pushing.”

Energy responded.
Slow.
Controlled.
Cleaner this time.

Tomas stirred.
Just slightly.

Mateo adjusted instantly—
reducing pressure,
letting the body take over.

“…No, don’t wake up,” he said calmly. “You’ll just make it worse.”

Another breath.
Another small adjustment.

Then he pulled away.

No surge.
No visible transformation.
No dramatic release of power.

Just—
alignment.

Slightly better than before.

Enough.

Mateo straightened.
Looked down at him for a moment longer.

“…You won’t notice it,” he said quietly. “Not yet.”
A faint smirk touched his expression.
“…But you’ll stop feeling like something’s off.”

He turned toward the window.
Paused.
Then glanced back once more.

“…And when it starts working properly…” he added, softer now, “you’re going to think you finally figured it out.”
A small pause.

“…That’s fine.”

He moved to the window.
Stopped.
Half-turned.

“…Try not to ruin it.”

Then he stepped out.

Gone.

The room returned to silence.

Morning came.

Tomas woke up sore.
He groaned, shifting in his bed.
“…Why do I feel like I got hit by something…” he muttered.
He sat up slowly, rolling his shoulder.
Paused.

“…Huh.”

Something felt…
different.

Not stronger.
Not faster.

Just—
smoother.

He stood.
Took a step.
Then another.

“…That’s weird.”

He shrugged it off.
Like he always did.

Later, he would train.
He would strike.
He would move.

And without realizing it—
things that used to feel forced…
would start working.

A little easier.
A little cleaner.

Over time—
those small corrections would build.
Layer by layer.
Unnoticed.

Until one day—
someone would watch him fight…
and wonder when it happened.

A sleeping dragon.

Back in the cave—
Mateo remained half-present.

Healing continued beneath awareness.
The wounds closed.
The deeper damage stabilized.

He shifted.
Carefully.

No sharp pain.
Only pressure.

“…Better,” he said quietly.
