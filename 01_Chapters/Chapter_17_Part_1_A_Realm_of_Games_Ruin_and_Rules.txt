The fireworks had faded, but their absence did not restore normalcy.
Deep within the cave, Mateo remained still, one hand resting lightly against the rough wall beside the newly opened passage, the other close to the small cube hidden in his clothing. The laughter had not fully left him yet. It lingered in the edge of his expression, in the quiet ease of his breathing, in the simple fact that something so absurd had spoken across worlds and then responded to being noticed by insulting him directly.
That alone made the entire event worthwhile.
He looked upward again, though there was no real "up" to look toward in the cave. The folded fireworks were gone. The pressure of the announcement had softened. And yet a faint trace lingered, as though a thread of impossible structure still hung through the world.
Mateo exhaled once, almost amused.
"…So you can see me."
The answer came immediately.
"Oh, I can do many things," the Chihuahua said, its voice appearing in the cave without echo, as wrong in its presence now as it had been during the announcement. "Seeing you was not supposed to be one of them."
Mateo's smile widened slightly. "…Unfortunate."
"It is deeply inconvenient," the spirit replied with obvious offense. "You are a low-level cave boy. Cave boys should not look back through cross-realm broadcast layers. That is against basic dramatic hierarchy."
Mateo tilted his head. "…And yet."
"And yet," the Chihuahua repeated bitterly. "Here we are. You laughing. Me being disrespected. Reality losing standards."
Mateo let out a quiet breath that might have become another laugh if he allowed it.
"…You started it."
The spirit gasped. "I gave you a gesture of divine contempt. That is not the same thing."
"…You gave me the middle finger."
"Yes," said the Chihuahua with dignity. "A sacred sign of organizational displeasure."
Mateo looked deeper into the darkness of the passage, then back toward the impossible source of the voice.
"…You're ridiculous."
"And you're in a cave bleeding on old stones while talking to a cosmic event host. Let's not become judgmental."
For a brief moment, neither of them spoke.
Then the Chihuahua sighed dramatically, as though burdened by the intellectual limitations of entire realms.
"Well," it said, recovering its tone instantly, "since you are apparently capable of noticing things you should not notice, I suppose I can allow you a slightly better explanation than everyone else is getting."
Mateo's eyes narrowed just slightly.
"…That seems generous."
"It is," the spirit replied. "You should appreciate it more."
Then, without warning, its tone shifted—not into seriousness exactly, because seriousness was not natural to it, but into something larger, more deliberate, as though it were speaking from a stage too vast for ordinary thinking.
"The realm opening," it said, "is not a single realm."
The cave seemed to listen.
"It is many realms. Many worlds. Many structures. Some were made. Some were found. Some were stolen, improved, repurposed, abandoned, remembered, forgotten, or all of those at once. Some are tiny. Some are larger than nations. Some are folded over each other. Some only exist if entered correctly. Some reject entire bloodlines. Some change their rules the moment they realize someone has started understanding them."
Mateo remained silent.
The Chihuahua continued.
"Long ago," it said, "there was… something. A being. One that could see too much, understand too much, do too much. It could shape realities the way normal creatures shape intention. It could witness worlds without crossing them. Build laws. Break laws. Refine them. Remove them. Rearrange possibility until even impossibility became administrative."
A pause drifted through the cave.
"But power has a sense of humor," the spirit said lightly. "The more complete that being became, the less any ordinary world could hold it. Too much awareness. Too much force. Too much structure in one place. It became trapped—not by chains, not by enemies, not by any external prison, but by the sheer fact that it had become too great to fit anywhere except a sealed existence of its own making."
Mateo's expression did not visibly change.
The Chihuahua, very deliberately, did not look directly at him when it said the next part.
"So the sealed place remained," it continued. "Not dead. Not fully alive. Just… there. A prison formed from excess and intention. And inside or around or adjacent to that structure—depending on which version of the truth you prefer—many lesser worlds, trials, spaces, and game-like domains came into being."
The spirit took a small drag from the pipe in its mouth and blew a stream of bubbles into the air. They floated briefly, then vanished into folded space.
"That," it said, "is the realm opening."
Mateo finally spoke.
"…A prison."
The Chihuahua sniffed. "A prison is a very unfriendly word for what is, in many cases, a truly excellent collection of opportunities."
Another pause.
"…Also yes."
The spirit's voice sharpened again, becoming more animated as it continued.
"There are worlds inside it where cultivation behaves differently from anything your current realm accepts. Some allow no spiritual energy at all and force everyone who enters to begin as mortals, stripped of everything except instinct, memory, and whether they panic attractively. Some are race-locked, meaning if you enter incorrectly you may emerge with scales, fur, wings, or deeply inconvenient social obligations."
Mateo blinked once.
"…Inconvenient."
"Oh yes," the Chihuahua said. "Some worlds are perfectly serious death trials with points and rankings and reward systems. Some are dungeon realms with shifting floors, hidden bosses, contribution tallies, and participants who believe teamwork means stealing from one another politely. Some are hunt worlds where beasts are the points. Some are herb worlds where enough ignorance can get you eaten by medicinal plants."
A beat.
"Which, to be fair, is usually deserved."
The cave remained quiet.
"Some worlds are games," the spirit went on. "Actual games. Strategy games. Survival games. Hide-and-seek worlds. Racing worlds. Tactical siege worlds. Funny games. Horrible games. Games that begin funny and stop being funny very quickly. Some worlds are made for fighters, yes—but not all of them. There are worlds for blacksmiths where spiritual metals only reveal themselves through proper forging logic. Worlds for healers where preserving a single thread of life matters more than winning. Worlds for alchemists where ingredient compatibility changes every hour. Worlds for rune makers where structure itself is the enemy."
The Chihuahua paused again, then added in a much more conversational tone, "Some are even cooking worlds, though personally I consider those cruel."
Mateo let out a soft breath through his nose.
"…Of course there are."
"Of course," the spirit said. "Why should existence limit itself now, after being absurd for so long?"
It shifted again, bubbles drifting from the pipe as it spoke.
"There are worlds of mythical herbs, where entire valleys shine with living medicine and every plant has enough instinct to resent harvesting. There are spirit marshes where ghost-lotuses bloom beneath moonless skies. Forge worlds where mountains bleed ore. Beast plains where creatures large enough to flatten cities wander around considering whether your bones are worth chewing. There are cities that only exist during certain alignments, forests that move when watched, lakes that reflect other realms, towers that reward failure correctly interpreted, and ruins that can only be opened by people too stubborn to know they should have left."
Mateo's gaze shifted slightly toward the passage ahead.
That last one felt pointed.
The spirit continued anyway.
"Each world has rules," it said. "Some are obvious. Most are not. Some reward force. Some punish it. Some favor talent. Some absolutely despise it. Some elevate the weak if they adapt correctly and bury the strong if they arrive with too much certainty. A few are simply mean."
The voice softened just enough to become almost thoughtful.
"And all of them were shaped by a place born from too much power belonging to one being in one sealed space for too long. That kind of pressure changes things. Leaves traces. Makes oddities. Creates wonders. Creates monsters. Creates opportunity."
Then, abruptly, the Chihuahua's normal nonsense returned.
"So naturally," it said brightly, "I turned it into a six-month event."
Mateo actually laughed again.
"…Naturally."
"You say that like you question my management."
"I question all of your management."
"Very rude," said the spirit. "And impressively accurate."
For a moment, the cave felt less oppressive.
Then the pressure beneath the floor pulsed faintly again, reminding both of them that the world below had not stopped existing simply because ridiculous explanations were being offered above it.
Mateo's expression settled.
"…And what happens if people die in those worlds?"
The Chihuahua took a thoughtful pause.
"That depends on the world," it said. "Some expel you. Some keep score. Some kill you normally. Some destroy only what entered. Some return you altered. Some consider death a lesson. A few consider it a prerequisite."
A beat.
"Very educational realms."
"And all of them open in six months?"
"Yes."
"And everyone was invited."
"Yes."
"That seems irresponsible."
The Chihuahua sounded offended.
"It is inclusive."
Mateo let the silence answer for him.
The spirit ignored that.
"In any case," it said, "none of this changes your immediate problem. You are still in a cave. There is still something old beneath you. And despite your current injuries, I suspect you are going deeper anyway."
Mateo glanced toward the narrow opening in the wall, where colder air drifted from below.
"…Yes."
"Of course you are."
The Chihuahua's voice shifted once more, playful again.
"Well then. Try not to die before the event. It would be tremendously inconvenient for the narrative."
"Is that concern?"
"No," said the spirit. "That is scheduling."
Then it vanished.
Completely this time.
No bubbles.
No pressure.
No trace except the lingering sense that something impossible had just finished treating the laws of reality like suggestions.
The cave returned.
The silence returned.
The old air remained.
Mateo stood there for a few moments without moving, letting the shape of the explanation settle where it could. A realm born from excess. Many worlds inside it. Games, trials, craft domains, death spaces, cultivation anomalies. A prison, a treasury, a battlefield, a joke.
And somehow—
He believed it.
Not because it was reasonable.
Because too much of it felt structurally true.
His fingers brushed lightly against the pendant hidden beneath his clothing. It pulsed once, warmer now, as if the mention of all those worlds had touched something buried within it.
"Interesting," he said softly.
Then he turned fully toward the opened passage.
The stone ahead narrowed into darkness, but not natural darkness. This one felt built, layered, patient. The faint cold rising from below no longer carried only age. It carried attention.
Waiting.
Mateo adjusted his breathing, then his stance, letting the injuries in his side and shoulder settle into the kind of awareness that could be moved with rather than ignored. The herbs within the cube rested quietly, separated and stable. His body was not fully recovered.
It was enough.
He stepped forward.
The passage beyond the wall did not descend cleanly. It bent, split, and narrowed, the stone changing from rough cave structure into something more deliberate. Dust lay thick in some places and absent in others. The farther he went, the more the space stopped feeling abandoned and started feeling… arranged.
The cave was over.
Whatever this place was, it had begun.
And now, without chihuahuas, fireworks, or impossible jokes to distract him, Mateo did what he always did when faced with something hidden, old, and potentially catastrophic.
He went deeper.
