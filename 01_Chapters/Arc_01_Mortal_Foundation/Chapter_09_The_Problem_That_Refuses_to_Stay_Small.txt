Mateo was ten years old.
That fact explained very little.
At an age where most children were still refining basic stances and struggling with breath control, Mateo had already begun to understand the boundaries of the systems around him—not just the physical ones, but the social ones as well. He did not push recklessly, nor did he challenge openly unless it served a purpose.
Most of the time, he simply… ignored limits until someone proved they mattered.
This approach had worked well so far.
It was beginning not to.

“They’ve come.”
Tomas stood beside him near the edge of the training yard, where the packed dirt met the uneven roots of the surrounding forest. His posture was more alert than usual. Unlike the previous visitors, there was no curiosity in his voice this time.
Only certainty.
Mateo didn’t stop what he was doing. He moved through a slow sequence, each step deliberate, each motion simple on the surface but precise underneath. The ground beneath him held steady, though the surrounding space felt slightly more rigid than before—as if tension had already settled ahead of conflict.
“…Yes,” he replied. “I noticed.”
Across the yard, a small group stood still—not drifting, not observing casually, but waiting. Their attention did not move.
It remained fixed.
On him.

Diego, who had claimed he was “just passing through,” leaned in slightly.
“…They don’t look friendly.”
“They’re not,” Tomas said before Mateo could answer. “They’re from the Varex family.”
Mateo blinked once.
“…The herb shop.”
Diego winced.
“…Oh, that’s not great.”
A pause.
“…Wait,” Diego added, quieter now. “…didn’t you—”
“Yes,” Mateo said calmly.
“…That’s worse.”
 Expected outcome.

Mateo completed his movement, lowering his stance before straightening calmly.
“…I took very little.”
“…That’s not the point,” Tomas said.
“…Correct,” Mateo replied. “…The point is that I wasn’t supposed to be there.”
Diego nodded eagerly.
“…You’re learning.”
Mateo ignored him.

The group approached slowly.
At their front stood a boy slightly older than Mateo, perhaps twelve or thirteen, his expression composed in the way of someone used to being obeyed. His clothes were cleaner than those worn in the village, his stance unhurried—not because he lacked urgency, but because he expected others to wait.
Behind him, two older attendants stood in silence.
Watching everything.
Saying nothing.

“We’ve been looking for you,” he said.
Mateo tilted his head slightly.
“…That seems to be a recurring issue.”
The boy’s gaze narrowed faintly.
“You entered one of our family’s shops.”
“Yes.”
“You took materials.”
“Yes.”

A brief pause followed.
The boy studied him more closely now.
“…You’re not denying it.”
Mateo considered the question.
“…Was there uncertainty?”
Silence stretched.
One of the attendants shifted slightly.
Not discomfort.
Assessment.

Diego stepped slightly behind Mateo.
“…This is going badly.”
 No, it’s proceeding as expected.

“We’ll settle this properly,” the boy said at last. “You’ll return what you took and compensate for the intrusion.”
Mateo shook his head slightly.
“…Returning what I took would reduce its effectiveness.”
The boy frowned.
“…Excuse me?”
“I’ve already used part of it,” Mateo said calmly. “Returning it would be inefficient.”
Diego whispered loudly, “You’re negotiating the wrong part of this.”

Tomas exhaled slowly.
“…Mateo.”
Mateo didn’t look at him.
“…Yes.”
“…We may need to approach this differently.”
 Agreed.
“…Define differently,” Mateo said.

The boy spoke before Tomas could respond.
“…You think this is something you can talk your way through,” he said. “It’s not.”
His tone shifted slightly.
Less measured.
More personal.
“…Do you know who I am?”
Mateo looked at him.
“…Yes.”
A pause.
“…You told me.”
The boy’s expression tightened.
One of the attendants almost smiled.

“I am from the Varex main branch,” the boy continued, lifting his chin slightly. “This village exists because families like mine allow it to.”
That drew attention.
Not loud.
But sharp.

“…That is inaccurate,” Mateo said calmly.
Silence.
Even Tomas blinked.

“What did you say?” the boy asked.
Mateo didn’t shift.
“…Your influence extends here,” he clarified, “…but existence is not dependent on permission.”
The boy stared at him.
Longer this time.

“…You speak like you understand things you shouldn’t,” he said.
Mateo considered that.
“…That happens frequently.”

A Measured Conflict
The boy stepped forward slightly.
“You think this is beneath you,” he said. “It’s not.”
Mateo studied him.
 Controlled. Not reckless. Expecting submission.
“…Then how would you like to proceed?” Mateo asked.
“…We settle it here.”
Diego blinked.
“…Oh. That means fighting.”
“…Yes,” Tomas said quietly.
 Predictable escalation.
Mateo nodded once.
“…Alright.”

Tomas stepped forward immediately.
“…Stay behind me.”
Mateo blinked.
“…Why.”
“…Because they’re not the same as before.”
A short pause followed.
Then Mateo said,
“…That is correct.”
Tomas stopped.
“…You agreeing with me does not make this better.”
“…I didn’t say it would.”

The Exchange
The fight began quickly.
Not chaotic. Not loud.
But controlled.
Deliberate.

Tomas intercepted the first strike.
Clean.
Measured.
His movements had improved—no longer reactive, but anticipatory. The weight of his stance held even through impact.
Mateo watched.
 Improvement confirmed.

The opposing boy moved well.
Better than most in the village.
Not overwhelming—
But structured.
 Core refining stage approaching… not complete.

Tomas pushed him back slightly.
Not enough to win.
Enough to create space.

Mateo stepped in.
Briefly.
Not to dominate.
To adjust.

A deflection.
A shift.
A disruption in timing that the opponent noticed too late.
Then—
He stepped back.

“…What was that?” Diego whispered.
“…Help,” Tomas replied.
 Minimal involvement. Maximum efficiency.

The opposing group hesitated.
Not because they were losing.
But because something didn’t align.

“…You’re not fighting seriously,” the boy said.
Mateo considered that.
“…Correct.”
“…Why.”
A pause.
“…Because I don’t need to.”

Silence.
Tighter this time.

Resolution Without Escalation
The tension peaked.
But it did not break.

The boy held Mateo’s gaze for a long moment.
Longer than before.
Less anger.
More calculation.

“…You’re not worth escalating over herbs,” he said.
Mateo nodded.
“…That is also correct.”

But the boy didn’t move immediately.
Instead—
“…There’s someone else coming through this region,” he said casually.
That line shifted everything.
Slightly.
Subtly.
But enough.

“…Someone who won’t be as… restrained.”
The attendants behind him glanced at each other briefly.
Then stilled.

Tomas frowned.
“…What does that mean.”
The boy smiled faintly.
“…It means this village is about to become inconvenient.”
Mateo watched him closely.
 Additional variable introduced.

Then the group turned and began to withdraw.

“…You’re drawing attention,” the boy said without looking back. “Whether you want it or not.”
Mateo nodded once.
“…I’m aware.”

There was a brief pause after they left.
Not silence.
More like the space hadn’t decided how to settle yet.
“…We should tell the elders,” Tomas said quietly.
Mateo shook his head slightly.
“…They already know.”
Tomas frowned.
“…How.”
Mateo glanced toward the distance, not toward the departing group—but past them.
As if measuring something further out.
 Because they’re adjusting faster than before.
“…They were expecting this,” he said.

Aftermath
The yard slowly returned to something resembling normal.
Not fully.
Not quite.
Something had shifted.
And this time—
It wasn’t local.

Tomas turned to Mateo.
“…You could have ended that faster.”
“…Yes.”
“…Why didn’t you.”
Mateo thought briefly.
Then answered simply:
“…I prefer slower outcomes.”
“…That doesn’t make sense.”
Mateo turned slightly, beginning to walk.
“…Faster outcomes create larger problems.”
A pause.
“…Slower ones let you observe.”

Tomas stood still for a moment.
 That… makes sense.
 …Why does he say it like that.

Diego ran up beside them.
“…You’re both overthinking it. That was just you being lazy.”
Mateo glanced at him.
“…Also correct.”

A Quiet Reinforcement
That evening, Mateo sat alone again.
The pendant rested in his hand.
It pulsed steadily now.
More consistently.
Stronger.

 Something is accelerating.

He watched it carefully.
 Fragments aligning… faster.

The memory surfaced again.
Clearer.
Closer.

The forge.
The sound of metal.
The steady rhythm of shaping something ordinary into something precise.

This time—
It lingered slightly longer.

Then faded.

Mateo exhaled quietly.
“…Not yet.”

At ten years old, he was still early.
Still consolidating.
Still choosing.
 There’s no need to rush.

And yet—
 If I did…

The thought didn’t finish.
It didn’t need to.

Mateo closed his hand around the pendant and leaned back slightly.
“…Later.”

For now—
He would take things slowly.
Even if everything around him—
Was beginning to speed up.
