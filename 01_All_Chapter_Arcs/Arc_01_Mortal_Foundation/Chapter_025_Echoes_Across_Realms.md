# Chapter 25 - Echoes Across Realms

Chapter 25 — Echoes Across Realms  

But the explanation did not end with Mateo.  
Across the countless realms the announcement had touched — from the lowest mortal worlds where cultivators still measured progress in Body Tempering stages  
to the highest planes where beings who had transcended physical form existed as structures of pure intent — questions followed. The broadcast had not been a  
whisper. It had been a declaration, delivered with the subtlety of a firework and the reach of a natural law. And declarations, by their nature, demanded response.  
The Realm Spirit had expected this.  
It had, in fact, been counting on it.  
World of Endless Waters  
Far beneath a shifting ocean sky, where the boundary between water and air existed more as a suggestion than a fact, currents moved in deliberate spirals around  
a massive form. The ocean here was not the ocean that mortals knew — it was deeper, denser, saturated with ambient energy that gave the water itself a  
luminescence that shifted between deep blue and pale silver depending on the depth and the mood of whatever governed its tide s.  
Blue scales, each the size of a shield, rippled with quiet power as a colossal python coiled around a submerged ruin. The ruin itself was ancient — columns of  
carved stone rising from the ocean floor like the bones of a civilization that had drowned so long ago that even the memory o f its name had dissolved into the  
current. The python had been here when the ruin was built. It would be here long after the ocean decided to reclaim it.  
Its eyes opened — ancient, steady, aware. Two points of luminance in the deep water that carried the specific quality of intelligence that existed beyond what  
most beings could process. It had felt the announcement. The fireworks had passed through water without resistance, which should not have been possible —  
water attenuated energy, dispersed it, broke it into fragments that lost coherence with distance. These fireworks had not lost coherence. They had not even  
acknowledged the water's presence.  
That alone was unacceptable.  
The python spoke, its voice vibrating through the ocean itself — not as sound, but as pressure, each word arriving as a wave that traveled through the water and  
through the stone and through the scales of every creature within range.  
"...If this is a realm... what governs entry?"  
The water stilled. Not gradually — completely. As though the ocean had been given an instruction it could not refuse.  
Then — the Chihuahua appeared.  
Not standing. Floating. Treading water that did not exist with the casual indifference of something that had decided the laws of buoyancy were optional. For  
exactly half a second, it wore a lifeguard outfit — red shorts, a whistle on a lanyard, a rescue float tucked under one arm — the ensemble so absurd against the  
backdrop of the ancient ocean and the colossal serpent that the visual itself constituted a form of cosmic disrespect.  
Then it spun lazily, and the outfit dissolved back into its usual robe and pipe.  
"...Excellent question, large and dramatically scaled aquatic individual," it said, blowing a bubble that drifted upward through the water as though it had forgotten  
how gravity worked. Or perhaps it had simply decided that gravity did not apply to bubbles produced by cosmic entities. The distinction was academic.  
A tiny pause.  
"...Also, very good timing."  
The python did not react. It had existed for long enough that reacting to absurdity was no longer in its behavioral repertoir e. Absurdity was acknowledged,  
cataloged, and dismissed. This particular absurdity was being cataloged more thoroughly than most.  
"...Entry is simple," the spirit continued, its tone shifting from theatrical to informational with the practiced ease of someone who had delivered this briefing across  
multiple realms and was now operating on autopilot that was somehow still entertaining. "If you have a token, you enter. If y ou do not, you do not. If you are  
strong enough to cheat the system..."  
It squinted slightly — the specific squint of someone who had already dealt with cheaters and was preemptively exhausted by the prospect of dealing with more.  
"...please don't. It's very inconvenient for bookkeeping."  
A pause.  
"...Power will not determine entry."  
Another.  
"...But it will determine what happens after."  
The python's eyes narrowed — the slow, deliberate constriction of pupils that were each larger than the Chihuahua's entire body. "...So the strong are not  
favored?"  
The Chihuahua grinned — the wide, unrestrained grin of something that found the question delightful not because the answer was simple but because the  
answer was not.  
"...They are noticed."  
A beat.  
"...Sometimes that's worse."  
Fragmented Ruin World  
A lone cultivator stepped carefully across shifting stone platforms suspended in empty air. The world around him was broken — not destroyed, but disassembled,  
as though someone had taken a continent apart and rearranged its pieces at varying heights without any consideration for how the inhabitants might feel about  
walking on surfaces that had no visible support and no logical reason to remain where they were.  
The fireworks had reflected across surfaces that were not aligned with each other, crossing distances that should not have connected, the light bending around  
corners that did not exist in normal geometry. The cultivator had watched them with the careful attention of someone who survived in environments like this by  
noticing things that did not belong — and the fireworks had not belonged so thoroughly that their presence had been almost offensive.  
He spoke quietly, his voice carrying only as far as the nearest platform. "...If these worlds have rules... can they be broken?"  
The Chihuahua appeared sitting cross-legged in midair beside him, legs folded, posture relaxed, chewing thoughtfully on something that did not exist. It had  
materialized without transition — one moment the space beside the cultivator was empty, the next it contained a small creature with large ears and an expressio n  
of philosophical consideration that it had no right to possess.  
"...Everything can be broken," it said casually, as though discussing the weather in a world that did not have weather. The words carried the weight of experience  
rather than theory — the statement of something that had watched many things break and had formed opinions about the process.  

A beat.  
"...But some things break back harder."  
The cultivator paused. His footing shifted on the platform — a subtle adjustment that communicated more wariness than his voice had. "...So there is risk."  
"...Oh, absolutely," the Chihuahua replied, its tone brightening with the specific enthusiasm of someone whose favorite topic had just been raised. "There are  
entire worlds designed to punish overconfident people."  
A pause.  
"...Those are my favorites."  
A tiny shift in tone — barely noticeable, the way a blade's edge was barely noticeable until it cut.  
"...They're very educational."  
Mountain Sect  
In a hall carved from the mountain itself, where the stone walls held the accumulated spiritual pressure of generations of sword cultivators and where the air  
vibrated faintly with the residual intent of ten thousand practice strikes, an elder finally spoke.  
He was old — not in the way that villagers were old, worn by weather and work, but in the way that cultivators were old, refined by decades of compression until  
what remained was denser than what had been removed. His robes were simple. His sword rested across his knees. His expression carried the specific stillness of  
someone who had killed enough to find killing uninteresting and who now evaluated everything through the lens of whether it deserved his attention.  
"...What determines reward?"  
The Chihuahua reappeared — balanced atop a floating sword that hovered at eye level, its tiny legs dangling over the edges, its pipe trailing bubbles th at drifted  
through the hall's heavy air with cheerful disregard for the atmosphere. Its posture had shifted subtly from the previous app earances. The humor did not vanish —  
it never vanished — but it sharpened, the way a comedian's delivery sharpened when the audience was one that would not tolerate imprecision.  
"...For those who walk the sword path," it said, voice lowering slightly — not in volume but in register, the words arriving with more weight per syllable than the  
preceding banter had carried, "reward is not as simple as gain."  
The air grew still. The disciples who lined the hall's walls — dozens of them, each one carrying a blade, each one trained to perceive intent through posture — did  
not move.  
"...There are worlds within this opening that respond only to intent sharpened by discipline. Realms where sword spirits wander without masters, waiting for  
something worthy enough to resonate with. Places where fragments of will — left behind by those who cut through their limits — still linger. Watching. Rejecting.  
Or acknowledging."  
The disciples did not move. The elder's expression remained unchanged. But the quality of attention in the room had shifted — from evaluative to absorptive, the  
way stone absorbed water, taking it in without showing the change on the surface.  
"...There are sword tombs," the spirit continued. "Fields filled with blades of every grade and history. Weapons that remembe r the hands that held them. Some will  
tremble when approached. Some will reject you outright. A few..."  
A small pause — deliberate, timed with the precision of someone who understood that silence was a blade too.  
"...will test you."  
Its gaze drifted slightly — not toward any disciple in particular, but toward the space between them, as though addressing not the people but the potenti al they  
carried.  
"...And there are those who came before. Legends. Not alive in the way you understand, but not entirely gone either. Echoes o f mastery bound into trials, waiting  
for someone to challenge them — not with strength alone..."  
A slight pause.  
"...but with comprehension."  
A longer one.  
"...Those who fail are cut down. Correctly."  
Silence took hold of the hall. The elder's expression shifted — just slightly. A fractional tightening around the eyes that communicated more than words could  
have. He had spent his life pursuing the sword path. He had reached heights that most cultivators could not imagine. And the Realm Spirit had just described a  
space where his life's work could be tested against the echoes of people who had gone further.  
The Chihuahua leaned back on the floating sword, the seriousness cracking again at the edges like ice on a warming river.  
"...Context," it added.  
"...That is not helpful," the elder said.  
"It is extremely helpful," the spirit corrected. "You just don't like it."  
A pause.  
"...Some worlds reward survival. Some reward progress. Some reward technique. Some reward creativity. Some reward not dying i n a particularly impressive way."  
A longer pause.  
"...And some worlds reward things you will not understand until it is too late to regret them."  
The disciples went silent.  
Veil of Endless Night  
In a space where light did not exist because light had been forbidden — not extinguished but prohibited, the darkness itself carrying the specific density of  
something that had been chosen rather than suffered — a figure sat upon a throne that was visible only because it was darker than the darkness around it.  
One kneeling figure, positioned at the base of the throne with the precise posture of someone who understood that proximity t o this particular being was  
measured in risk rather than distance, dared to ask:  
"My lord... should one such as us participate?"  
Vael Noctis said nothing. His silence was not empty. It was full — occupied by a presence so dense that the darkness around him bent toward it the way light bent  
toward gravity. He had heard the announcement. He had felt the fireworks. He had assessed the Realm Spirit's capabilities and found them irritating in the specific  
way that only something genuinely powerful could irritate something that considered itself supreme.  
The Chihuahua appeared behind him. Not in front. Not beside. Behind.  
The positioning was deliberate. Appearing in front of Vael would have been a challenge. Appearing beside him would have been an equivalence. Appearing  
behind him communicated something else entirely — the specific message that the Realm Spirit existed in a space that Vael's awareness should have covered and  
that it had occupied without permission, detection, or concern.  
"...Oh, you absolutely should," it said cheerfully.  
Silence deepened — not the absence of sound but the presence of something that consumed it.  
"...People like you make things very interesting."  
A faint pause.  
"...And occasionally very short-lived."  

Another pause — subtle, carrying the specific weight of a warning delivered through comedy.  
"...But you already knew that."  
Vael did not move.  
But the darkness around him folded slightly — a response that was not physical but structural, the environment itself reacting to an emotional state that its master  
would never admit to possessing.  
Flame World  
In a world where fire was not an element but a medium — where the air itself burned at a temperature that should have been lethal but that the inhabitants had  
adapted to the way fish adapted to water — a small flame spirit hovered beside a woman whose calm was so complete it bordered on geological.  
The flame spirit spoke again, its voice carrying the wavering uncertainty of something young enough to still ask questions th at older beings had learned to avoid.  
"...Will it hurt?"  
The woman's hand remained steady against its small form — a gesture of comfort that she maintained without visible effort, her spiritual energy dampening the  
ambient heat around the spirit to a level it could tolerate.  
The Chihuahua had materialized in a beach chair. The beach chair should not have existed in a world made of fire. Neither should the tropical drink in its paw,  
which glowed faintly and which the spirit sipped with the relaxed contentment of something on vacation from the responsibilit ies it had just created for itself.  
"...Yes," it said immediately.  
Then paused — the specific pause of someone who had answered honestly and was now deciding how much to soften the honesty.  
"...But only the parts you're still attached to."  
A brief glance upward — toward the burning sky, toward whatever passed for heaven in a world where the ground was fire and the air was flame and comf ort was  
a concept that required active spiritual effort to maintain.  
"...Which tends to be the important parts."  
The flame spirit wavered — its form flickering, the edges of its luminance dimming briefly in the way that fire dimmed when uncertainty touched it.  
The woman did not waver. Her expression remained as still as the stone that fire could not touch — not because she did not feel concern, but because she had  
decided that concern was a response she could not afford and that stability was what the spirit beside her needed more than h er fear.  
"...Pain is refinement," she said quietly.  
The Chihuahua blinked — the specific blink of something that had prepared a reassuring platitude and had just been rendered unnecessary by someone wh o  
understood the truth better than the comfort it had planned to offer.  
"...I was going to say 'don't worry about it,' but that works too."  
Final Realm-Wide Clarification  
The Chihuahua's voice spread again. Not forced. Not projected. Just present — existing simultaneously in every space it had visited and every space it had not,  
arriving without traveling, heard without being listened for.  
"Let me make one thing very clear," it said.  
"There is no single path in these realms."  
It blew a slow stream of bubbles from its pipe.  
At first, they were small. Drifting. Harmless. The kind of bubbles that children chased and that adults ignored.  
Then they grew.  
Not just in size — in depth.  
The bubbles expanded as they floated, their surfaces thickening like layers of glass, each layer adding complexity to what ha d begun as simplicity. Light bent  
across them unnaturally — not reflecting but refracting, splitting, recombining — and within each one, images began to form.  
Clear. Alive. Moving.  
Across realms — people looked up.  
Within one bubble — a forest of enormous glowing herbs stretched endlessly, each leaf pulsing with energy that was visible even through the bubble's curved  
surface. Creatures moved silently beneath the canopy, their forms barely visible, their attention directed downward toward ro ots that extended deeper than the  
trees were tall. The forest was not passive. It watched. It assessed. It decided, with the patience of something that measured time in seasons rather than seconds,  
whether the things that walked beneath it deserved to continue walking.  
Another — a battlefield inside a storm. Lightning crashed in patterns that were too regular to be natural and too chaotic to be control led, and cultivators raced  
across broken terrain that shifted beneath their feet with every strike. They were not fighting each other. They were chasing something — a shape that moved at  
the storm's edge, barely visible, always ahead, leading them deeper into conditions that tested not their strength but their willingness to continue when every  
instinct said stop.  
Another — a quiet forge world. A lone blacksmith stood before an anvil that glowed not with heat but with something deeper — the accumulated intent of every  
strike that had ever been delivered upon it. The smith's hammer fell slowly, deliberately, each impact sending ripples throug h the air itself. Weapons formed not  
from metal but from intention — blades that existed because someone had decided, with absolute certainty, that they should.  
Another — a shifting maze whose walls rearranged themselves with the fluid malice of something that was not merely a structure but a pa rticipant. Figures ran  
through it — some laughing, some hiding, some screaming as they realized that the maze was not testing their navigation. It was testing their reactions. And it  
was watching them back with the specific attention of something that found panic entertaining.  
Another — a vast ocean where the water rose upward instead of falling down, defying gravity with the casual arrogance of a realm that had decided physics was a  
suggestion it did not intend to follow. Creatures swam through suspended currents, their forms adapted to conditions that sho uld not have been survivable, while  
something enormous moved beneath the surface — not hunting, not resting, simply existing at a scale that made the ocean itself feel like a puddle.  
The bubbles continued to grow. Multiplying. Layering. Filling the sky of every realm they reached. Some burst — not into nothing, but into fragments of possibility  
that scattered across space before fading, each fragment carrying a glimpse of a world that could be entered, explored, survi ved, or lost.  
Silence spread across realities. Not from fear. From realization.  
"...All of that..." someone whispered, somewhere, in one of the countless spaces where the bubbles had arrived. "...Those are the realms?"  
The Chihuahua nodded, pipe still drifting bubbles lazily.  
"Oh, that's barely scratching the surface," it said casually.  
Another bubble drifted forward. Inside it — cultivators laughed while racing across shifting platforms, their competition carrying the specific energy of people who  
were having the time of their lives despite — or perhaps because of — the genuine risk of death.  
Another — a figure collapsed alone in darkness, their body broken, their cultivation shattered, their expression carrying not despair b ut the quiet recognition of  
someone who had learned something that could not be learned any other way.  
Another — a child sat peacefully beside a spirit, their conversation inaudible through the bubble's surface, the image carrying a warmth that had nothing to do  
with temperature and everything to do with the simple fact of connection in a space designed to isolate.  

"Some of you will enter and become weaker."  
"Some will lose everything and start again."  
"Some will dominate one world and fail completely in the next."  
"Some will find things you were never supposed to find."  
Another bubble formed. This one was different — distorted, incomplete, its surface rippling with instability. Something inside it was visible, but only barely. A  
shape. A structure. Something that was not a world but that existed within the collection of worlds the way a crack existed w ithin a wall — not part of the design,  
but impossible to ignore.  
"Some —"  
A pause. Longer than the others. Carrying a weight that the comedy had been keeping at bay.  
"...will realize they were never meant to survive at all."  
One bubble shattered quietly. Not burst — shattered. The difference was significant. Bursting was energetic. Shattering was structural. The bubble simply failed —  
its surface collapsing inward, its contents dissolving, the image it had held vanishing as though it had never existed.  
As if it had simply... failed.  
Silence stretched. Longer. Heavier.  
But still — no one looked away.  
Because the possibilities remained. The fear was real. The risk was real. The death was real.  
And so were the worlds.  
The Chihuahua grinned — the wide, unrestrained, completely inappropriate grin of something that had just shown the universe its own reflection and wa s deeply  
satisfied with the reaction.  
"But don't worry about all that right now."  
A clap — its tiny paws meeting with a sound that should not have carried across realms but that did anyway, because the Realm Spirit had decided it would.  
"You have six months."  
"Train. Think. Panic."  
"Organize yourselves if you can."  
"And try not to die too early."  
A beat.  
"...It makes the later parts less exciting."  
Then the presence withdrew. Not instantly. Gradually. Like something closing a door it had never fully opened — the awareness that had been everywhere  
contracting back to wherever the Realm Spirit resided when it was not treating the fundamental architecture of existence as a stage.  
A faint mutter lingered — the last trace of the broadcast, drifting through the fading connection like smoke from a pipe that had been set down but not  
extinguished:  
"...honestly, I should have charged more for this..."  
Then silence.  
Real silence.  
The kind that followed events that no one had been prepared for and that everyone would spend the next six months trying to u nderstand.  
Transition Back to Mateo  
The cave remained. Unchanged. Unaffected by the cosmic spectacle that had just played out across every realm in existence. The stone walls held their moisture.  
The ambient energy held its passive concentration. The cold air from the deeper passage continued its slow, patient drift upward.  
And yet — not untouched.  
Mateo stood there, still facing the passage. His expression had settled back into the analytical composure that was his defau lt — the amusement from the Realm  
Spirit's conversation stored but not displayed, the information from the broadcast cataloged but not yet processed.  
Most of what had been said did not matter.  
Not yet.  
Worlds. Games. Competitors. Rules. Sword tombs and forge realms and racing tracks with banana warfare and bathtub formations. Herb valleys where the plants  
resented being harvested. Mazes that watched you back. Oceans that flowed upward.  
All distant. All future problems.  
Though a faint trace of amusement lingered — the residual warmth of having witnessed something so thoroughly, comprehensively absurd that even his  
composure had been insufficient to contain the response.  
"...Still ridiculous," he murmured quietly.  
A pause.  
"...Consistently ridiculous."  
The pendant pulsed once against his chest — warmer than before, the rhythm carrying something that had not been present prior to the broadcast. Not urgency.  
Not direction. Recognition. As though the mention of all those worlds — the realms born from excess, the spaces shaped by the sealed existence of something  
that had been too vast to contain — had resonated with something the pendant carried. Something it remembered. Something it had been part of, once, before it  
became a fragment resting against the chest of a boy in a cave.  
The resonance faded.  
But the connection it had established did not.  
What mattered now was directly in front of him.  
The air below was colder. Denser. Carrying the specific quality of space that had been sealed for a very long time and that was now, incrementally, opening.  
Waiting.  
Mateo stepped forward.  
And the deeper world began.
