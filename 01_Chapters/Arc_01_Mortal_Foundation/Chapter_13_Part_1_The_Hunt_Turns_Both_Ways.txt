The alpha did not hesitate. It moved first—not with wild aggression, but with certainty. Its body lowered slightly, muscles coiling beneath metallic fur that caught fractured sunlight in muted flashes, and the space between it and Mateo closed in an instant.
Mateo stepped, not backward, but forward and to the side. Its jaws snapped through empty air where his shoulder had been, the force of the missed bite sending a gust past him strong enough to ripple the pond’s surface behind him. His right foot slid across stone, toes gripping just enough to hold friction, his ankle adjusting before imbalance could form.
Fast. Not fast enough.
A second wolf came from the flank, lower and quicker, not aiming to kill but to disrupt. Mateo dropped his center immediately, knees bending sharply as his weight shifted into the ball of his foot. His left hand moved—not striking, but redirecting—pushing the beast’s snout just off angle. Its teeth scraped across his sleeve but failed to catch flesh.
He pivoted through the motion, his right foot pressing hard as power rose cleanly through his body—calf, knee, hip, core. His strike drove into the wolf’s shoulder with precision, shifting bone and sending it stumbling into the shallows. Water erupted as the alpha closed in.
Mateo exhaled quietly. “…You don’t like warning shots, do you?”
The answer came as a claw slicing toward his ribs. He slipped beneath it, but not perfectly. The edge caught him just enough. Pain sparked along his side as the cut opened across his ribs. He didn’t retreat fully. Instead, he stepped through it, letting the force carry him forward into position rather than away from danger.
The pack had spread out now. This was no longer a single fight—it was a hunt.
Mateo moved first. He angled outward instead of holding the center, forcing the wolves to reposition and break their timing. A root shifted under his step, but his body corrected before it reached his hips. One wolf lunged—he dipped. Another attacked—he twisted. A third came from behind—
He felt it before he saw it.
For a brief instant, the world clarified. Not slower—just clearer. Movements separated into sequences. Angles revealed themselves. Choices unfolded.
He chose.
Mateo stepped into the strike instead of away from it. His shoulder rolled just enough for the claws to miss his centerline, catching cloth instead of flesh. At the same instant, his knee drove upward with grounded force, breaking the wolf’s momentum and sending it crashing hard to the ground.
The alpha reacted immediately. This time it didn’t rush. It adapted.
“That’s better,” Mateo muttered.
From that moment, the fight changed completely. The alpha stopped leading direct assaults and instead began controlling movement, guiding him into narrowing angles while the remaining wolves cut off escape paths.
Smart. Very smart.
Mateo smiled faintly. “…Now we’re getting somewhere.”
He shifted toward the trees, letting terrain reshape the fight. Roots twisted closer together. The ground rose unevenly. One wolf lunged too aggressively—he stepped aside, letting it collide with a low trunk. Another came fast—he redirected. Then the alpha struck again.
This time Mateo blocked—not fully. The impact hit his forearm at an angle, but the force was heavy, driving through muscle and bone and forcing a slight collapse in his stance before he corrected it.
Too dense.
He slid back, then stepped forward again. The exchange tightened—movement, breath, impact, reaction collapsing into a single flow.
Mateo stopped holding back completely.
His next strike carried more complete force. The chain of motion flowed cleaner, more direct. Power traveled upward without loss, releasing through his fist and driving a wolf off its feet entirely. Another came—he moved before it committed, anticipating instead of reacting.
The pressure built.
Then broke.
The alpha struck again.
This time he saw it—but not soon enough.
Claws tore into his side, deeper than before. Mateo hit the ground hard as breath left him in a sharp, uncontrolled release. Pain flared violently, heat spreading across his ribs as blood soaked into cloth.
The world narrowed—
Then steadied.
“…Good,” he said quietly.
He pushed himself up, slower now, more careful. The pack closed in.
Now the hunt shifted.

Mateo ran.
Not blindly. Deliberately.
He drove forward through the trees, forcing the pack into pursuit rather than control. Branches snapped behind him as the wolves chased, faster in straight lines but less adaptable in shifting terrain.
He used everything.
A fallen trunk became elevation—he vaulted over it, twisting mid-air to alter his landing angle. A tight cluster of trees broke the pack’s formation. Loose stone became weapons, kicked backward mid-stride.
The alpha did not slow.
It learned.
“That’s the problem with you,” Mateo muttered as he pivoted again. “You adapt too quickly.”
Then—
He misstepped.
Just barely.
His injured side lagged a fraction too long. His balance shifted imperfectly.
The alpha seized the moment.
Its jaws closed around his shoulder.
Cloth tore. Flesh followed.
Pain exploded outward as Mateo twisted violently, forcing himself free before the bite could fully lock, but not without cost. Blood spread quickly now, his movements tightening under strain.
Enough testing.
He turned sharply, facing the alpha directly.
The forest seemed to hold its breath.
Mateo’s breathing slowed—not because the pain faded, but because he forced control over it. His stance lowered, subtly different—more complete.
The alpha hesitated.
Only slightly.
Mateo smiled faintly. “…There you are.”
The next exchange sharpened. Not faster—more precise.
He stepped in.
This time, when the alpha struck, Mateo absorbed part of the motion and redirected the rest, opening a momentary weakness in its shoulder. His fist drove forward, fully connected through his body.
The strike landed deep.
The alpha staggered.
Not defeated—but no longer unchallenged.
Mateo stepped back immediately.
This was not the place to finish it.
He turned and ran again—
This time toward something worse.

Village — The First Damage
Far from the forest, the village no longer held its normal rhythm.
It had tried.
The forge still rang.
Voices still carried.
Training still continued.
But something underneath had shifted.

Near the herb shop—
the doorway had been split.
Not broken from decay.
Not worn by time.
Cut.
Clean.
One side leaned inward, barely held in place.
Inside, herbs had been disturbed—not taken, not gathered.
Displaced.
Mixed incorrectly.
Ruined.

At the edge of the training yard—
a wooden post lay on the ground.
The cut was smooth.
Too smooth.

No one spoke about it directly.
Which made it worse.

The mission board stood newly placed.
More tasks.
More urgency.
Less explanation.

Diego leaned in.
“…Did we break something?”
“…No,” Tomas said.
Pause.
“…Someone else did.”

“…We should report this,” Diego added.
“…They already know,” Tomas said quietly.

Across the yard—
two unfamiliar figures stood at the edge of the path.
Not interacting.
Not participating.
Watching.

“…This place holds longer than expected,” one said.
“…It won’t matter,” the other replied.

They left without waiting.

Adjustment
That night, Tomas slept.
Or thought he did.
His breathing slowed.
Then shifted.
A presence moved quietly near him.
Mateo stood there, silent.
“Don’t move,” Mateo murmured softly.
(His correction sequence remains intact)
“…You’ll do better like this,” Mateo said.

Tomas continued breathing—
unaware—
but changed.

Back in the Forest
Mateo didn’t know.
Not yet.

But something did.

Not the wolves.
Not the forest.

Something beyond both.

The hunt was no longer one-sided.

And it was far from over.
