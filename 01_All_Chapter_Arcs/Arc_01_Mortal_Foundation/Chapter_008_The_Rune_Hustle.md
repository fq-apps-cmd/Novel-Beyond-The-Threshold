# Chapter 8 - The Rune Hustle

Chapter 8 — The Rune Hustle  

"…Wait."  
The word came from Elder Marcos, his expression narrowing as he studied Mateo more carefully. He had been allowing the exchange to proceed, letting the other  
elders react while he measured Mateo's responses against their questions. But the boy's composure had not cracked. Not once. And that, more than anything Mateo  
had said, was what made Elder Marcos lean forward.  
"…On whose authority are you conducting this inspection?"  
Mateo paused.  
He did not turn immediately. He remained facing the door, hands still folded behind his back, holding the specific stillness of someone who had been waiting for  
exactly this question and was now deciding how much of the answer to give.  
Oh no, thought Tomas, who could see the back of Mateo's head and recognized, with the sinking certainty of experience, the particular angle of his shoulders that  
preceded something deeply inadvisable.  
They are uncertain now, Mateo thought, suppressing a grin. The question itself confirms it. If they were certain of their authority, they would not be asking about mine.  
They would be exercising theirs. They are negotiating, and they have not noticed that they started.  
When he did face them again, his expression remained calm.  
"…Authority," he said slowly, "is often misunderstood. If a door can be opened, it is not truly closed. If information can be heard, it is not truly controlled. If a presence  
goes unnoticed…"  
He allowed the sentence to trail.  
The room felt unsteady for a moment. Not physically, but in certainty. The air held the specific tension of a group of experienced people confronting the possibility that  
a child had just described their security architecture more accurately than they could.  
"…then the limitation only exists in theory."  
The elders exchanged brief glances, and the uncertainty grew in a way it had not moments before. Elder Kurosawa's fingers had steepled beneath his chin, and his gaze  
moved from Mateo to Elder Shen and back, triangulating something between the two of them that no one else in the room had noticed. Elder Doña Reyes's hands,  
which had rested on the table with their usual patient weight, had gone perfectly still.  
"…That still does not explain why you are here," Elder Park pressed. His tone was sharper than the others, the voice of someone who recognized that the conversation  
was being steered and was attempting to anchor it before it drifted further.  
Mateo considered the question, then answered plainly.  
"…Because no one stopped me."  
That answer lingered longer than it should have. It hung in the room's adjusted acoustics, which were still not functioning properly, and that gave the words slightly  
more resonance than they deserved.  
Behind him, Tomas closed his eyes briefly.  
Why did I follow him here. I could have stayed in the yard. I could have eaten breakfast. I could have done anything other than stand in the doorway of a restricted  
meeting hall while Mateo dismantles the self-confidence of nine people who could collectively flatten this village if they sneezed in formation.  
"…Wait."  
Elder Doña Reyes leaned forward. The movement was unhurried, the deliberate motion of someone who had been listening far longer than she had been speaking and  
had decided the moment required her.  
"…You are Rafael's boy."  
The room shifted. Not the conversation, the room itself. Rafael's name introduced a context several elders had not yet connected, and the connections they now made  
showed in the small adjustments of their faces. Elder Tomás Aguilar's eyebrows rose. Elder Vargas's frown deepened. Elder Hiro's jaw, already clenched for several  
minutes, tightened further, which should not have been physically possible and which his face managed anyway.  
Mateo blinked once.  
"…Correct."  
"…You are not an inspector," Elder Marcos said.  
"…Also correct."  
"…Then what are you doing here?"  
Mateo thought about it. Genuinely. Not the performative pause of someone preparing a clever answer, but the honest consideration of someone choosing between  
several true responses and selecting the most accurate one.  
"…Testing something."  
"…And the result?" Elder Park asked.  
"…It works."  
Elder Hiro, who had been growing steadily redder since Mateo first opened his mouth, finally reached his limit. His hands gripped the edge of the table hard enough  
that the wood creaked, a sound audible to everyone in the room and that the wood itself seemed to resent.  
"…GET OUT," he said through his teeth, his voice carrying the restrained energy of a man actively choosing not to vault across the table and throttle a child in front of  
his colleagues. The restraint was commendable. That it was visibly costing him years of his life was less so.  
Mateo nodded once without resistance.  
"…That is reasonable."  
He turned toward the door.  
Then stopped.  
Just barely. Just enough that the motion read as an afterthought rather than something planned, though Tomas, who had seen enough of Mateo's afterthoughts to  
know better, felt his stomach drop with the specific gravity of a man watching a catastrophe develop and being unable to prevent it.  
"…One more thing," Mateo said.  
Elder Hiro looked like he might actually combust. The vein on his forehead, which had appeared during the initial exchange and had been growing more prominent  
since, now pulsed with its own distinct rhythm, powered by what appeared to be an independent circulatory system running entirely on indignation.  
Mateo turned back to face the room, his expression shifting into something that could only be described as professionally helpful. It was warm. It was approachable. It  

was, on every conceivable level, deeply suspicious.  
"The formations in this room," he said, gesturing at the walls as though pointing out a stain on someone's shirt, "are misaligned. That is why your conversations are  
leaking. The sound suppression runes have drifted from their anchor points, and the spiritual dampening layer has developed gaps wide enough that a Qi Gathering  
trainee standing outside could pick up fragments if they knew where to listen."  
The room went very still.  
Elder Liang, who had checked the wall moments ago and confirmed the misalignment with his own hands, opened his mouth, then closed it. The internal conflict was  
visible, the part of him that wanted to challenge the claim fighting the part that had already verified it independently. He could not argue. The frustration of not being  
able to argue was written across his face in the specific handwriting of a man whose own evidence had betrayed him.  
Elder Doña Reyes's eyes had sharpened. She was studying Mateo with the attention of someone who had just heard a child describe formation architecture using  
terminology most cultivators twice his age did not possess, and who was now running through the possible explanations for that and finding none of them  
comfortable.  
"…How do you know about formations?" Elder Vargas asked carefully, his tone carrying the measured caution of someone defusing something that might be explosive.  
Mateo shrugged with the casual ease of a person discussing weather.  
"…I dabble."  
"…You dabble," Elder Marcos repeated. The two words contained an entire essay on the inadequacy of that explanation.  
"In runes and talismans. A bit of formation work. Nothing extensive." He paused, then added with the specific modesty of someone being neither modest nor specific,  
"Mostly self-taught. Some reading. Some observation."  
The elders stared at him. Nine faces, nine expressions, nine variations on the same fundamental reaction: the deeply uncomfortable realization that the situation had  
layers no one had anticipated.  
Mateo continued, his tone shifting into something almost businesslike, the register of a vendor who had identified a customer's need and was now positioning the  
solution.  
"I could fix it."  
Silence.  
"…Fix it," Elder Marcos repeated flatly.  
"Realign the anchor points. Patch the dampening gaps. Reinforce the sound suppression lattice. I could also add a secondary layer of my own design, something that  
would not only seal conversations within the room but create a subtle feedback distortion for anyone attempting to listen from outside. They would hear something. It  
would be nonsense. Fragments of old conversations. Ambient noise. Perhaps someone ordering lunch."  
A brief, bewildered pause.  
"…Someone ordering lunch?" Elder Park echoed, his expression caught between incredulity and the reluctant recognition that he was genuinely curious about the  
technical implementation.  
"It is a design choice," Mateo said. "I find misdirection more effective than silence. Silence tells an eavesdropper something is being hidden. Nonsense tells them there  
is nothing worth hearing."  
The logic was, infuriatingly, sound.  
Elder Hiro, who had been ready to throw Mateo through the wall thirty seconds earlier, now sat with his mouth slightly open, caught between rage and the deeply  
uncomfortable recognition that the child who had violated every protocol in the building was also offering the most practical security improvement anyone had  
proposed in years. His expression cycled through fury, confusion, reluctant interest, and renewed fury at the reluctant interest, before settling into a grimace that  
communicated all four simultaneously.  
"…And what would this cost?" Elder Marcos asked, his tone careful in a way that suggested he already regretted asking and was asking anyway, because not knowing  
was worse.  
Mateo tilted his head, as if the question surprised him. As if the notion that a service might have a cost had only just occurred to him, rather than being the entire point  
of the exercise.  
"…A small fee. Materials, mostly. A few spiritual inks. Some etching tools. Access to the formation base layer so I can integrate rather than override."  
He paused.  
"…And perhaps," he added, "a formal acknowledgment that my inspection was, in fact, useful."  
Elder Hiro made a sound somewhere between a cough and a growl, the vocal equivalent of a man's dignity attempting to escape through his throat.  
At the far end of the table, Elder Kurosawa had turned his face away from the group entirely and appeared to be studying the grain of the wooden wall with  
unprecedented academic interest. His shoulders were suspiciously still. Beside him, Elder Tomás Aguilar had raised a hand to his mouth and was coughing with a  
regularity that had no medical basis whatsoever. Above the hand, his eyes were bright with the strain of someone fighting a losing battle against laughter, who had  
accepted the loss several seconds ago and was maintaining the performance out of professional obligation.  
Elder Doña Reyes sat perfectly still, but the faintest crease had appeared at the corner of her mouth. She had seen many things in her decades on the council. She had  
not, until today, watched someone attempt to sell the elders a security upgrade for a vulnerability the seller had created. It was, she had to admit, elegant.  
Elder Shen, still by the window, had not moved. But his gaze had shifted from the sky to Mateo and stayed there, with the focused stillness of someone who had  
stopped observing casually and begun observing with intent. Whatever conclusion he was forming was not yet complete. The foundation of it was being laid.  
"…We will discuss this," Elder Marcos said finally. The words carried the weight of a man ending a conversation not because it had been resolved but because  
continuing it would cause structural damage to the council's composure.  
Mateo nodded.  
"…Take your time. The formations will continue to degrade in the interim, but that is, of course, your decision."  
Elder Hiro stood up.  
Tomas grabbed Mateo by the shoulder and steered him toward the door with the urgent efficiency of someone evacuating a building that was about to collapse,  
which, in terms of Elder Hiro's restraint, was not entirely inaccurate.  
"We are leaving," Tomas said.  
"…I was already leaving."  
"You were about to get us killed."  
"…That seems like an overreaction."  
The door closed behind them.  
Inside, the elders sat in silence for a long moment. The kind that follows an event everyone has witnessed and no one is prepared to be the first to characterize.  
Then Elder Marcos spoke.  
"…Check every formation in this building. Now."  
Chairs scraped against stone as the room erupted into motion. Elder Liang was already at the far wall, palms flat against the surface, extending his awareness into the  
stone with the urgency of a man who had just been told the foundation might be compromised. Elder Vargas moved toward the foundation stones near the entrance,  
crouching to examine the inscriptions with a brush he produced from his sleeve. Elder Doña Reyes remained seated but closed her eyes, extending her awareness  

down through the floor into the deeper formation layers most of the council did not know how to reach, the layers she had helped restore forty years ago and now  
examined with the careful attention of someone checking whether her own work had been undermined.  
Elder Kurosawa, who had recovered his composure, mostly, caught Elder Tomás Aguilar's eye across the table. Neither spoke. But the look they exchanged  
communicated a complete thought: that child is going to be a problem of historic proportions, and I am not entirely opposed to watching it happen.  

Mateo walked calmly down the corridor with Tomas still gripping his shoulder, though the grip had loosened from urgent to resigned, the hold of someone who knew  
that physical restraint was not actually preventing anything but who maintained it on principle.  
The corridor was dim, lit by narrow windows set high in the walls to allow light without sightlines. The stone floor carried their footsteps in flat, contained echoes that  
bounced between the walls and faded quickly, or would have, if the formations had been working. Instead the echoes carried slightly further than they should have,  
blending with the muffled sounds of the elder discussion now audible through the walls.  
Behind them, faintly, the elders were arguing. Mateo could hear them. Fragments of sentences, accusations, questions no one could answer. Elder Hiro's voice was the  
loudest, which was unsurprising. Elder Liang's was the most technical, which was also unsurprising. Elder Park was asking questions no one wanted to address. Elder  
Marcos was attempting to impose order on a situation that had moved past orderly.  
Obviously he could hear them.  
The formation was not working, after all.  
Tomas, who had been watching Mateo's face with growing suspicion throughout the walk, finally spoke.  
"…You did something."  
"…I do many things."  
"To the formations."  
"…That is a serious accusation."  
"Your face is doing the thing."  
"…I do not have a thing."  
"You absolutely have a thing. You are doing it right now."  
Mateo said nothing.  
Which was, of course, the thing.  

Outside, the air felt lighter.  
Not calmer, only less constrained. Sound moved freely again, and the subtle structure of the meeting space no longer held around them. The village resumed its  
presence: the distant clang of the blacksmith's hammer, a child shouting something about a lost sandal, and, from the market path, a familiar voice explaining to a  
customer why a particular root was worth exactly three times what the customer believed.  
Mateo knew the voice before he saw the stall. He had been buying from that table since he was old enough to reach the top of it.  
"…It's not about the root itself," the herb seller was saying, holding it up without any apparent concern for whether the customer stayed. "It's about the soil it grew in.  
Location determines potency. You'll never get this quality out of the eastern plots."  
She is not wrong.  
His step did not slow, but his attention sharpened briefly. She did not know how right she was, or why, but the principle held at every scale he had ever tested it.  
Where a thing grew mattered as much as what it was. It was true of roots. It was true of cultivators. It had been true of entire realms.  
The herb seller understood through forty years of handling plants what most people never worked out at all. She also, unfortunately, did not stock spiritual inks, or  
etching tools, or anything else that would let a person work on formations, because nobody with sense stocked materials the village had no demand for.  
Old man Chen did. Which was inconvenient, given how their last conversation had ended.  
Mateo filed that alongside everything else and kept walking.  
Tomas stood still for a moment on the path, then followed.  
"…Explain."  
Mateo continued walking as though nothing unusual had occurred.  
"…They were already uncertain."  
"That is not an explanation."  
Mateo glanced at him.  
"…It is."  
Tomas stopped walking altogether.  
"…You walked into a restricted meeting, convinced them you had authority, disrupted their discussion, exposed a security flaw you probably caused, offered to fix it for  
a fee, and left."  
"…Yes."  
"…Why?"  
Mateo paused.  
For once, he did not answer immediately. The silence was genuine, not the calculated pause he used during performances, but the brief stillness of someone choosing  
between the answer that was clever and the answer that was true.  
Then he turned, and his posture shifted again, subtly but unmistakably. The casual looseness that defined his public manner tightened into something more aligned,  
more present, more honest.  
"…Understanding a system requires testing its boundaries," he said, his voice steady. "If a rule cannot withstand pressure, it was never solid to begin with. Questioning  
is not the issue. Ignorance is."  
Tomas did not interrupt. He simply stared.  
"…If no one challenges unnecessary limitations, then those limitations persist without reason."  
For a moment, it made sense.  
Then Mateo relaxed, and the tension in his posture dissolved as quickly as it had appeared.  
"…Also," he added casually, "it was funny."  
Tomas blinked.  
"…What?"  
Mateo turned and resumed walking.  
"…Watching them question everything was entertaining."  
Diego, who had somehow been present the entire time without being noticed, a feat of stealth that was either impressive or concerning depending on perspective,  
rushed up beside them with the energy of someone who had been containing an observation for several minutes and had reached the structural limit of his patience.  
"…You walked into an elder meeting, broke their security, made them doubt themselves, and then offered to sell them the fix?"  

"…Briefly."  
"That is the most incredible thing I have ever witnessed."  
"…It was a productive morning."  
Tomas remained where he stood a moment longer before finally moving again.  
I do not understand him. He builds arguments with the precision of someone who has been thinking about systems for longer than he has been alive. He reads rooms the  
way other people read text, instantly and completely, with an awareness of subtext that should not exist in someone his age. He speaks like someone far older.  
Then ruins it immediately by saying things like that.  
"…You are a problem," Tomas said.  
Mateo did not look back.  
"…That has already been established."  

That night, Mateo sat alone beneath the open sky as the quiet of the village settled around him. The stillness was not empty. It held small inconsistencies, faint sounds  
that carried too far or blended into the background, the ordinary evidence that quiet does not mean absence. A dog barked once somewhere to the south and the  
sound traveled further than it should have before the canopy absorbed it. Cooking smoke drifted from the homes nearest the market path, carrying ginger and  
something sweeter he could not identify.  
The pendant rested in his palm, its fractured star catching the dim light above.  
This time, it pulsed.  
Not strongly. But distinctly.  
More consistently now. The intervals between pulses had shortened since the previous evening, not dramatically, but enough that the change was measurable. The  
rhythm was settling into something steadier and more deliberate, as though the pendant were learning to sustain its own activity rather than producing it in bursts.  
Mateo watched it closely.  
The fragments inside the fractured star appeared to be moving. Not visibly, and not in any way another person would detect, but through the subtle shift in how the  
light caught their edges as he turned it. Each rotation produced a slightly different reflection, as though the internal arrangement were not fixed but fluid, the pieces  
drifting in slow deliberate patterns that suggested convergence rather than random motion.  
They are moving inward. Not randomly. Toward something. As though they remember a configuration they once held and are finding their way back to it.  
The thought came more clearly than before.  
Not broken.  
A pause.  
Incomplete. Not damaged, unfinished. As though whatever created this was interrupted before completion, and the pieces that remain are continuing the work on their  
own, without instruction, following a design that exists in their structure rather than anywhere outside it.  
He closed his hand around it.  
And for just a moment, an image surfaced.  
A forge.  
Heat.  
Metal shaping under steady hands. Large hands, calloused, unhurried, belonging to someone who understood that shaping required patience and that patience  
required trust in the process. The metal glowed beneath the hammer's rhythm, its color shifting from dark orange to bright amber as the temperature rose, the surface  
becoming fluid enough to accept correction without losing its form.  
A large figure stood behind the hands. Unmoving. Unwavering. Broad shoulders, and a presence that filled the space the way the forge's heat filled it, not through  
force but through the simple fact of existing somewhere too small to contain it comfortably.  
The edges were sharper than memory allowed.  
The glow of the metal held longer than it should have, and the details were more defined than in any previous instance: the texture of the anvil's surface, the pattern of  
the sparks, the way the forge light painted the surrounding darkness in shades of orange and amber that shifted with every strike.  
Then it was gone.  
Mateo exhaled slowly.  
"…Gramps."  
The word came without certainty, but it did not feel wrong. It carried the weight of a connection existing below conscious knowledge, a thread linking the image to  
something in his history that he could feel and could not yet see. The figure at the forge was not a stranger. The hands were not unfamiliar. The rhythm of the hammer  
was not new.  
It was remembered.  
From somewhere memory alone could not explain.  
He leaned back slightly and looked up. The stars were visible through the gaps in the canopy, scattered and fragmented by leaves, their light arriving from distances so  
vast that the journey mattered more than the arrival. Some of them were already dead. The light continued anyway.  
Something was left behind.  
Something is still forming.  
A faint smile touched his expression.  
"…Good."  
For once, the unknown was not inconvenient.  
It was interesting.  
And Mateo had always preferred things that were worth understanding.
